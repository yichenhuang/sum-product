Assistant

Below is the proof route I would pursue first. It is not the classical smooth-number route; that route naturally gives only \( |A|^{2-o(1)} \)-type upper bounds. The more promising route is to use high-degree totally real number fields, view their rings of integers in their Minkowski embeddings, and choose algebraic integers lying in a carefully chosen high-dimensional body. The key geometric point is that certain non-convex \(L^p\)-balls, \(0<p<1\), are simultaneously stable under addition and have strong volume contraction under coordinatewise multiplication.

I will develop this approach in detail and isolate the main remaining gap precisely.

1. Guiding idea

Let \(K/\mathbb Q\) be totally real of degree \(d\), with real embeddings

\[ \sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_d:K\hookrightarrow \mathbb R. \]

The Minkowski embedding

\[ \sigma:K\to \mathbb R^d,\qquad \alpha\mapsto (\sigma_1(\alpha),\dots,\sigma_d(\alpha)) \]

turns addition and multiplication in \(K\) into coordinatewise addition and coordinatewise multiplication in \(\mathbb R^d\):

\[ \sigma(\alpha+\beta)=\sigma(\alpha)+\sigma(\beta), \]
\[ \sigma(\alpha\beta)=\sigma(\alpha)\cdot \sigma(\beta) \]

where the product on the right is coordinatewise.

Thus, if we choose a finite set of algebraic integers

\[ \mathcal A\subset \mathcal O_K \]

whose Minkowski vectors lie in a body \(B\subset \mathbb R^d\), then

\[ \mathcal A+\mathcal A \]

is controlled by \(B+B\), and

\[ \mathcal A\mathcal A \]

is controlled by the coordinatewise product body \(B\cdot B\).

Finally, choosing one real embedding \(\iota=\sigma_1\), the set

\[ A=\iota(\mathcal A)\subset \mathbb R \]

has exactly the same sumset/product-set cardinalities as \(\mathcal A\), because \(\iota\) is injective.

So the construction reduces to the following high-dimensional geometric/lattice problem:

Find many lattice points in a body \(B\subset \mathbb R^d\) such that both \(B+B\) and \(B\cdot B\) have substantially smaller volume than \(\operatorname{vol}(B)^2\).

The body that appears to be best suited is an \(L^p\)-ball with \(0<p<1\).

2. The \(L^p\)-bodies and their basic geometry

For \(0<q\le 1\), \(d\ge 1\), and \(Y>0\), define

\[ B_{q,d}(Y) = \left\{ x=(x_1,\dots,x_d)\in \mathbb R^d: \sum_{j=1}^d |x_j|^q\le Y^q \right\}. \]

This is not convex when \(q<1\), but it has two crucial properties:

  1. It is stable under addition up to a controlled dilation.
  2. Coordinatewise multiplication of two \(B_{p,d}(H)\)-vectors lands inside a much smaller \(B_{p/2,d}(H^2)\)-body.

We record these facts carefully.

Lemma 2.1: Volume formula

For every \(q>0\),

\[ \operatorname{vol} B_{q,d}(Y) = Y^d\cdot \frac{2^d \Gamma(1+1/q)^d}{\Gamma(1+d/q)}. \]

Proof

By scaling, it is enough to take \(Y=1\). On the positive orthant, set

\[ u_j=x_j^q. \]

Then

\[ dx_j = \frac1q u_j^{1/q-1}\,du_j. \]

Therefore the positive-orthant volume is

\[ \int_{\substack{u_j\ge 0\\ u_1+\cdots+u_d\le 1}} \prod_{j=1}^d \frac1q u_j^{1/q-1}\,du_1\cdots du_d. \]

By the Dirichlet integral, this equals

\[ \frac{\Gamma(1+1/q)^d}{\Gamma(1+d/q)}. \]

Multiplying by \(2^d\) for the \(2^d\) orthants gives

\[ \operatorname{vol} B_{q,d}(1) = \frac{2^d \Gamma(1+1/q)^d}{\Gamma(1+d/q)}. \]

Scaling by \(Y\) gives the claimed formula. \(\square\)

Lemma 2.2: Additive stability

If \(0<q\le 1\), then

\[ B_{q,d}(Y)+B_{q,d}(Y) \subseteq B_{q,d}(2^{1/q}Y). \]

Proof

For \(0<q\le 1\), the function \(t\mapsto t^q\) is subadditive on \(\mathbb R_{\ge 0}\):

\[ |a+b|^q\le |a|^q+|b|^q. \]

Thus, if \(x,y\in B_{q,d}(Y)\), then

\[ \sum_{j=1}^d |x_j+y_j|^q \le \sum_{j=1}^d |x_j|^q+\sum_{j=1}^d |y_j|^q \le 2Y^q. \]

Equivalently,

\[ x+y\in B_{q,d}(2^{1/q}Y). \]

\(\square\)

Lemma 2.3: Multiplicative stability

Let \(0<p\le 1\). If \(x,y\in B_{p,d}(H)\), then their coordinatewise product

\[ x\cdot y=(x_1y_1,\dots,x_dy_d) \]

belongs to

\[ B_{p/2,d}(H^2). \]

Proof

We need to show

\[ \sum_{j=1}^d |x_jy_j|^{p/2}\le (H^2)^{p/2}=H^p. \]

By Cauchy–Schwarz,

\[ \sum_{j=1}^d |x_jy_j|^{p/2} = \sum_{j=1}^d |x_j|^{p/2}|y_j|^{p/2} \le \left(\sum_{j=1}^d |x_j|^p\right)^{1/2} \left(\sum_{j=1}^d |y_j|^p\right)^{1/2}. \]

Since \(x,y\in B_{p,d}(H)\), this is at most

\[ (H^p)^{1/2}(H^p)^{1/2}=H^p. \]

Therefore \(x\cdot y\in B_{p/2,d}(H^2)\). \(\square\)

3. The key volume saving

The central geometric advantage is that

\[ \operatorname{vol} B_{p/2,d}(H^2) \]

is exponentially smaller than

\[ \operatorname{vol} B_{p,d}(H)^2. \]

This is the source of the eventual power saving.

Lemma 3.1: Volume-ratio asymptotic

Fix \(0<p\le 1\). For every \(H>0\),

\[ \frac{\operatorname{vol} B_{p,d}(H)^2} {\operatorname{vol} B_{p/2,d}(H^2)} = \exp\left( S(p)d+o(d)\right), \]

where

\[ S(p) = \log\left( 2^{1+2/p} \frac{\Gamma(1+1/p)^2}{\Gamma(1+2/p)} \right). \]

Moreover,

\[ S(p)\to +\infty \qquad\text{as }p\to 0^+. \]

More precisely,

\[ S(p)=\log 2+\frac12\log\frac{\pi}{p}+o(1) \qquad (p\to 0^+). \]

Proof

By Lemma 2.1,

\[ \operatorname{vol} B_{p,d}(H) = H^d\frac{2^d\Gamma(1+1/p)^d}{\Gamma(1+d/p)}, \]

and

\[ \operatorname{vol} B_{p/2,d}(H^2) = H^{2d} \frac{2^d\Gamma(1+2/p)^d}{\Gamma(1+2d/p)}. \]

Therefore

\[ \frac{\operatorname{vol} B_{p,d}(H)^2} {\operatorname{vol} B_{p/2,d}(H^2)} = 2^d \left( \frac{\Gamma(1+1/p)^2}{\Gamma(1+2/p)} \right)^d \cdot \frac{\Gamma(1+2d/p)}{\Gamma(1+d/p)^2}. \]

By Stirling’s formula,

\[ \frac{\Gamma(1+2d/p)}{\Gamma(1+d/p)^2} = \exp\left( \frac{2d}{p}\log 2+o(d) \right). \]

Thus

\[ \frac1d \log \frac{\operatorname{vol} B_{p,d}(H)^2} {\operatorname{vol} B_{p/2,d}(H^2)} = \log 2 + \frac{2}{p}\log 2 + 2\log \Gamma(1+1/p) - \log \Gamma(1+2/p) + o(1). \]

This gives the claimed expression for \(S(p)\).

For \(p\to 0^+\), put \(z=1/p\). Then by Stirling,

\[ \frac{\Gamma(1+2z)}{\Gamma(1+z)^2} \sim \frac{4^z}{\sqrt{\pi z}}. \]

So

\[ 2^{1+2/p} \frac{\Gamma(1+1/p)^2}{\Gamma(1+2/p)} \sim 2\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{p}}, \]

and hence

\[ S(p)=\log 2+\frac12\log\frac{\pi}{p}+o(1). \]

Thus \(S(p)\to\infty\) as \(p\to0^+\). \(\square\)

4. How the number-field construction would work

We now explain the construction conditionally on a lattice-counting statement. The counting statement is the main remaining gap.

4.1. Number-field input

We need an infinite sequence of totally real number fields \(K_i\) with degrees

\[ d_i=[K_i:\mathbb Q]\to\infty \]

and uniformly bounded root discriminant:

\[ \operatorname{rd}(K_i) = D_{K_i}^{1/d_i} \le R_0 \]

for some absolute constant \(R_0\).

This is a known type of input: such sequences follow from Martinet’s construction of totally real class-field towers using Golod–Shafarevich. For the purposes of this proof plan, I will treat the following as available:

Bounded-root-discriminant tower input. There exists an absolute \(R_0<\infty\) and an infinite sequence of totally real fields \(K_i\) with \(d_i\to\infty\) and \(\operatorname{rd}(K_i)\le R_0\).

This is not the main obstruction; it is a standard algebraic-number-theoretic input.

4.2. Weighted Minkowski embeddings

For technical flexibility, it is useful to allow degree-zero archimedean weights.

For a totally real field \(K\) of degree \(d\), let

\[ w=(w_1,\dots,w_d)\in \mathbb R_{>0}^d \]

with

\[ \prod_{j=1}^d w_j=1. \]

Define the weighted Minkowski embedding

\[ \sigma_w(\alpha) = (w_1\sigma_1(\alpha),\dots,w_d\sigma_d(\alpha)). \]

Because \(\prod_j w_j=1\), the weighted lattice \(\sigma_w(\mathcal O_K)\) has the same covolume as the unweighted Minkowski lattice:

\[ \operatorname{covol}(\sigma_w(\mathcal O_K)) = \sqrt{D_K}. \]

For \(q>0\), define

\[ N_{K,w,q}(Y) = \left| \left\{ \alpha\in \mathcal O_K: \sigma_w(\alpha)\in B_{q,d}(Y) \right\} \right|. \]

The intended set is

\[ \mathcal A_K = \left\{ \alpha\in \mathcal O_K: \sigma_w(\alpha)\in B_{p,d}(H) \right\}. \]

Then, using Lemmas 2.2 and 2.3:

\[ \mathcal A_K+\mathcal A_K \subseteq \left\{ \gamma\in \mathcal O_K: \sigma_w(\gamma)\in B_{p,d}(2^{1/p}H) \right\}, \]

and

\[ \mathcal A_K\mathcal A_K \subseteq \left\{ \gamma\in \mathcal O_K: \sigma_{w^2}(\gamma)\in B_{p/2,d}(H^2) \right\}, \]

where

\[ w^2=(w_1^2,\dots,w_d^2). \]

Indeed, if \(\alpha,\beta\in \mathcal A_K\), then

\[ \sigma_w(\alpha+\beta) = \sigma_w(\alpha)+\sigma_w(\beta), \]

and

\[ \sigma_{w^2}(\alpha\beta) = \sigma_w(\alpha)\cdot \sigma_w(\beta) \]

coordinatewise.

Thus

\[ |\mathcal A_K+\mathcal A_K| \le N_{K,w,p}(2^{1/p}H), \]

and

\[ |\mathcal A_K\mathcal A_K| \le N_{K,w^2,p/2}(H^2). \]

5. The main lattice-counting proposition needed

The proof would be complete if we can prove the following.

Proposition G: Balanced \(p\)-ball counting in bounded-root-discriminant towers

Fix \(0<p\le 1\), \(T>0\), and \(\varepsilon>0\). Let \(K_i\) be a totally real bounded-root-discriminant tower as above, with \(d_i=[K_i:\mathbb Q]\to\infty\).

After passing to an infinite subsequence, there exist degree-zero weights

\[ w^{(i)}=(w^{(i)}_1,\dots,w^{(i)}_{d_i}) \]

such that, with

\[ H_i=T d_i^{1/p}, \]

one has

\[ N_{K_i,w^{(i)},p}(H_i) = \exp(o(d_i)) \frac{\operatorname{vol} B_{p,d_i}(H_i)} {\sqrt{D_{K_i}}}, \]

and the corresponding upper bounds

\[ N_{K_i,w^{(i)},p}(2^{1/p}H_i) \le \exp(\varepsilon d_i) \frac{\operatorname{vol} B_{p,d_i}(2^{1/p}H_i)} {\sqrt{D_{K_i}}}, \]
\[ N_{K_i,(w^{(i)})^2,p/2}(H_i^2) \le \exp(\varepsilon d_i) \frac{\operatorname{vol} B_{p/2,d_i}(H_i^2)} {\sqrt{D_{K_i}}}. \]

An \(e^{o(d_i)}\)-quality estimate would be ideal. For the construction, an \(e^{C_p d_i}\)-quality estimate would also suffice if the constant \(C_p\) is controlled well enough relative to \(S(p)\).

This proposition is the major gap.

6. Conditional proof of the theorem assuming Proposition G

Assume Proposition G.

Fix a root-discriminant bound \(R_0\) for the tower.

We first choose \(p>0\) sufficiently small. By Lemma 3.1,

\[ S(p)\to\infty \qquad (p\to0^+). \]

So choose \(p\in(0,1]\) and then \(\varepsilon>0\) so small that

\[ s := S(p)-\frac12\log R_0-4\varepsilon > 0. \]

Next choose \(T>0\) large enough so that the eventual additive exponent is \(<2\). To make this explicit, define

\[ U(p) = \log\bigl(2\Gamma(1+1/p)\bigr) + \frac{1+\log p}{p}. \]

By Lemma 2.1 and Stirling’s formula,

\[ \frac1d\log \operatorname{vol}B_{p,d}(T d^{1/p}) = \log T+U(p)+o(1). \]

Since \(D_K^{1/d}\le R_0\),

\[ \frac1d \log \frac{\operatorname{vol}B_{p,d}(T d^{1/p})}{\sqrt{D_K}} \ge \log T+U(p)-\frac12\log R_0+o(1). \]

Choose \(T\) so large that

\[ a := \log T+U(p)-\frac12\log R_0-2\varepsilon > \frac{\log 2}{p}+2\varepsilon. \]

Also set

\[ A := \log T+U(p)+2\varepsilon. \]

For all sufficiently large \(i\), Proposition G gives

\[ |\mathcal A_i| \ge \exp(a d_i), \]

and also

\[ |\mathcal A_i| \le \exp(A d_i), \]

where

\[ \mathcal A_i = \left\{ \alpha\in \mathcal O_{K_i}: \sigma_{w^{(i)}}(\alpha)\in B_{p,d_i}(H_i) \right\}. \]

Let

\[ n_i=|\mathcal A_i|. \]

Then \(n_i\to\infty\), since \(a>0\).

Now choose a real embedding \(\iota_i:K_i\hookrightarrow \mathbb R\) and set

\[ A_i=\iota_i(\mathcal A_i)\subset \mathbb R. \]

Because \(\iota_i\) is injective,

\[ |A_i|=|\mathcal A_i|=n_i, \]

and

\[ |A_i+A_i|=|\mathcal A_i+\mathcal A_i|, \]
\[ |A_iA_i|=|\mathcal A_i\mathcal A_i|. \]

We now bound the sumset and product set.

6.1. Sumset bound

By Lemma 2.2 and Proposition G,

\[ |\mathcal A_i+\mathcal A_i| \le N_{K_i,w^{(i)},p}(2^{1/p}H_i) \]
\[ \le \exp(\varepsilon d_i) \frac{\operatorname{vol}B_{p,d_i}(2^{1/p}H_i)} {\sqrt{D_{K_i}}}. \]

But

\[ \operatorname{vol}B_{p,d_i}(2^{1/p}H_i) = 2^{d_i/p} \operatorname{vol}B_{p,d_i}(H_i). \]

Using the lower bound for \(n_i\),

\[ n_i \ge \exp(-\varepsilon d_i) \frac{\operatorname{vol}B_{p,d_i}(H_i)} {\sqrt{D_{K_i}}}, \]

we get

\[ |\mathcal A_i+\mathcal A_i| \le \exp(2\varepsilon d_i)2^{d_i/p} n_i. \]

Thus

\[ \log |\mathcal A_i+\mathcal A_i| \le \log n_i + \left(\frac{\log2}{p}+2\varepsilon\right)d_i. \]

Since \(\log n_i\ge a d_i\),

\[ d_i\le \frac{\log n_i}{a}. \]

Therefore

\[ \log |\mathcal A_i+\mathcal A_i| \le \left( 1+ \frac{(\log2)/p+2\varepsilon}{a} \right) \log n_i. \]

By our choice of \(T\),

\[ \frac{(\log2)/p+2\varepsilon}{a}<1. \]

Hence there is a positive number

\[ \delta_+ = 1- \frac{(\log2)/p+2\varepsilon}{a} >0 \]

such that

\[ |\mathcal A_i+\mathcal A_i| \le n_i^{2-\delta_+}. \]

Equivalently,

\[ |A_i+A_i|\le |A_i|^{2-\delta_+}. \]

6.2. Product-set bound

By Lemma 2.3 and Proposition G,

\[ |\mathcal A_i\mathcal A_i| \le N_{K_i,(w^{(i)})^2,p/2}(H_i^2) \]
\[ \le \exp(\varepsilon d_i) \frac{\operatorname{vol}B_{p/2,d_i}(H_i^2)} {\sqrt{D_{K_i}}}. \]

Meanwhile,

\[ n_i \ge \exp(-\varepsilon d_i) \frac{\operatorname{vol}B_{p,d_i}(H_i)} {\sqrt{D_{K_i}}}. \]

Therefore

\[ \frac{\operatorname{vol}B_{p,d_i}(H_i)} {\sqrt{D_{K_i}}} \le \exp(\varepsilon d_i)n_i. \]

So

\[ |\mathcal A_i\mathcal A_i| \le \exp(3\varepsilon d_i)n_i^2 \cdot \sqrt{D_{K_i}} \frac{ \operatorname{vol}B_{p/2,d_i}(H_i^2) }{ \operatorname{vol}B_{p,d_i}(H_i)^2 }. \]

Taking logs and using Lemma 3.1,

\[ \log \frac{ \operatorname{vol}B_{p,d_i}(H_i)^2 }{ \operatorname{vol}B_{p/2,d_i}(H_i^2) } = S(p)d_i+o(d_i). \]

Also,

\[ \sqrt{D_{K_i}} \le R_0^{d_i/2}. \]

Thus, for all sufficiently large \(i\),

\[ |\mathcal A_i\mathcal A_i| \le n_i^2 \exp\left( -\left(S(p)-\frac12\log R_0-4\varepsilon\right)d_i \right). \]

By definition of \(s\),

\[ |\mathcal A_i\mathcal A_i| \le n_i^2 e^{-s d_i}. \]

Using the upper bound \(\log n_i\le A d_i\), we have

\[ d_i\ge \frac{\log n_i}{A}. \]

Therefore

\[ |\mathcal A_i\mathcal A_i| \le n_i^2 \exp\left( -\frac{s}{A}\log n_i \right) = n_i^{2-s/A}. \]

So with

\[ \delta_\times=\frac{s}{A}>0, \]

we get

\[ |\mathcal A_i\mathcal A_i| \le n_i^{2-\delta_\times}. \]

Equivalently,

\[ |A_iA_i|\le |A_i|^{2-\delta_\times}. \]

6.3. Conclusion of the conditional proof

Let

\[ \delta = \min(\delta_+,\delta_\times)>0. \]

Then for all sufficiently large \(i\),

\[ \max(|A_i+A_i|,|A_iA_i|) \le |A_i|^{2-\delta}. \]

Since \(n_i=|A_i|\to\infty\), this gives the desired infinite sequence.

Thus, modulo Proposition G, the theorem follows.

7. What has been proved unconditionally here

The following pieces are fully established above:

  1. The correct high-dimensional bodies \(B_{p,d}(H)\) have the required algebraic closure properties:

    \[ B_{p,d}(H)+B_{p,d}(H)\subseteq B_{p,d}(2^{1/p}H), \]
    \[ B_{p,d}(H)\cdot B_{p,d}(H)\subseteq B_{p/2,d}(H^2). \]

  2. Their volumes are explicit:

    \[ \operatorname{vol}B_{q,d}(Y) = Y^d \frac{2^d\Gamma(1+1/q)^d}{\Gamma(1+d/q)}. \]

  3. The multiplicative product body is exponentially smaller than the square of the original body:

    \[ \frac{\operatorname{vol}B_{p,d}(H)^2} {\operatorname{vol}B_{p/2,d}(H^2)} = \exp(S(p)d+o(d)), \]

    with

    \[ S(p)\to\infty \qquad (p\to0^+). \]

  4. If the relevant lattice-point counts match volumes up to subexponential error in a bounded-root-discriminant tower, the theorem follows with an explicit positive exponent

    \[ \delta = \min\left( 1-\frac{(\log2)/p+2\varepsilon}{a}, \frac{S(p)-\frac12\log R_0-4\varepsilon}{A} \right)>0. \]

So the main proof architecture is complete.

8. Remaining gaps and assessment

The remaining work is concentrated in one serious but plausible lattice-counting problem.

Gap 1: Balanced \(p\)-ball lattice counting

Precise statement needed

For an infinite totally real bounded-root-discriminant tower \(K_i\), with \(d_i\to\infty\), we need degree-zero weights \(w^{(i)}\) such that, for fixed \(p,T\),

\[ N_{K_i,w^{(i)},p}(T d_i^{1/p}) \approx \frac{\operatorname{vol}B_{p,d_i}(T d_i^{1/p})}{\sqrt{D_{K_i}}}, \]

and also the corresponding upper estimates for

\[ B_{p,d_i}(2^{1/p}T d_i^{1/p}) \]

and

\[ B_{p/2,d_i}(T^2 d_i^{2/p}) \]

with weights \((w^{(i)})^2\).

An \(e^{o(d_i)}\) error is more than enough. A weaker \(e^{C d_i}\) error is also usable if \(C\) is controlled well enough relative to \(S(p)\).

Why this is plausible

Several features point in the right direction.

  1. Bounded root discriminant means the covolume is only exponential in \(d\):

    \[ \sqrt{D_K}\le R_0^{d/2}. \]

  2. The \(L^p\)-body has exponential volume at the correct scale \(H=Td^{1/p}\):

    \[ \operatorname{vol}B_{p,d}(Td^{1/p}) = \exp((\log T+U(p)+o(1))d). \]

  3. Degree-zero weights allow Arakelov balancing. In Arakelov language, we can multiply the embeddings by a degree-zero divisor without changing the covolume. The hope is to choose such weights so that the lattice is sufficiently well-rounded for the relevant bodies.

  4. The weighted product set naturally uses \(w^2\). This is compatible with multiplication:

    \[ \sigma_{w^2}(\alpha\beta) = \sigma_w(\alpha)\cdot \sigma_w(\beta). \]

  5. Reduction theory may provide the required weights. A plausible route is to choose \(w^{(i)}\) so that \(\sigma_{w^{(i)}}(\mathcal O_{K_i})\) is semistable or lies in a compact part of the Arakelov moduli space modulo units. Then one needs to ensure that \(\sigma_{(w^{(i)})^2}(\mathcal O_{K_i})\) remains sufficiently controlled.

Why this is a serious obstruction

This is not a routine geometry-of-numbers estimate. The bodies \(B_{p,d}\) are non-convex when \(p<1\), and arbitrary number-field lattices may be skewed. Volume alone does not guarantee the correct lattice-point count for arbitrary lattices.

The most serious quantitative issue is this:

  • The product-saving constant \(S(p)\) grows only like
\[ S(p)\sim \frac12\log(1/p). \]
  • If the lattice-counting error constants grow faster than \(O(\log(1/p))\), the parameter choice could fail.

So the next round should focus on proving Proposition G with quantitative constants that remain compatible with the growth of \(S(p)\).

Gap 2: Choosing an explicit bounded-root-discriminant tower

This is less serious. One may use known Martinet/Golod–Shafarevich totally real class-field towers.

The only thing needed for the construction is a finite absolute bound

\[ \operatorname{rd}(K_i)\le R_0. \]

Even if \(R_0\) is large, the parameter \(p\) can be taken small enough so that

\[ S(p)>\frac12\log R_0. \]

The resulting \(\delta\) may be extremely small, but it is still absolute.

Gap 3: Simultaneously controlling \(w\) and \(w^2\)

The weighted construction requires lattice counting for both

\[ \sigma_w(\mathcal O_K) \]

and

\[ \sigma_{w^2}(\mathcal O_K). \]

This is subtle because even if \(w\) balances the lattice, \(w^2\) might not. Possible ways to handle this:

  1. Choose \(w\) from a compact subset of the Arakelov torus whose doubling map remains controlled modulo units.
  2. Prove counting estimates uniformly for all degree-zero \(w\) in a large enough fundamental domain.
  3. Avoid weights entirely if the chosen tower has naturally well-rounded Minkowski lattices.

This is probably part of the same technical core as Gap 1.

9. Next steps for continuing the proof

The next round should try to prove Proposition G. A concrete program is:

  1. Work in Arakelov language. For a degree-zero divisor \(D=(D_\sigma)_\sigma\), define the weighted lattice

    \[ \Lambda_D = \{(e^{D_\sigma}\sigma(\alpha))_\sigma:\alpha\in\mathcal O_K\}. \]

    The determinant is unchanged when \(\sum_\sigma D_\sigma=0\).

  2. Find a semistable divisor \(D\). Use reduction theory to choose \(D\) so that \(\Lambda_D\) has no low-rank sublattice of abnormally small covolume.

  3. Prove \(L^p\)-ball lattice counting for semistable lattices. One target estimate is

    \[ |\Lambda_D\cap B_{q,d}(Y)| \le \exp(o(d)) \frac{\operatorname{vol}B_{q,d}(Y)}{\det\Lambda_D} \]

    for \(q=p,p/2\) and the relevant \(Y\)-values.

  4. Get the matching lower bound. Prove

    \[ |\Lambda_D\cap B_{p,d}(Td^{1/p})| \ge \exp(-o(d)) \frac{\operatorname{vol}B_{p,d}(Td^{1/p})}{\det\Lambda_D}. \]

  5. Control \(2D\). Since the product set is counted using \(w^2\), one needs the same type of upper bound for the lattice \(\Lambda_{2D}\).

If these steps are established, the conditional proof above becomes a full proof of the theorem.

10. Summary

\[ \log \operatorname{vol}\mathcal B_{q,d}(L) = d(\log L+u(q))+O_q(\log d), \]

\[ u(q) = \log\bigl(2\Gamma(1+1/q)\bigr) + \frac{1+\log q}{q}. \]

\[ B_{q,d}(Y)=\{x:\sum |x_j|^q\le Y^q\} \]

\[ Y^d\frac{2^d\Gamma(1+1/q)^d}{\Gamma(1+d/q)}. \]

\[ dx_j=\frac1q t_j^{1/q-1}dt_j, \]

\[ \log \Gamma(1+d/q) = \frac dq\log(d/q)-\frac dq+O_q(\log d). \]

\[ \begin{aligned} \log \operatorname{vol}\mathcal B_{q,d}(L) &= d\log L+\frac dq\log d +d\log\bigl(2\Gamma(1+1/q)\bigr) -\log\Gamma(1+d/q)\\ &= d\log L +d\log\bigl(2\Gamma(1+1/q)\bigr) +\frac dq(1+\log q) +O_q(\log d), \end{aligned} \]

4. Covering-radius estimate for Minkowski lattices

\[ \operatorname{covol}(\Lambda_K)=\sqrt{D_K}. \]

The main lattice fact is the following.

Lemma 4.1: Uniform covering radius

Suppose \(D_K^{1/d}\le \mathcal R\). Then the Euclidean covering radius of \(\Lambda_K\) satisfies

\[ \rho_2(\Lambda_K)\le \frac{\mathcal R}{2}\sqrt d. \]

Proof

\[ \Lambda_K^* = \sigma(\mathfrak D_K^{-1}). \]

Indeed, \(y=\sigma(\beta)\) pairs integrally with every \(\sigma(\alpha)\), \(\alpha\in\mathcal O_K\), precisely when

\[ \sum_{j=1}^d \sigma_j(\alpha)\sigma_j(\beta) = \operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbb Q}(\alpha\beta) \in\mathbb Z \]

for every \(\alpha\in\mathcal O_K\), which is the defining condition \(\beta\in\mathfrak D_K^{-1}\).

Now let \(0\neq \beta\in \mathfrak D_K^{-1}\). Since

\[ (\beta)\subseteq \mathfrak D_K^{-1}, \]

and ideal norm reverses inclusions, we have

\[ |N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\beta)| = N((\beta)) \ge N(\mathfrak D_K^{-1}) = \frac1{D_K}. \]

Therefore, by AM–GM,

\[ \|\sigma(\beta)\|_2^2 = \sum_{j=1}^d |\sigma_j(\beta)|^2 \ge d \left(\prod_{j=1}^d |\sigma_j(\beta)|^2\right)^{1/d} = d\, |N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\beta)|^{2/d} \ge d\,D_K^{-2/d}. \]

Since \(D_K^{1/d}\le\mathcal R\), this gives

\[ \|\sigma(\beta)\|_2 \ge \frac{\sqrt d}{\mathcal R}. \]

Hence

\[ \lambda_1(\Lambda_K^*)\ge \frac{\sqrt d}{\mathcal R}. \]

We now use the classical transference inequality for Euclidean lattices:

\[ \rho_2(\Lambda)\lambda_1(\Lambda^*)\le \frac d2. \]

Applying it to \(\Lambda_K\), we get

\[ \rho_2(\Lambda_K) \le \frac d{2\lambda_1(\Lambda_K^*)} \le \frac d{2(\sqrt d/\mathcal R)} = \frac{\mathcal R}{2}\sqrt d. \]

\(\square\)

Set from now on

\[ C:=\frac{\mathcal R}{2}. \]

Thus for every field \(K_i\) in our tower,

\[ \rho_2(\Lambda_{K_i})\le C\sqrt{d_i}. \]

This Euclidean covering bound implies covering bounds for the \(L^q\)-quasinorms.

\[ F\subseteq \mathcal B_{q,d}(C). \]

\[ \sum_{j=1}^d |x_j|^q \le d^{1-q/2}\|x\|_2^q. \]

\[ F\subseteq \mathcal B_{q,d}(C). \]

\(\square\)

12. Summary of the mechanism

The proof rests on four ingredients.

  2. Covering radius from the inverse different. The dual lattice of \(\sigma(\mathcal O_K)\) is \(\sigma(\mathfrak D_K^{-1})\), and every nonzero element of \(\mathfrak D_K^{-1}\) has norm at least \(D_K^{-1}\). Therefore the dual lattice has shortest vector at least \(\sqrt d/\mathcal R\), so by transference the Minkowski lattice has covering radius \(O_{\mathcal R}(\sqrt d)\).

  3. Volume-accurate counting in non-convex \(L^p\)-balls. The covering-radius bound implies that

    \[ N_{K,q}(L) \asymp \frac{\operatorname{vol}\mathcal B_{q,d}(L)} {\sqrt{D_K}} \]

    up to replacing \(L\) by \((L^q\pm C^q)^{1/q}\). Since \(L\) is chosen large, this fixed dilation is harmless.

  4. Entropy saving under coordinatewise multiplication. For \(0<p\le1\),

    \[ \mathcal B_p(T)\cdot \mathcal B_p(T) \subseteq \mathcal B_{p/2}(T^2), \]

    and

    \[ \frac{\operatorname{vol}\mathcal B_{p,d}(T)^2} {\operatorname{vol}\mathcal B_{p/2,d}(T^2)} = \exp(S(p)d+o(d)), \]

    where

    \[ S(p)\to\infty \qquad(p\to0^+). \]

    Taking \(p\) small beats the discriminant loss.

Together, these produce real sets with both sumset and product set of size at most \(|A|^{2-\delta}\).

I use two standard external inputs:

  1. Martinet’s theorem: there are totally real number fields of arbitrarily large degree with bounded root discriminant.

  2. The classical transference inequality for lattices:

    \[ \rho_2(\Lambda)\lambda_1(\Lambda^\ast)\le \frac d2. \]

Everything else is proved explicitly.

1. The \(L^q\)-bodies

For \(0<q\le 1\), \(d\ge 1\), and \(L>0\), define

\[ B_{q,d}(L) = \left\{ x=(x_1,\dots,x_d)\in\mathbb R^d: \sum_{j=1}^d |x_j|^q\le L^q d \right\}. \]

Let

\[ V_{q,d}(L)=\operatorname{vol} B_{q,d}(L). \]

Lemma 1.1: Volume formula

For every \(q>0\),

\[ V_{q,d}(L) = (Ld^{1/q})^d \frac{2^d\Gamma(1+1/q)^d}{\Gamma(1+d/q)}. \]

Consequently, for fixed \(q,L\),

\[ \log V_{q,d}(L) = d(\log L+u(q))+O_q(\log d), \]

where

\[ u(q) = \log\bigl(2\Gamma(1+1/q)\bigr) + \frac{1+\log q}{q}. \]

Proof

On the positive orthant, put \(t_j=x_j^q\). Then

\[ dx_j=\frac1q t_j^{1/q-1}\,dt_j. \]

The region becomes

\[ t_j\ge 0, \qquad t_1+\cdots+t_d\le L^q d. \]

By the Dirichlet integral,

\[ \int_{\sum t_j\le L^q d}\prod_{j=1}^d \frac1q t_j^{1/q-1}\,dt_j = (L^q d)^{d/q}\frac{\Gamma(1+1/q)^d}{\Gamma(1+d/q)}. \]

Multiplying by \(2^d\) for the orthants gives the formula.

The asymptotic follows from Stirling:

\[ \log\Gamma(1+d/q) = \frac dq\log(d/q)-\frac dq+O_q(\log d). \]

\(\square\)

Lemma 1.2: Additive and multiplicative closure

Let \(0<p\le 1\). Then

\[ B_{p,d}(T)+B_{p,d}(T) \subseteq B_{p,d}(2^{1/p}T), \]

and, with coordinatewise multiplication,

\[ B_{p,d}(T)\cdot B_{p,d}(T) \subseteq B_{p/2,d}(T^2). \]

Proof

For \(0<p\le1\),

\[ |a+b|^p\le |a|^p+|b|^p. \]

Thus, if \(x,y\in B_{p,d}(T)\), then

\[ \sum_j |x_j+y_j|^p \le \sum_j |x_j|^p+\sum_j |y_j|^p \le 2T^p d, \]

so \(x+y\in B_{p,d}(2^{1/p}T)\).

For products, by Cauchy–Schwarz,

\[ \sum_j |x_jy_j|^{p/2} = \sum_j |x_j|^{p/2}|y_j|^{p/2} \le \left(\sum_j |x_j|^p\right)^{1/2} \left(\sum_j |y_j|^p\right)^{1/2} \le T^p d. \]

Since

\[ (T^2)^{p/2}d=T^p d, \]

we get \(x\cdot y\in B_{p/2,d}(T^2)\). \(\square\)

2. The entropy saving under multiplication

Define

\[ S(p)=2u(p)-u(p/2). \]

Using the expression for \(u(q)\),

\[ S(p) = \log\left( 2^{1+2/p} \frac{\Gamma(1+1/p)^2}{\Gamma(1+2/p)} \right). \]

Lemma 2.1

As \(p\to0^+\),

\[ S(p)=\log 2+\frac12\log\frac{\pi}{p}+o(1). \]

In particular,

\[ S(p)\to+\infty. \]

Proof

Set \(z=1/p\). By Stirling,

\[ \frac{\Gamma(1+2z)}{\Gamma(1+z)^2} \sim \frac{4^z}{\sqrt{\pi z}}. \]

Hence

\[ 2^{1+2/p} \frac{\Gamma(1+1/p)^2}{\Gamma(1+2/p)} = 2\cdot 4^z \frac{\Gamma(1+z)^2}{\Gamma(1+2z)} \sim 2\sqrt{\pi z} = 2\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{p}}. \]

Taking logarithms proves the claim. \(\square\)

3. Number-field input

We use Martinet’s theorem.

Theorem 3.1: Totally real bounded-root-discriminant tower

There exists an absolute constant \(R_0<\infty\) and an infinite sequence of totally real number fields \(K_i\) such that

\[ d_i=[K_i:\mathbb Q]\to\infty \]

and

\[ D_{K_i}^{1/d_i}\le R_0 \]

for every \(i\), where \(D_{K_i}\) is the absolute discriminant.

Fix such a sequence.

4. Covering radius of the Minkowski lattice

Let \(K/\mathbb Q\) be totally real of degree \(d\). Let

\[ \sigma:K\hookrightarrow\mathbb R^d, \qquad \alpha\mapsto (\sigma_1(\alpha),\dots,\sigma_d(\alpha)) \]

be the Minkowski embedding, and set

\[ \Lambda_K=\sigma(\mathcal O_K). \]

Then

\[ \operatorname{covol}(\Lambda_K)=\sqrt{D_K}. \]

Lemma 4.1: Uniform covering radius

If \(D_K^{1/d}\le R_0\), then

\[ \rho_2(\Lambda_K)\le C\sqrt d \]

with

\[ C=\frac{R_0}{2}. \]

Proof

The Euclidean dual lattice is

\[ \Lambda_K^\ast=\sigma(\mathfrak D_K^{-1}), \]

where \(\mathfrak D_K\) is the different of \(K\). Indeed,

\[ \langle \sigma(\alpha),\sigma(\beta)\rangle = \operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbb Q}(\alpha\beta), \]

so \(\sigma(\beta)\) pairs integrally with \(\sigma(\mathcal O_K)\) exactly when

\[ \operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbb Q}(\beta\mathcal O_K)\subseteq \mathbb Z, \]

i.e. \(\beta\in\mathfrak D_K^{-1}\).

Now take \(0\ne\beta\in\mathfrak D_K^{-1}\). Since

\[ (\beta)\subseteq \mathfrak D_K^{-1}, \]

ideal norms give

\[ |N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\beta)| = N((\beta)) \ge N(\mathfrak D_K^{-1}) = D_K^{-1}. \]

Therefore, by AM–GM,

\[ \|\sigma(\beta)\|_2^2 = \sum_{j=1}^d |\sigma_j(\beta)|^2 \ge d\left(\prod_{j=1}^d |\sigma_j(\beta)|^2\right)^{1/d} = d|N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\beta)|^{2/d} \ge dD_K^{-2/d}. \]

Since \(D_K^{1/d}\le R_0\),

\[ \|\sigma(\beta)\|_2\ge \frac{\sqrt d}{R_0}. \]

Thus

\[ \lambda_1(\Lambda_K^\ast)\ge \frac{\sqrt d}{R_0}. \]

The standard transference inequality gives

\[ \rho_2(\Lambda_K)\lambda_1(\Lambda_K^\ast)\le \frac d2. \]

Hence

\[ \rho_2(\Lambda_K) \le \frac d{2(\sqrt d/R_0)} = \frac{R_0}{2}\sqrt d. \]

\(\square\)

Lemma 4.2: Fundamental domain inside every \(B_{q,d}(C)\)

Let \(0<q\le1\). If \(D_K^{1/d}\le R_0\), then \(\Lambda_K\) has a measurable fundamental domain \(F\) such that

\[ F\subseteq B_{q,d}(C), \]

where \(C=R_0/2\).

Proof

Take a measurable Voronoi fundamental domain \(F\) for \(\Lambda_K\). By Lemma 4.1,

\[ F\subseteq \{x\in\mathbb R^d:\|x\|_2\le C\sqrt d\}. \]

For \(0<q\le2\),

\[ \sum_{j=1}^d |x_j|^q \le d^{1-q/2}\|x\|_2^q. \]

Thus, if \(\|x\|_2\le C\sqrt d\), then

\[ \sum_j |x_j|^q \le d^{1-q/2}(C\sqrt d)^q = C^q d. \]

So \(x\in B_{q,d}(C)\). \(\square\)

5. Lattice-point counting in \(B_{q,d}(L)\)

For \(K\) totally real of degree \(d\), define

\[ N_{K,q}(L) = |\Lambda_K\cap B_{q,d}(L)| = \left| \left\{ \alpha\in\mathcal O_K: \sigma(\alpha)\in B_{q,d}(L) \right\} \right|. \]

Lemma 5.1: Counting by fixed dilation

Assume \(D_K^{1/d}\le R_0\), and let \(C=R_0/2\). Then for every \(0<q\le1\) and every \(L>0\),

\[ N_{K,q}(L) \le \frac{V_{q,d}\bigl((L^q+C^q)^{1/q}\bigr)}{\sqrt{D_K}}. \]

If \(L>C\), then

\[ N_{K,q}(L) \ge \frac{V_{q,d}\bigl((L^q-C^q)^{1/q}\bigr)}{\sqrt{D_K}}. \]

Proof

Let \(F\) be a measurable fundamental domain for \(\Lambda_K\) satisfying

\[ F\subseteq B_{q,d}(C). \]

For the upper bound, the translates \(\lambda+F\), \(\lambda\in\Lambda_K\cap B_{q,d}(L)\), are disjoint up to measure zero. If \(\lambda\in B_{q,d}(L)\) and \(f\in F\subseteq B_{q,d}(C)\), then

\[ \sum_j |\lambda_j+f_j|^q \le \sum_j |\lambda_j|^q+\sum_j |f_j|^q \le (L^q+C^q)d. \]

Thus

\[ \lambda+F\subseteq B_{q,d}\bigl((L^q+C^q)^{1/q}\bigr). \]

Therefore

\[ N_{K,q}(L)\sqrt{D_K} \le V_{q,d}\bigl((L^q+C^q)^{1/q}\bigr). \]

For the lower bound, set

\[ L_-=(L^q-C^q)^{1/q}. \]

Take \(x\in B_{q,d}(L_-)\). Since the translates \(\lambda+F\) partition \(\mathbb R^d\), write

\[ x=\lambda+f \]

with \(\lambda\in\Lambda_K\), \(f\in F\). Then

\[ \lambda=x-f. \]

By \(q\)-subadditivity,

\[ \sum_j |\lambda_j|^q \le \sum_j |x_j|^q+\sum_j |f_j|^q \le L_-^q d+C^q d = L^q d. \]

Thus \(\lambda\in B_{q,d}(L)\). Hence

\[ B_{q,d}(L_-) \subseteq \bigcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda_K\cap B_{q,d}(L)}(\lambda+F). \]

Taking volumes gives

\[ V_{q,d}(L_-) \le N_{K,q}(L)\sqrt{D_K}. \]

\(\square\)

6. Choosing parameters

Choose \(p\in(0,1]\) so small that

\[ S(p)>\frac12\log R_0. \]

This is possible by Lemma 2.1.

Fix

\[ C=\frac{R_0}{2}. \]

Now choose \(T>C\) sufficiently large. Define

\[ T_-=(T^p-C^p)^{1/p}, \]
\[ T_+=(T^p+C^p)^{1/p}, \]
\[ T_\Sigma=(2T^p+C^p)^{1/p}, \]

and

\[ T_\Pi=(T^p+C^{p/2})^{2/p}. \]

Set

\[ a= \log T_-+u(p)-\frac12\log R_0, \]
\[ b= \log\frac{T_\Sigma}{T_-}, \]

and

\[ s= S(p)+2\log T_- -\log T_\Pi-\frac12\log R_0. \]

As \(T\to\infty\),

\[ T_-\sim T, \qquad T_\Sigma\sim 2^{1/p}T, \qquad T_\Pi\sim T^2. \]

Therefore

\[ a\to+\infty, \]
\[ b\to \frac{\log2}{p}, \]

and

\[ s\to S(p)-\frac12\log R_0>0. \]

So we may choose \(T\) so large that

\[ a>b \]

and

\[ s>0. \]

Fix such \(p,T\).

Choose

\[ 0<\eta<\min(a-b,s). \]

Finally set

\[ M=\max\{1,\log T_+ +u(p)+1\}. \]

All of these constants are now absolute.

7. Constructing the sets

Let \(K_i\) be the totally real fields from Martinet’s theorem, with

\[ d_i=[K_i:\mathbb Q]\to\infty \]

and

\[ D_{K_i}^{1/d_i}\le R_0. \]

For each \(i\), define

\[ \mathcal A_i = \left\{ \alpha\in\mathcal O_{K_i}: \sigma(\alpha)\in B_{p,d_i}(T) \right\}. \]

Equivalently,

\[ \mathcal A_i = \left\{ \alpha\in\mathcal O_{K_i}: \sum_{j=1}^{d_i}|\sigma_j(\alpha)|^p\le T^p d_i \right\}. \]

Let

\[ n_i=|\mathcal A_i|. \]

We first estimate \(n_i\).

By Lemma 5.1,

\[ n_i = N_{K_i,p}(T) \ge \frac{V_{p,d_i}(T_-)}{\sqrt{D_{K_i}}}. \]

Using Lemma 1.1,

\[ \log V_{p,d_i}(T_-) = d_i(\log T_-+u(p))+O_p(\log d_i). \]

Since \(D_{K_i}^{1/d_i}\le R_0\),

\[ \log n_i \ge d_i\left(\log T_-+u(p)-\frac12\log R_0\right) + O_p(\log d_i) = a d_i+O_p(\log d_i). \]

Thus, for all sufficiently large \(i\),

\[ \log n_i\ge (a-\eta)d_i. \]

In particular, since \(a-\eta>b>0\),

\[ n_i\to\infty. \]

Similarly, Lemma 5.1 gives

\[ n_i \le \frac{V_{p,d_i}(T_+)}{\sqrt{D_{K_i}}} \le V_{p,d_i}(T_+). \]

Hence

\[ \log n_i \le d_i(\log T_+ + u(p))+O_p(\log d_i). \]

By the definition of \(M\), for all sufficiently large \(i\),

\[ \log n_i\le M d_i. \]

Discard finitely many \(i\), so the two inequalities

\[ \log n_i\ge (a-\eta)d_i \]

and

\[ \log n_i\le M d_i \]

hold for every remaining \(i\).

8. Sumset estimate

By Lemma 1.2,

\[ \mathcal A_i+\mathcal A_i \subseteq \left\{ \gamma\in\mathcal O_{K_i}: \sigma(\gamma)\in B_{p,d_i}(2^{1/p}T) \right\}. \]

Therefore

\[ |\mathcal A_i+\mathcal A_i| \le N_{K_i,p}(2^{1/p}T). \]

By Lemma 5.1,

\[ N_{K_i,p}(2^{1/p}T) \le \frac{V_{p,d_i}(T_\Sigma)}{\sqrt{D_{K_i}}}, \]

because

\[ \bigl((2^{1/p}T)^p+C^p\bigr)^{1/p} = (2T^p+C^p)^{1/p} = T_\Sigma. \]

Also,

\[ n_i\ge \frac{V_{p,d_i}(T_-)}{\sqrt{D_{K_i}}}. \]

Thus

\[ |\mathcal A_i+\mathcal A_i| \le n_i \frac{V_{p,d_i}(T_\Sigma)}{V_{p,d_i}(T_-)}. \]

Since the volume scales exactly as \(L^d\),

\[ \frac{V_{p,d_i}(T_\Sigma)}{V_{p,d_i}(T_-)} = \left(\frac{T_\Sigma}{T_-}\right)^{d_i} = e^{b d_i}. \]

Hence

\[ |\mathcal A_i+\mathcal A_i| \le n_i e^{b d_i}. \]

Using

\[ \log n_i\ge (a-\eta)d_i, \]

we have

\[ d_i\le \frac{\log n_i}{a-\eta}. \]

Therefore

\[ |\mathcal A_i+\mathcal A_i| \le n_i^{1+b/(a-\eta)}. \]

Since \(\eta<a-b\),

\[ b<a-\eta, \]

so

\[ \delta_+ = 1-\frac{b}{a-\eta} > 0. \]

Thus

\[ |\mathcal A_i+\mathcal A_i| \le n_i^{2-\delta_+}. \]

9. Product-set estimate

Set

\[ q=\frac p2. \]

By Lemma 1.2,

\[ \mathcal A_i\mathcal A_i \subseteq \left\{ \gamma\in\mathcal O_{K_i}: \sigma(\gamma)\in B_{q,d_i}(T^2) \right\}. \]

Thus

\[ |\mathcal A_i\mathcal A_i| \le N_{K_i,q}(T^2). \]

By Lemma 5.1,

\[ N_{K_i,q}(T^2) \le \frac{V_{q,d_i}(T_\Pi)}{\sqrt{D_{K_i}}}, \]

because

\[ \bigl((T^2)^q+C^q\bigr)^{1/q} = (T^p+C^{p/2})^{2/p} = T_\Pi. \]

Meanwhile,

\[ n_i \ge \frac{V_{p,d_i}(T_-)}{\sqrt{D_{K_i}}}. \]

Therefore

\[ |\mathcal A_i\mathcal A_i| \le n_i^2 \sqrt{D_{K_i}} \frac{V_{q,d_i}(T_\Pi)}{V_{p,d_i}(T_-)^2}. \]

Taking logarithms and using Lemma 1.1,

\[ \log V_{q,d_i}(T_\Pi) = d_i(\log T_\Pi+u(q))+O_p(\log d_i), \]

and

\[ \log V_{p,d_i}(T_-) = d_i(\log T_-+u(p))+O_p(\log d_i). \]

Thus

\[ \begin{aligned} \log\left( \sqrt{D_{K_i}} \frac{V_{q,d_i}(T_\Pi)}{V_{p,d_i}(T_-)^2} \right) &\le \frac12 d_i\log R_0\\ &\quad+ d_i(\log T_\Pi+u(q))\\ &\quad- 2d_i(\log T_-+u(p)) + O_p(\log d_i). \end{aligned} \]

Since \(q=p/2\) and

\[ S(p)=2u(p)-u(p/2)=2u(p)-u(q), \]

the main term equals

\[ -d_i\left( S(p)+2\log T_- -\log T_\Pi-\frac12\log R_0 \right) + O_p(\log d_i). \]

By definition, the expression in parentheses is \(s>0\). Therefore, for all sufficiently large \(i\),

\[ \sqrt{D_{K_i}} \frac{V_{q,d_i}(T_\Pi)}{V_{p,d_i}(T_-)^2} \le e^{-(s-\eta)d_i}. \]

Hence

\[ |\mathcal A_i\mathcal A_i| \le n_i^2 e^{-(s-\eta)d_i}. \]

Using

\[ \log n_i\le M d_i, \]

we get

\[ d_i\ge \frac{\log n_i}{M}. \]

Therefore

\[ |\mathcal A_i\mathcal A_i| \le n_i^2 \exp\left(-\frac{s-\eta}{M}\log n_i\right) = n_i^{2-(s-\eta)/M}. \]

Set

\[ \delta_\times = \min\left(1,\frac{s-\eta}{M}\right)>0. \]

Then

\[ |\mathcal A_i\mathcal A_i| \le n_i^{2-\delta_\times}. \]

10. Embedding into \(\mathbb R\)

For each \(i\), choose one real embedding

\[ \iota_i:K_i\hookrightarrow\mathbb R. \]

Define

\[ A_i=\iota_i(\mathcal A_i)\subset\mathbb R. \]

Because \(\iota_i\) is injective,

\[ |A_i|=|\mathcal A_i|=n_i. \]

Also,

\[ A_i+A_i=\iota_i(\mathcal A_i+\mathcal A_i), \]

and

\[ A_iA_i=\iota_i(\mathcal A_i\mathcal A_i). \]

Thus

\[ |A_i+A_i| = |\mathcal A_i+\mathcal A_i|, \]

and

\[ |A_iA_i| = |\mathcal A_i\mathcal A_i|. \]

Let

\[ \delta=\min(\delta_+,\delta_\times)>0. \]

Then for every sufficiently large \(i\),

\[ |A_i+A_i|\le |A_i|^{2-\delta}, \]

and

\[ |A_iA_i|\le |A_i|^{2-\delta}. \]

Since \(|A_i|=n_i\to\infty\), after discarding finitely many initial terms and reindexing, we obtain an infinite sequence of finite sets \(A_i\subset\mathbb R\) such that

\[ |A_i|\to\infty \]

and

\[ \max(|A_i+A_i|,|A_iA_i|) \le |A_i|^{2-\delta} \]

for every \(i\).

This proves the theorem. \(\square\)
