Below is the approach I would pursue first. It is not the “usual” smooth-number/integer-multiplication-table approach; that approach seems unable to give a fixed power saving. The more promising mechanism is:

Use high-degree totally real number fields with many small units. Units give multiplicative collisions, while algebraic-integer boxes give additive density.

1. Core idea

Suppose \(K/\mathbb Q\) is totally real of degree \(d\), with embeddings

\[ \sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_d:K\hookrightarrow \mathbb R. \]

Let \(\mathcal O_K\) be its ring of integers. We will construct a finite set

\[ A\subset \mathcal O_K \]

and then view it as a subset of \(\mathbb R\) through one fixed embedding, say \(\sigma_1\).

The construction has two pieces:

  1. A set \(U\) of small totally positive units:

    \[ U\subset \mathcal O_K^{\times,+}, \]

    where “small” means

    \[ e^{-L}\le \sigma_j(u)\le e^L \qquad \text{for all } j=1,\dots,d. \]

    In high-degree fields with bounded root discriminant, there are exponentially many such units in \(d\), and their product set is still only exponentially larger:

    \[ |U|\ge \exp(c_1 d),\qquad |UU|\le \exp(c_2 d)|U|. \]

    More quantitatively, one gets \(|U|\asymp L^d\) and \(|UU|\ll (2L)^d\), so \(|UU|\ll |U|^2/L^d\), giving a power saving.

  2. A dense additive box \(B\subset \mathcal O_K\), shifted far away from the origin:

    \[ B=T+C,\qquad C=\{c\in \mathcal O_K: |\sigma_j(c)|\le R\ \forall j\}. \]

    The shift \(T\in \mathbb Z\) is chosen large compared to \(R\), so that the translates \(uB\), \(u\in U\), are disjoint.

Then define

\[ A:=UB=\{u b: u\in U,\ b\in B\}. \]

The key estimates are:

  • Because the sets \(uB\) are disjoint,
\[ |A|=|U||B|. \]
  • The product set is controlled by
\[ AA\subset (UU)(BB), \]

hence

\[ |AA|\le |UU||BB|\le |UU||B|^2. \]

Since \(|A|^2=|U|^2|B|^2\), we save roughly a factor \(|U|^2/|UU|\), which is exponential in \(d\).

  • The sumset is controlled because \(A\) lies in an archimedean box:
\[ |\sigma_j(a)|\le e^L(T+R), \]

so

\[ A+A\subset \{x\in \mathcal O_K: |\sigma_j(x)|\le 2e^L(T+R)\ \forall j\}. \]

Algebraic integers in such a box are separated in sup-norm, so this gives an upper bound of the shape

\[ |A+A|\le \exp(Cd). \]

Choosing the constants \(L,R,T\) carefully gives

\[ |A+A|,\ |AA|\le |A|^{2-\delta} \]

for an absolute \(\delta>0\).

2. Number-theoretic inputs

The approach needs the following standard inputs.

Input 1: Totally real bounded-root-discriminant tower

There exists an absolute constant \(\rho>1\) and infinitely many totally real fields \(K_i/\mathbb Q\) with degrees

\[ d_i=[K_i:\mathbb Q]\to\infty \]

such that

\[ |D_{K_i}|^{1/d_i}\le \rho. \]

This is known from Martinet’s construction of infinite totally real class-field towers. For the purposes of this proof plan, I will take it as an external input.

Input 2: Regulator bound in bounded-root-discriminant towers

For those fields \(K_i\), the regulator satisfies

\[ R_{K_i}\le C_{\mathrm{reg}}^{d_i} \]

for some absolute constant \(C_{\mathrm{reg}}\).

This follows from the analytic class number formula plus a standard Landau-type upper bound for the residue of \(\zeta_K(s)\) at \(s=1\). I spell out the argument below in §3.3.

Input 3: Schinzel’s height lower bound for totally real units

There is an absolute constant \(c_{\mathrm{Sch}}>0\) such that if \(K\) is totally real and \(u\in \mathcal O_K^\times\) is a nontrivial unit, then

\[ \max_{1\le j\le d} |\log |\sigma_j(u)||\ge c_{\mathrm{Sch}}. \]

One may take, for example,

\[ c_{\mathrm{Sch}}=\frac12\log\varphi, \qquad \varphi=\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2}, \]

up to harmless changes in constants. This is a theorem of Schinzel: a totally real algebraic integer \(\alpha\notin\{-1,0,1\}\) has Mahler measure at least \(\varphi^{\deg(\alpha)/2}\).

This gives a uniform separation property for the logarithmic unit lattice.

3. Preliminary lemmas

We now prove the elementary pieces needed for the construction.

3.1. Minkowski embedding and algebraic-integer separation

Let \(K/\mathbb Q\) be totally real of degree \(d\). Write

\[ \Phi:K\hookrightarrow \mathbb R^d, \qquad \Phi(x)=(\sigma_1(x),\dots,\sigma_d(x)). \]

The image \(\Phi(\mathcal O_K)\) is a lattice in \(\mathbb R^d\) of covolume

\[ \operatorname{covol}(\Phi(\mathcal O_K))=\sqrt{|D_K|}. \]

We will use the sup-norm

\[ \|x\|_\infty=\max_j |x_j|. \]

Lemma 3.1: Algebraic integers are sup-norm separated

If \(x,y\in \mathcal O_K\) and \(x\ne y\), then

\[ \|\Phi(x)-\Phi(y)\|_\infty\ge 1. \]

Proof

Let \(\alpha=x-y\in \mathcal O_K\setminus\{0\}\). Then

\[ N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\alpha)=\prod_{j=1}^d \sigma_j(\alpha) \]

is a nonzero integer, so

\[ |N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\alpha)|\ge 1. \]

If \(|\sigma_j(\alpha)|<1\) for all \(j\), then the product would have absolute value \(<1\), contradiction. Hence some \(j\) satisfies \(|\sigma_j(\alpha)|\ge 1\). Therefore

\[ \|\Phi(x)-\Phi(y)\|_\infty\ge 1. \]

\(\square\)

3.2. Counting lattice points in symmetric convex bodies

We need a lower bound of van der Corput type.

Lemma 3.2: van der Corput lower bound

Let \(\Lambda\subset \mathbb R^n\) be a lattice of covolume \(\det\Lambda\), and let \(K\subset \mathbb R^n\) be centrally symmetric and convex. Then

\[ |\Lambda\cap K| \ge \frac{\operatorname{vol}(K)}{2^n\det\Lambda}. \]

Up to harmless floor/constant issues, this is the usual van der Corput lemma.

Proof sketch

Average the number of lattice points in translates of \(\frac12 K\) over the torus \(\mathbb R^n/\Lambda\). The average is

\[ \frac{\operatorname{vol}(K/2)}{\det\Lambda} = \frac{\operatorname{vol}(K)}{2^n\det\Lambda}. \]

Thus some translate \(x+\frac12K\) contains at least this many lattice points. Fix one such point \(p\). Differences between the other points and \(p\) are distinct elements of \(\Lambda\cap K\), since

\[ (x+\tfrac12K)-(x+\tfrac12K)\subset K. \]

This gives the claimed lower bound. \(\square\)

3.3. Regulator bound from bounded root discriminant

Let \(K\) be totally real of degree \(d\), discriminant \(D_K\), regulator \(R_K\), class number \(h_K\), and residue

\[ \kappa_K=\operatorname*{Res}_{s=1}\zeta_K(s). \]

The analytic class number formula gives

\[ \kappa_K = \frac{2^{d-1}h_KR_K}{\sqrt{|D_K|}}. \]

Thus

\[ R_K \le h_KR_K = \frac{\kappa_K\sqrt{|D_K|}}{2^{d-1}}. \]

A standard Landau bound gives

\[ \kappa_K \le \left(\frac{e\log |D_K|}{2(d-1)}\right)^{d-1} \]

for \(d\ge 2\). If \(|D_K|^{1/d}\le \rho\), then

\[ \log |D_K|\le d\log \rho, \]

so

\[ \kappa_K\le C_\rho^d \]

for some constant \(C_\rho\). Also

\[ \sqrt{|D_K|}\le \rho^{d/2}. \]

Therefore

\[ R_K\le C_{\mathrm{reg}}^d \]

for some absolute constant \(C_{\mathrm{reg}}\) depending only on \(\rho\).

Now let

\[ \mathcal O_K^{\times,+} = \{u\in \mathcal O_K^\times:\sigma_j(u)>0\ \forall j\} \]

be the group of totally positive units. Its logarithmic embedding is

\[ \ell:\mathcal O_K^{\times,+}\to H_d, \qquad \ell(u)=(\log\sigma_1(u),\dots,\log\sigma_d(u)), \]

where

\[ H_d=\left\{x\in \mathbb R^d:\sum_{j=1}^d x_j=0\right\}. \]

The lattice

\[ \Lambda_K^+:=\ell(\mathcal O_K^{\times,+}) \subset H_d \]

has rank \(d-1\). Its covolume is at most \(2^d\sqrt d\,R_K\), because the totally positive units form a sublattice of index at most \(2^d\) in the full unit lattice. Thus, after enlarging constants,

\[ \operatorname{covol}(\Lambda_K^+)\le C_0^d \]

for some absolute \(C_0\).

3.4. Volume of cube sections

Let

\[ Q_d(L):=H_d\cap [-L,L]^d. \]

There are absolute constants \(c_\square,C_\square>0\) such that

\[ (c_\square L)^{d-1} \le \operatorname{vol}_{H_d}(Q_d(L)) \le (C_\square L)^{d-1}. \]

This is a standard estimate for central hyperplane sections of cubes. One way to see it is to identify the section volume with the density at \(0\) of the sum of \(d\) independent uniform random variables on \([-L,L]\), up to a factor \(\sqrt d\), and then use the local central limit theorem. Ball’s cube-slicing theorem also gives this directly.

For our purposes, the precise constants are irrelevant.

3.5. Counting small totally positive units

For \(L\ge 1\), define

\[ U_K(L) := \{u\in \mathcal O_K^{\times,+}: \|\ell(u)\|_\infty\le L\}. \]

Lemma 3.3: Lower and upper bounds for \(U_K(L)\)

There exist absolute constants \(a,b>0\), depending only on the bounded-root-discriminant tower, such that for all sufficiently large \(L\) and all \(K\) in the tower,

\[ |U_K(L)|\ge (aL)^d, \]

and

\[ |U_K(2L)|\le (bL)^d. \]

Proof

Lower bound. Apply Lemma 3.2 to the lattice \(\Lambda_K^+\subset H_d\) and the symmetric convex body \(Q_d(L)\). Since

\[ \operatorname{covol}(\Lambda_K^+)\le C_0^d \]

and

\[ \operatorname{vol}(Q_d(L))\ge (c_\square L)^{d-1}, \]

we get

\[ |U_K(L)| = |\Lambda_K^+\cap Q_d(L)| \ge \frac{(c_\square L)^{d-1}}{2^{d-1}C_0^d}. \]

After changing constants and assuming \(L\) is large enough, this gives

\[ |U_K(L)|\ge (aL)^d. \]

Upper bound. By Schinzel’s theorem, if \(u\in \mathcal O_K^{\times,+}\) is nontrivial, then

\[ \|\ell(u)\|_\infty\ge c_{\mathrm{Sch}}. \]

Therefore distinct points of the lattice \(\Lambda_K^+\) are separated by at least \(c_{\mathrm{Sch}}\) in sup-norm.

Hence the translates

\[ x+Q_d(c_{\mathrm{Sch}}/2), \qquad x\in \Lambda_K^+\cap Q_d(2L), \]

have disjoint interiors and all lie in

\[ Q_d(2L+c_{\mathrm{Sch}}/2). \]

Using the cube-section volume estimates,

\[ |U_K(2L)| \le \frac{\operatorname{vol}(Q_d(2L+c_{\mathrm{Sch}}/2))} {\operatorname{vol}(Q_d(c_{\mathrm{Sch}}/2))} \le (bL)^d \]

for some absolute \(b>0\). \(\square\)

3.6. Counting algebraic integers in archimedean boxes

For \(R\ge 1\), define

\[ C_K(R):=\{c\in \mathcal O_K: \|\Phi(c)\|_\infty\le R\}. \]

Lemma 3.4: Size of \(C_K(R)\)

If \(|D_K|^{1/d}\le \rho\), then

\[ |C_K(R)|\ge \left(\frac{R}{\sqrt\rho}\right)^d. \]

Also,

\[ |C_K(R)|\le (3R)^d. \]

Proof

The lower bound follows from Lemma 3.2 applied to \(\Phi(\mathcal O_K)\subset \mathbb R^d\) and the cube \([-R,R]^d\). Since the covolume is \(\sqrt{|D_K|}\le \rho^{d/2}\),

\[ |C_K(R)| \ge \frac{(2R)^d}{2^d\sqrt{|D_K|}} \ge \left(\frac{R}{\sqrt\rho}\right)^d. \]

For the upper bound, use Lemma 3.1. Distinct algebraic integers are separated by at least \(1\) in sup-norm. Thus the closed cubes of side length \(1\) centered at the points of \(\Phi(C_K(R))\) have disjoint interiors and lie inside \([-R-1/2,R+1/2]^d\). Hence

\[ |C_K(R)|\le (2R+1)^d\le (3R)^d \]

for \(R\ge 1\). \(\square\)

4. The construction inside one field

Fix one field \(K\) in the tower, of degree \(d\).

Let \(L\ge 1\) and \(R\ge 1\) be constants to be chosen later, independently of \(K\). Put

\[ U:=U_K(L) = \{u\in \mathcal O_K^{\times,+}: \|\ell(u)\|_\infty\le L\}. \]

Let

\[ C:=C_K(R) = \{c\in \mathcal O_K: \|\Phi(c)\|_\infty\le R\}. \]

We will shift \(C\) by a large rational integer \(T\).

Let

\[ \alpha:=1-e^{-c_{\mathrm{Sch}}}>0. \]

Choose

\[ \kappa:=\frac{2(1+e^{2L})}{\alpha} \]

and take an integer

\[ T\ge \kappa R. \]

Define

\[ B:=T+C=\{T+c:c\in C\}. \]

Finally define

\[ A_K:=UB=\{u(T+c):u\in U,\ c\in C\}\subset \mathcal O_K. \]

Later we set

\[ A:=\sigma_1(A_K)\subset \mathbb R. \]

Because \(\sigma_1\) is injective and preserves \(+\) and \(\cdot\), all cardinality estimates for \(A_K\) carry over to \(A\).

5. Disjointness of the unit translates

We need

\[ |A_K|=|U||C|. \]

For that it suffices to prove that the sets

\[ uB,\qquad u\in U, \]

are disjoint.

Lemma 5.1: The translates \(uB\) are disjoint

If \(u,v\in U\) and \(uB\cap vB\ne \varnothing\), then \(u=v\).

Proof

Suppose

\[ u(T+c)=v(T+c') \]

for some \(c,c'\in C\). If \(u=v\), then \(c=c'\). Suppose \(u\ne v\). Then \(w:=u/v\) is a nontrivial totally positive unit.

By Schinzel’s separation,

\[ \|\ell(w)\|_\infty\ge c_{\mathrm{Sch}}. \]

Thus for some embedding \(j\),

\[ |\log\sigma_j(w)|\ge c_{\mathrm{Sch}}. \]

Since \(w\) is totally positive,

\[ |\sigma_j(w)-1|\ge \alpha. \]

Also, because \(u,v\in U_K(L)\),

\[ e^{-2L}\le \sigma_j(w)\le e^{2L}. \]

Apply \(\sigma_j\) to the equality \(u(T+c)=v(T+c')\) and divide by \(\sigma_j(v)\). We get

\[ \sigma_j(w)(T+\sigma_j(c))=T+\sigma_j(c'). \]

Rearranging,

\[ (\sigma_j(w)-1)T = \sigma_j(c')-\sigma_j(w)\sigma_j(c). \]

Taking absolute values,

\[ \alpha T \le |\sigma_j(w)-1|T \le |\sigma_j(c')|+|\sigma_j(w)||\sigma_j(c)| \le R+e^{2L}R = (1+e^{2L})R. \]

But \(T\ge \kappa R\), and

\[ \kappa=\frac{2(1+e^{2L})}{\alpha}, \]

so

\[ \alpha T \ge 2(1+e^{2L})R, \]

a contradiction. Therefore \(u=v\). \(\square\)

Thus

\[ |A_K|=|U||C|. \]

Let

\[ N:=|A_K|. \]

By Lemmas 3.3 and 3.4,

\[ N=|U||C| \ge (aL)^d\left(\frac{R}{\sqrt\rho}\right)^d = \left(\frac{aLR}{\sqrt\rho}\right)^d. \]

Also,

\[ N \le |U_K(2L)|\,|C| \le (bL)^d(3R)^d = (3bLR)^d. \]

Define

\[ n_-:=\log\left(\frac{aLR}{\sqrt\rho}\right), \qquad n_+:=\log(3bLR). \]

Then

\[ e^{n_-d}\le N\le e^{n_+d}. \]

6. Sumset estimate

Every element of \(A_K\) has the form

\[ u(T+c), \qquad u\in U,\ c\in C. \]

For every embedding \(\sigma_j\),

\[ |\sigma_j(u(T+c))| \le e^L(T+R). \]

Since \(T\le \kappa R+1\), and \(R\ge 1\), we may absorb constants and write

\[ T+R\le (\kappa+2)R. \]

Thus

\[ |\sigma_j(a)|\le e^L(\kappa+2)R \qquad \forall a\in A_K,\ \forall j. \]

Therefore for \(a,a'\in A_K\),

\[ |\sigma_j(a+a')| \le 2e^L(\kappa+2)R. \]

By Lemma 3.1, the number of algebraic integers whose conjugates all have absolute value at most \(S\) is at most \((2S+1)^d\). Taking

\[ S=2e^L(\kappa+2)R, \]

we get

\[ |A_K+A_K| \le \left(4e^L(\kappa+2)R+1\right)^d. \]

For simplicity, define

\[ s:=\log\left(5e^L(\kappa+2)R\right). \]

Then

\[ |A_K+A_K|\le e^{sd}. \]

Since \(N\ge e^{n_-d}\), we get

\[ |A_K+A_K| \le N^{s/n_-}. \]

Thus, if we choose \(R\) so that

\[ \frac{s}{n_-}<2, \]

then the sumset satisfies a power saving.

Equivalently, we need

\[ 5e^L(\kappa+2)R < \left(\frac{aLR}{\sqrt\rho}\right)^2. \]

For fixed \(L\), the left-hand side grows linearly in \(R\), while the right-hand side grows quadratically in \(R\). Therefore this inequality holds for all sufficiently large constant \(R\).

Define

\[ \delta_+:=2-\frac{s}{n_-}>0. \]

Then

\[ |A_K+A_K|\le N^{2-\delta_+}. \]

7. Product-set estimate

We have

\[ A_KA_K\subset (UU)(BB). \]

Therefore

\[ |A_KA_K|\le |UU|\,|BB|. \]

Since \(UU\subset U_K(2L)\),

\[ |UU|\le |U_K(2L)|\le (bL)^d. \]

Also trivially

\[ |BB|\le |B|^2=|C|^2. \]

Hence

\[ |A_KA_K| \le (bL)^d |C|^2. \]

But

\[ N=|U||C| \]

and

\[ |U|\ge (aL)^d. \]

Thus

\[ N^2 = |U|^2|C|^2 \ge (aL)^{2d}|C|^2. \]

Therefore

\[ |A_KA_K| \le N^2\left(\frac{bL}{(aL)^2}\right)^d. \]

Define

\[ \gamma:=2\log(aL)-\log(bL) = \log\left(\frac{(aL)^2}{bL}\right). \]

If \(L\) is chosen large enough, then

\[ \gamma>0. \]

Then

\[ |A_KA_K|\le N^2e^{-\gamma d}. \]

Since \(N\le e^{n_+d}\), we have

\[ e^{-\gamma d}\le N^{-\gamma/n_+}. \]

Thus

\[ |A_KA_K| \le N^{2-\delta_\times}, \]

where

\[ \delta_\times:=\frac{\gamma}{n_+}>0. \]

8. Choice of parameters

We now choose constants once and for all.

  1. Choose \(L\ge 1\) so large that

    \[ \gamma=2\log(aL)-\log(bL)>0. \]

    This is possible because

    \[ \gamma=\log L + O(1)\to\infty. \]

  2. With this \(L\), define

    \[ \alpha:=1-e^{-c_{\mathrm{Sch}}}, \qquad \kappa:=\frac{2(1+e^{2L})}{\alpha}. \]

  3. Choose \(R\ge 1\) sufficiently large that

    \[ s=\log(5e^L(\kappa+2)R) < 2\log\left(\frac{aLR}{\sqrt\rho}\right) = 2n_-. \]

    This is possible because as \(R\to\infty\),

    \[ \frac{s}{n_-}\to 1. \]

Then define

\[ \delta:=\min\left(\delta_+,\delta_\times\right)>0. \]

This \(\delta\) is absolute because \(L,R,a,b,\rho\) are absolute constants.

9. Passing to infinitely many real sets

Let \(K_i\) be the infinite sequence of totally real fields from Input 1, with degrees \(d_i\to\infty\). For each \(K_i\), construct \(A_{K_i}\subset \mathcal O_{K_i}\) as above.

Fix one real embedding

\[ \sigma_{i,1}:K_i\hookrightarrow \mathbb R \]

and define

\[ A_i:=\sigma_{i,1}(A_{K_i})\subset \mathbb R. \]

Because \(\sigma_{i,1}\) is an injective ring homomorphism, we have

\[ |A_i|=|A_{K_i}|, \]
\[ |A_i+A_i|=|A_{K_i}+A_{K_i}|, \]

and

\[ |A_iA_i|=|A_{K_i}A_{K_i}|. \]

From the construction,

\[ |A_i|\ge e^{n_-d_i}\to\infty. \]

Also,

\[ |A_i+A_i|\le |A_i|^{2-\delta}, \]

and

\[ |A_iA_i|\le |A_i|^{2-\delta}. \]

Therefore

\[ \max(|A_i+A_i|,\ |A_iA_i|) \le |A_i|^{2-\delta} \]

for all \(i\).

This proves the theorem, modulo the standard number-theoretic inputs listed above.

10. Step-by-step proof plan for completion

Here is the distilled proof plan.

Step 1: Obtain a totally real bounded-root-discriminant tower

Prove or cite:

There exist totally real fields \(K_i\) with degrees \(d_i\to\infty\) and \(|D_{K_i}|^{1/d_i}\le \rho\).

This is known from Martinet’s class-field-tower construction.

Status: Standard external input. Obstruction level: Not a conceptual obstruction to the sum-product construction; proving it self-contained would require substantial algebraic number theory.

Step 2: Bound regulators in the tower

Using the analytic class number formula and Landau’s residue bound, prove:

\[ R_{K_i}\le C_{\mathrm{reg}}^{d_i}. \]

Then for the totally positive unit lattice

\[ \Lambda_i^+=\ell(\mathcal O_{K_i}^{\times,+})\subset H_{d_i}, \]

show

\[ \operatorname{covol}(\Lambda_i^+)\le C_0^{d_i}. \]

Status: Essentially proved above. Obstruction level: Routine analytic number theory.

Step 3: Count small totally positive units

For

\[ U_i(L)=\{u\in \mathcal O_{K_i}^{\times,+}: \|\ell(u)\|_\infty\le L\}, \]

prove

\[ |U_i(L)|\ge (aL)^{d_i}, \]

and

\[ |U_i(2L)|\le (bL)^{d_i}. \]

The lower bound uses van der Corput and the regulator bound. The upper bound uses Schinzel’s separation theorem for totally real units.

Status: Proved modulo Schinzel’s theorem and the cube-section volume estimate. Obstruction level: Standard; no serious obstruction.

Step 4: Count algebraic integers in boxes

For

\[ C_i(R)=\{c\in \mathcal O_{K_i}: \|\Phi_i(c)\|_\infty\le R\}, \]

prove

\[ |C_i(R)|\ge (R/\sqrt\rho)^{d_i}, \]

and

\[ |C_i(R)|\le (3R)^{d_i}. \]

Status: Proved above. Obstruction level: None.

Step 5: Build the set

Choose constants \(L,R,T\) as above and define

\[ A_{K_i}:= \{u(T+c):u\in U_i(L),\ c\in C_i(R)\}. \]

Prove the translates \(u(T+C_i(R))\) are disjoint using Schinzel separation and the choice \(T\gg e^{2L}R\). Hence

\[ |A_{K_i}|=|U_i(L)|\,|C_i(R)|. \]

Status: Proved above. Obstruction level: None.

Step 6: Sumset bound

Use the archimedean box containment:

\[ A_{K_i}+A_{K_i} \subset \{x\in \mathcal O_{K_i}: |\sigma_j(x)|\le 2e^L(T+R)\ \forall j\}. \]

Then use algebraic-integer separation to bound the size by \(\exp(sd_i)\). Choose \(R\) large enough so

\[ s<2n_-, \]

where

\[ n_-=\log(aLR/\sqrt\rho). \]

Then

\[ |A_{K_i}+A_{K_i}| \le |A_{K_i}|^{2-\delta_+}. \]

Status: Proved above. Obstruction level: None.

Step 7: Product-set bound

Use

\[ A_{K_i}A_{K_i}\subset U_i(2L)\cdot (B_iB_i). \]

Since \(|B_iB_i|\le |B_i|^2\),

\[ |A_{K_i}A_{K_i}| \le |U_i(2L)|\,|B_i|^2. \]

But

\[ |A_{K_i}|=|U_i(L)|\,|B_i|. \]

Thus

\[ |A_{K_i}A_{K_i}| \le |A_{K_i}|^2 \frac{|U_i(2L)|}{|U_i(L)|^2}. \]

The unit estimates give

\[ \frac{|U_i(2L)|}{|U_i(L)|^2} \le \exp(-\gamma d_i) \]

for \(L\) large. Therefore

\[ |A_{K_i}A_{K_i}| \le |A_{K_i}|^{2-\delta_\times}. \]

Status: Proved above. Obstruction level: None.

12. Summary

The most promising approach is to exploit high-degree totally real fields with many small units. The construction is:

\[ A_i = \sigma_{i,1}\Bigl(\{u(T+c):u\in U_i(L),\ c\in C_i(R)\}\Bigr) \subset \mathbb R, \]

where

\[ U_i(L)=\{u\in \mathcal O_{K_i}^{\times,+}: \|\ell(u)\|_\infty\le L\} \]

and

\[ C_i(R)=\{c\in \mathcal O_{K_i}: \|\Phi_i(c)\|_\infty\le R\}. \]

The set \(U_i(L)\) supplies multiplicative structure; the set \(C_i(R)\) supplies additive density. The shift \(T\) ensures the unit translates are disjoint, so

\[ |A_i|=|U_i(L)|\,|C_i(R)|. \]

Then:

\[ |A_i+A_i| \le |A_i|^{2-\delta_+}, \]

because \(A_i+A_i\) lies inside a controlled archimedean box of algebraic integers, and

\[ |A_iA_i| \le |A_i|^{2-\delta_\times}, \]

because

\[ A_iA_i\subset U_i(2L)\cdot (B_iB_i) \]

and

\[ |U_i(2L)|\ll |U_i(L)|^2 e^{-\gamma d_i}. \]

Choosing \(L,R,T\) as above yields an absolute

\[ \delta=\min(\delta_+,\delta_\times)>0. \]

The remaining inputs are standard number-theoretic facts, especially the existence of totally real bounded-root-discriminant towers. Once those are supplied, the construction gives the desired infinite sequence of finite real sets.

Complete proof

We prove the theorem using high-degree totally real number fields of bounded root discriminant and many small units. The construction is exactly of the type outlined earlier, but I will now keep all constants and rank-\((d-1)\) issues explicit.

Throughout, if \(K/\mathbb Q\) is totally real of degree \(d\), write its real embeddings as

\[ \sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_d:K\hookrightarrow \mathbb R, \]

and write

\[ \Phi:K\hookrightarrow \mathbb R^d,\qquad \Phi(x)=(\sigma_1(x),\dots,\sigma_d(x)). \]

We also write

\[ \|x\|_\infty=\max_j |x_j| \]

for the sup norm on \(\mathbb R^d\).

We shall use the following three standard number-theoretic facts.

1. Standard number-theoretic inputs

1.1. Martinet’s totally real bounded-root-discriminant tower

There exists an absolute constant \(\rho>1\) and an infinite sequence of totally real number fields \(K_i/\mathbb Q\) with degrees

\[ d_i=[K_i:\mathbb Q]\to\infty \]

such that

\[ |D_{K_i}|^{1/d_i}\le \rho \]

for every \(i\).

This is a theorem of Martinet, obtained using Golod–Shafarevich class-field towers. The precise numerical value of \(\rho\) is irrelevant.

1.2. Regulator bound in such a tower

For every field \(K_i\) in this tower, its regulator satisfies

\[ R_{K_i}\le C_{\mathrm{reg}}^{d_i} \]

for some absolute constant \(C_{\mathrm{reg}}\).

Indeed, let \(K\) be totally real of degree \(d\), discriminant \(D_K\), class number \(h_K\), regulator \(R_K\), and residue

\[ \kappa_K=\operatorname*{Res}_{s=1}\zeta_K(s). \]

The analytic class number formula gives

\[ \kappa_K=\frac{2^{d-1}h_KR_K}{\sqrt{|D_K|}}. \]

Hence

\[ R_K\le h_KR_K =\frac{\kappa_K\sqrt{|D_K|}}{2^{d-1}}. \]

Landau’s residue bound gives, for an absolute constant \(C_L\),

\[ \kappa_K\le C_L^d \left(1+\frac{\log |D_K|}{d}\right)^d. \]

Since \(|D_K|^{1/d}\le \rho\), we have \(\log |D_K|\le d\log \rho\), so

\[ \kappa_K\le C_\rho^d \]

for some absolute constant \(C_\rho\). Also

\[ \sqrt{|D_K|}\le \rho^{d/2}. \]

Therefore

\[ R_K\le C_{\mathrm{reg}}^d \]

for a suitable absolute constant \(C_{\mathrm{reg}}\).

Now let

\[ \mathcal O_K^{\times,+} = \{u\in \mathcal O_K^\times:\sigma_j(u)>0\ \forall j\} \]

be the group of totally positive units. Define the logarithmic embedding

\[ \ell:\mathcal O_K^{\times,+}\to H_d, \qquad \ell(u)=(\log\sigma_1(u),\dots,\log\sigma_d(u)), \]

where

\[ H_d=\left\{x\in \mathbb R^d:\sum_{j=1}^d x_j=0\right\}. \]

The image

\[ \Lambda_K^+:=\ell(\mathcal O_K^{\times,+}) \]

is a full lattice in \(H_d\) of rank \(d-1\). With the usual Euclidean measure on \(H_d\), the full unit lattice has covolume \(\sqrt d\,R_K\), and the totally positive unit lattice has index at most \(2^d\) in it. Hence

\[ \operatorname{covol}(\Lambda_K^+) \le 2^d\sqrt d\,R_K \le M^d \]

for some absolute constant \(M\), after enlarging \(M\) if necessary.

Thus we have:

\[ \boxed{\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda_K^+)\le M^d} \tag{1} \]

for every field \(K\) in the tower.

1.3. Schinzel’s lower bound for totally real units

Schinzel’s theorem says that if \(\alpha\) is a totally real algebraic integer with \(\alpha\notin\{-1,0,1\}\), then its Mahler measure satisfies

\[ M(\alpha)\ge \varphi^{\deg(\alpha)/2}, \qquad \varphi=\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2}. \]

We need the following consequence.

Lemma 1.1: Uniform separation of totally positive units

There is an absolute constant \(\eta>0\) such that for every totally real field \(K\) and every nontrivial totally positive unit \(u\in \mathcal O_K^{\times,+}\),

\[ \|\ell(u)\|_\infty\ge \eta. \]

One may take

\[ \eta=\frac12\log\varphi. \]

Proof

Let \(u\in\mathcal O_K^{\times,+}\), \(u\neq 1\). Let \(m=\deg(u)\). Since \(u\) is a unit,

\[ \log M(u)=\sum_{\tau:u\hookrightarrow \mathbb R} \max(0,\log|\tau(u)|). \]

Schinzel gives

\[ \log M(u)\ge \frac m2\log\varphi=m\eta. \]

Therefore at least one conjugate \(\tau(u)\) satisfies

\[ |\log|\tau(u)||\ge \eta. \]

All conjugates of \(u\) occur among the numbers \(\sigma_j(u)\), possibly with repetitions. Since \(u\) is totally positive, \(|\sigma_j(u)|=\sigma_j(u)\). Hence

\[ \max_j |\log\sigma_j(u)|\ge \eta. \]

So

\[ \|\ell(u)\|_\infty\ge \eta. \]

\(\square\)

We shall also use the number

\[ \alpha:=1-e^{-\eta}>0. \tag{2} \]

If \(x>0\) and \(|\log x|\ge \eta\), then

\[ |x-1|\ge \alpha. \tag{3} \]

2. Geometric and lattice-counting lemmas

We need several elementary geometric estimates.

2.1. Algebraic integers are separated in sup norm

Lemma 2.1

Let \(K/\mathbb Q\) be totally real of degree \(d\). If \(x,y\in\mathcal O_K\) and \(x\neq y\), then

\[ \|\Phi(x)-\Phi(y)\|_\infty\ge 1. \]

Proof

Let \(\beta=x-y\in\mathcal O_K\setminus\{0\}\). Then

\[ N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\beta) = \prod_{j=1}^d \sigma_j(\beta) \]

is a nonzero integer, so

\[ |N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\beta)|\ge 1. \]

If \(|\sigma_j(\beta)|<1\) for every \(j\), then the product would have absolute value \(<1\), contradiction. Hence for some \(j\),

\[ |\sigma_j(\beta)|\ge 1. \]

Thus

\[ \|\Phi(x)-\Phi(y)\|_\infty\ge 1. \]

\(\square\)

2.2. A van der Corput lattice-point lower bound

Lemma 2.2

Let \(\Lambda\subset\mathbb R^n\) be a lattice, and let \(K\subset\mathbb R^n\) be centrally symmetric and convex. Then

\[ |\Lambda\cap K| \ge \frac{\operatorname{vol}(K)}{2^n\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda)}. \]

Proof

Average the function

\[ x\mapsto |\Lambda\cap(x+\tfrac12 K)| \]

over the torus \(\mathbb R^n/\Lambda\). Its average equals

\[ \frac{\operatorname{vol}(\tfrac12K)}{\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda)} = \frac{\operatorname{vol}(K)}{2^n\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda)}. \]

So there is some translate \(x+\tfrac12K\) containing at least that many lattice points. If those points are \(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_m\), then fixing \(\lambda_1\), the differences

\[ \lambda_j-\lambda_1 \]

are distinct elements of \(\Lambda\cap K\), since

\[ (x+\tfrac12K)-(x+\tfrac12K)\subset K \]

by convexity and central symmetry. Therefore

\[ |\Lambda\cap K|\ge m \ge \frac{\operatorname{vol}(K)}{2^n\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda)}. \]

\(\square\)

2.3. Volumes of central cube sections

For \(L>0\), define

\[ Q_d(L):=H_d\cap[-L,L]^d, \qquad H_d=\left\{x\in\mathbb R^d:\sum_{j=1}^d x_j=0\right\}. \]

We use Euclidean \((d-1)\)-dimensional volume on \(H_d\).

Lemma 2.3

For every \(d\ge 2\) and every \(L>0\),

\[ L^{d-1} \le \operatorname{vol}_{H_d}(Q_d(L)) \le (4L)^{d-1}. \]

Proof

Let

\[ n=\frac1{\sqrt d}(1,\dots,1). \]

For \(t\in[-L\sqrt d,L\sqrt d]\), let

\[ V(t) = \operatorname{vol}_{d-1} \left( [-L,L]^d\cap\{x\cdot n=t\} \right). \]

By Brunn–Minkowski, \(V(t)^{1/(d-1)}\) is concave in \(t\), and by symmetry \(V(t)=V(-t)\). Hence \(V(t)\) is maximized at \(t=0\). Therefore

\[ \operatorname{vol}_{H_d}(Q_d(L)) = V(0) \ge \frac1{2L\sqrt d}\int_{-L\sqrt d}^{L\sqrt d}V(t)\,dt. \]

The integral is just the volume of the cube \([-L,L]^d\), namely \((2L)^d\). Hence

\[ V(0)\ge \frac{(2L)^d}{2L\sqrt d} = \frac{2^{d-1}}{\sqrt d}L^{d-1}. \]

Since \(2^{d-1}\ge \sqrt d\) for \(d\ge 2\),

\[ V(0)\ge L^{d-1}. \]

This proves the lower bound.

For the upper bound, project \(H_d\) to the first \(d-1\) coordinates. The inverse map is

\[ (x_1,\dots,x_{d-1}) \mapsto (x_1,\dots,x_{d-1},-(x_1+\cdots+x_{d-1})). \]

Its Jacobian is \(\sqrt d\). Since the projection of \(Q_d(L)\) is contained in \([-L,L]^{d-1}\), we get

\[ \operatorname{vol}_{H_d}(Q_d(L)) \le \sqrt d\,(2L)^{d-1}. \]

For \(d\ge 2\),

\[ \sqrt d\,2^{d-1}\le 4^{d-1}, \]

so

\[ \operatorname{vol}_{H_d}(Q_d(L)) \le (4L)^{d-1}. \]

\(\square\)

3. Counting small units and algebraic integers

Let \(K\) be one of the fields in Martinet’s tower, of degree \(d\ge 2\).

For \(L\ge 1\), define

\[ U_K(L) := \{u\in\mathcal O_K^{\times,+}:\|\ell(u)\|_\infty\le L\}. \]

Equivalently,

\[ U_K(L)=\ell^{-1}(\Lambda_K^+\cap Q_d(L)). \]

For \(R\ge 1\), define

\[ E_K(R) := \{c\in\mathcal O_K:\|\Phi(c)\|_\infty\le R\}. \]

3.1. Unit-counting estimates

Lemma 3.1

There are absolute constants \(a,b>0\) such that for every \(K\) in the tower, every \(L\ge 1\), and every \(d\ge \lceil \log_2 L\rceil+1\),

\[ |U_K(L)|\ge (aL)^d, \tag{4} \]

and for every \(d\ge 2\),

\[ |U_K(2L)|\le (bL)^d. \tag{5} \]

Proof

Let \(M\) be the constant from (1), so

\[ \operatorname{covol}(\Lambda_K^+)\le M^d. \]

By Lemma 2.2 and Lemma 2.3,

\[ |U_K(L)| = |\Lambda_K^+\cap Q_d(L)| \ge \frac{\operatorname{vol}_{H_d}(Q_d(L))} {2^{d-1}\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda_K^+)} \ge \frac{L^{d-1}}{2^{d-1}M^d}. \tag{6} \]

Set

\[ a:=\frac1{4M}. \tag{7} \]

Then

\[ \frac{L^{d-1}}{2^{d-1}M^d} = \frac{2^{d+1}}{L}(aL)^d. \]

If \(d\ge \lceil\log_2 L\rceil+1\), then \(2^{d+1}\ge L\), so (6) gives

\[ |U_K(L)|\ge (aL)^d. \]

This proves (4).

For the upper bound, Lemma 1.1 says that distinct points of the lattice \(\Lambda_K^+\) are separated by at least \(\eta\) in sup norm. Therefore the translates

\[ x+Q_d(\eta/2), \qquad x\in \Lambda_K^+\cap Q_d(2L), \]

have disjoint interiors, and all lie inside \(Q_d(2L+\eta/2)\). Hence

\[ |U_K(2L)| \le \frac{\operatorname{vol}_{H_d}(Q_d(2L+\eta/2))} {\operatorname{vol}_{H_d}(Q_d(\eta/2))}. \]

Using Lemma 2.3 again,

\[ |U_K(2L)| \le \frac{(4(2L+\eta/2))^{d-1}}{(\eta/2)^{d-1}} = \left(\frac{8(2L+\eta/2)}{\eta}\right)^{d-1}. \]

Since \(L\ge 1\),

\[ 2L+\eta/2\le (2+\eta/2)L. \]

Thus for a sufficiently large absolute constant \(b\),

\[ |U_K(2L)|\le (bL)^d. \]

\(\square\)

3.2. Algebraic-integer box estimates

Lemma 3.2

For every \(K\) in the tower and every \(R\ge 1\),

\[ |E_K(R)|\ge \left(\frac{R}{\sqrt\rho}\right)^d, \tag{8} \]

and

\[ |E_K(R)|\le (3R)^d. \tag{9} \]

Proof

The lattice \(\Phi(\mathcal O_K)\subset\mathbb R^d\) has covolume

\[ \sqrt{|D_K|}. \]

Applying Lemma 2.2 to the cube \([-R,R]^d\), we get

\[ |E_K(R)| \ge \frac{(2R)^d}{2^d\sqrt{|D_K|}} = \frac{R^d}{\sqrt{|D_K|}}. \]

Since \(|D_K|^{1/d}\le\rho\),

\[ \sqrt{|D_K|}\le \rho^{d/2}, \]

so

\[ |E_K(R)|\ge \left(\frac{R}{\sqrt\rho}\right)^d. \]

This proves (8).

For the upper bound, Lemma 2.1 says that distinct points of \(\Phi(\mathcal O_K)\) are separated by at least \(1\) in sup norm. Hence the unit cubes centered at the points of \(\Phi(E_K(R))\) have disjoint interiors and are contained in

\[ [-R-\tfrac12,R+\tfrac12]^d. \]

Therefore

\[ |E_K(R)| \le (2R+1)^d \le (3R)^d, \]

since \(R\ge 1\). This proves (9).

\(\square\)

4. Choice of absolute parameters

We now fix constants once and for all.

Let \(a,b,\rho,\eta\) be as above, with \(a,b>0\), \(\rho>1\), and

\[ \eta>0. \]

Recall

\[ \alpha=1-e^{-\eta}>0. \]

Choose \(L\ge 1\) so large that

\[ \frac{b}{a^2L}<1. \tag{10} \]

Define

\[ q:=\frac{b}{a^2L}. \tag{11} \]

Thus

\[ 0<q<1. \]

Next define

\[ \kappa:=\frac{2(1+e^{2L})}{\alpha}. \tag{12} \]

Choose \(R\ge 1\) so large that, with

\[ m:=\frac{aLR}{\sqrt\rho}, \tag{13} \]

we have

\[ m>1, \tag{14} \]

and

\[ 5e^L(\kappa+2)R<m^2. \tag{15} \]

This is possible because the left-hand side of (15) grows linearly in \(R\), while \(m^2\) grows quadratically in \(R\).

Now define

\[ \Sigma:=5e^L(\kappa+2)R. \tag{16} \]

By (15),

\[ \Sigma<m^2. \tag{17} \]

Finally define

\[ P:=3bLR. \tag{18} \]

Since \(P>1\) and \(q<1\), the number

\[ \delta_\times:=\frac{\log(1/q)}{\log P} = \frac{\log(a^2L/b)}{\log(3bLR)} \tag{19} \]

is positive.

Also, by (14) and (17),

\[ \delta_+:= 2-\frac{\log \Sigma}{\log m} \tag{20} \]

is positive.

Set

\[ \delta:=\min(\delta_+,\delta_\times)>0. \tag{21} \]

All of these constants are absolute.

5. Construction inside one field

Let \(K\) be one of the fields in the tower, of degree \(d\), with \(d\) sufficiently large so that Lemma 3.1 applies to our fixed \(L\). In particular, we assume

\[ d\ge \lceil\log_2 L\rceil+1. \tag{22} \]

Define

\[ U:=U_K(L) = \{u\in\mathcal O_K^{\times,+}:\|\ell(u)\|_\infty\le L\}, \]

and

\[ E:=E_K(R) = \{c\in\mathcal O_K:\|\Phi(c)\|_\infty\le R\}. \]

Let

\[ T:=\lceil \kappa R\rceil\in\mathbb Z. \tag{23} \]

Since \(R\ge 1\),

\[ T\le \kappa R+1\le (\kappa+1)R. \tag{24} \]

Define

\[ B:=T+E = \{T+c:c\in E\}. \]

Finally define

\[ A_K:=UB = \{u(T+c):u\in U,\ c\in E\} \subset\mathcal O_K. \]

We will later embed \(A_K\) into \(\mathbb R\) using one real embedding of \(K\).

6. The translates \(uB\) are disjoint

We first prove that

\[ |A_K|=|U||E|. \]

This is the key injectivity statement.

Lemma 6.1

If \(u,v\in U\) and

\[ uB\cap vB\neq\varnothing, \]

then \(u=v\). Consequently,

\[ |A_K|=|U||E|. \]

Proof

Suppose

\[ u(T+c)=v(T+c') \]

with \(u,v\in U\) and \(c,c'\in E\).

If \(u=v\), then \(c=c'\), so there is nothing to prove. Suppose \(u\neq v\). Put

\[ w:=u/v. \]

Then \(w\in\mathcal O_K^{\times,+}\), and \(w\neq 1\). By Lemma 1.1, there is an embedding \(\sigma_j\) such that

\[ |\log\sigma_j(w)|\ge \eta. \]

Since \(w\) is totally positive, \(\sigma_j(w)>0\), and by (3),

\[ |\sigma_j(w)-1|\ge \alpha. \tag{25} \]

Also, because \(u,v\in U=U_K(L)\),

\[ |\log\sigma_j(u)|\le L, \qquad |\log\sigma_j(v)|\le L. \]

Hence

\[ \sigma_j(w)=\frac{\sigma_j(u)}{\sigma_j(v)} \le e^{2L}. \tag{26} \]

Apply \(\sigma_j\) to

\[ u(T+c)=v(T+c') \]

and divide by \(\sigma_j(v)>0\). We obtain

\[ \sigma_j(w)(T+\sigma_j(c)) = T+\sigma_j(c'). \]

Rearranging,

\[ (\sigma_j(w)-1)T = \sigma_j(c')-\sigma_j(w)\sigma_j(c). \]

Taking absolute values and using \(c,c'\in E\), we get

\[ |\sigma_j(w)-1|T \le |\sigma_j(c')|+\sigma_j(w)|\sigma_j(c)| \le R+e^{2L}R = (1+e^{2L})R. \tag{27} \]

On the other hand, by (25), (12), and (23),

\[ |\sigma_j(w)-1|T \ge \alpha T \ge \alpha\kappa R = 2(1+e^{2L})R. \tag{28} \]

The estimates (27) and (28) contradict each other. Therefore \(u=v\), and then necessarily \(c=c'\).

Thus the sets \(uB\), \(u\in U\), are pairwise disjoint. Since \(|B|=|E|\), we get

\[ |A_K|=|U||E|. \]

\(\square\)

Let

\[ N:=|A_K|. \]

Using Lemmas 3.1 and 3.2, and the definitions of \(m\) and \(P\), we have

\[ N = |U||E| \ge (aL)^d\left(\frac{R}{\sqrt\rho}\right)^d = m^d, \tag{29} \]

and also

\[ N = |U||E| \le |U_K(2L)|\,|E| \le (bL)^d(3R)^d = P^d. \tag{30} \]

Thus

\[ m^d\le N\le P^d. \tag{31} \]

In particular, since \(m>1\), \(N\to\infty\) as \(d\to\infty\).

7. Sumset estimate

We prove

\[ |A_K+A_K|\le N^{2-\delta_+}. \]

Every element \(a\in A_K\) has the form

\[ a=u(T+c), \qquad u\in U,\ c\in E. \]

For every embedding \(\sigma_j\),

\[ |\sigma_j(u)|\le e^L \]

and

\[ |\sigma_j(T+c)| \le T+|\sigma_j(c)| \le T+R. \]

By (24),

\[ T+R \le (\kappa+2)R. \]

Hence

\[ |\sigma_j(a)| \le e^L(\kappa+2)R \]

for every \(j\). Therefore, for \(a,a'\in A_K\),

\[ |\sigma_j(a+a')| \le 2e^L(\kappa+2)R. \]

Thus \(A_K+A_K\) is contained in the set of algebraic integers \(x\in\mathcal O_K\) satisfying

\[ |\sigma_j(x)| \le 2e^L(\kappa+2)R \qquad \forall j. \]

By Lemma 2.1, the number of such algebraic integers is at most

\[ \left(4e^L(\kappa+2)R+1\right)^d. \]

Since \(e^L(\kappa+2)R\ge 1\), this is at most

\[ \left(5e^L(\kappa+2)R\right)^d = \Sigma^d. \]

Therefore

\[ |A_K+A_K| \le \Sigma^d. \tag{32} \]

By the definition of \(\delta_+\),

\[ \Sigma=m^{2-\delta_+}. \]

Hence

\[ |A_K+A_K| \le m^{(2-\delta_+)d}. \]

Using \(N\ge m^d\), we obtain

\[ |A_K+A_K| \le N^{2-\delta_+}. \tag{33} \]

8. Product-set estimate

We prove

\[ |A_KA_K|\le N^{2-\delta_\times}. \]

Since

\[ A_K=UB, \]

we have

\[ A_KA_K \subset (UU)(BB). \]

Because \(U=U_K(L)\), we have

\[ UU\subset U_K(2L). \]

Therefore

\[ |A_KA_K| \le |U_K(2L)|\,|BB|. \]

Trivially,

\[ |BB|\le |B|^2=|E|^2. \]

Using Lemma 3.1,

\[ |U_K(2L)|\le (bL)^d. \]

So

\[ |A_KA_K| \le (bL)^d|E|^2. \tag{34} \]

On the other hand,

\[ N=|U||E| \]

and by Lemma 3.1,

\[ |U|\ge (aL)^d. \]

Thus

\[ N^2 = |U|^2|E|^2 \ge (aL)^{2d}|E|^2. \]

Combining this with (34), we get

\[ |A_KA_K| \le N^2 \frac{(bL)^d}{(aL)^{2d}} = N^2 \left(\frac{b}{a^2L}\right)^d. \]

By definition, the last factor is \(q^d\). Thus

\[ |A_KA_K| \le N^2q^d. \tag{35} \]

Recall that

\[ q=P^{-\delta_\times}. \]

Hence

\[ q^d=P^{-\delta_\times d}. \]

Since \(N\le P^d\), we have

\[ P^{-\delta_\times d}\le N^{-\delta_\times}. \]

Therefore (35) gives

\[ |A_KA_K| \le N^{2-\delta_\times}. \tag{36} \]

9. Passing to subsets of \(\mathbb R\)

We now use the infinite tower.

Let \(K_i\) be the Martinet sequence, with degrees \(d_i\to\infty\). Discard finitely many fields so that

\[ d_i\ge \lceil\log_2 L\rceil+1 \]

for every remaining \(i\). Renumber the remaining infinite sequence as \(K_1,K_2,\dots\).

For each \(K_i\), construct \(A_{K_i}\subset\mathcal O_{K_i}\) as above. Choose one real embedding

\[ \tau_i:K_i\hookrightarrow\mathbb R. \]

Define

\[ A_i:=\tau_i(A_{K_i})\subset\mathbb R. \]

Because \(\tau_i\) is an injective ring homomorphism,

\[ |A_i|=|A_{K_i}|, \]

and also

\[ |A_i+A_i| = |A_{K_i}+A_{K_i}|, \]
\[ |A_iA_i| = |A_{K_i}A_{K_i}|. \]

\[ |A_i+A_i|\le |A_i|^{2-\delta}, \]

\[ |A_iA_i|\le |A_i|^{2-\delta}. \]

\[ \max(|A_i+A_i|,\ |A_iA_i|) \le |A_i|^{2-\delta} \]

for every \(i\).

This proves the theorem. \(\square\)

Proof

We use three standard facts from algebraic number theory.

1. Standard inputs

Fact 1: Martinet’s totally real bounded-root-discriminant tower

There exists an absolute constant \(\rho>1\) and infinitely many totally real number fields \(K_i/\mathbb Q\) such that, writing

\[ d_i=[K_i:\mathbb Q], \]

we have

\[ d_i\to\infty \]

and

\[ |D_{K_i}|^{1/d_i}\le \rho \]

for all \(i\).

This is Martinet’s construction using Golod–Shafarevich class-field towers.

Fact 2: Exponential regulator bound in such a tower

Let \(K\) be totally real of degree \(d\). Let \(R_K\) be its regulator, \(h_K\) its class number, and

\[ \kappa_K=\operatorname*{Res}_{s=1}\zeta_K(s). \]

The analytic class number formula gives

\[ \kappa_K=\frac{2^{d-1}h_KR_K}{\sqrt{|D_K|}}. \]

A standard Landau–Louboutin residue bound gives an absolute constant \(C_L\) such that

\[ \kappa_K\le C_L^d\left(1+\frac{\log |D_K|}{d}\right)^d. \]

Therefore, if \(|D_K|^{1/d}\le \rho\), then

\[ \kappa_K\le C_\rho^d \]

for some absolute constant \(C_\rho\). Hence

\[ R_K \le h_KR_K = \frac{\kappa_K\sqrt{|D_K|}}{2^{d-1}} \le C_{\mathrm{reg}}^d \]

for some absolute constant \(C_{\mathrm{reg}}\).

Now let

\[ \mathcal O_K^{\times,+} = \{u\in\mathcal O_K^\times:\sigma(u)>0\text{ for every real embedding }\sigma:K\hookrightarrow\mathbb R\} \]

be the group of totally positive units. Define

\[ H_d=\left\{x=(x_1,\dots,x_d)\in\mathbb R^d:\sum_{j=1}^d x_j=0\right\} \]

and the logarithmic embedding

\[ \ell:\mathcal O_K^{\times,+}\to H_d, \qquad \ell(u)=(\log\sigma_1(u),\dots,\log\sigma_d(u)). \]

Let

\[ \Lambda_K^+:=\ell(\mathcal O_K^{\times,+}). \]

This is a full lattice in \(H_d\). The usual logarithmic unit lattice has Euclidean covolume \(\sqrt d\,R_K\), and the totally positive unit lattice has index at most \(2^d\) in it. Thus, after enlarging constants if necessary, there is an absolute constant \(M_0\) such that

\[ \operatorname{covol}_{H_d}(\Lambda_K^+)\le M_0^d \tag{1} \]

for every field \(K\) in Martinet’s tower.

Fact 3: Schinzel’s lower bound for totally real units

Schinzel proved that if \(\alpha\) is a totally real algebraic integer with \(\alpha\notin\{-1,0,1\}\), then its Mahler measure satisfies

\[ M(\alpha)\ge \varphi^{\deg(\alpha)/2}, \qquad \varphi=\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2}. \]

We need the following consequence.

There is an absolute constant \(\eta>0\) such that for every totally real field \(K\) and every nontrivial totally positive unit \(u\in\mathcal O_K^{\times,+}\),

\[ \|\ell(u)\|_\infty\ge \eta. \tag{2} \]

Indeed, one may take \(\eta=\frac12\log\varphi\). If all conjugates of \(u\) had \(|\log|\tau(u)||<\eta\), then \(\log M(u)<\deg(u)\eta\), contradicting Schinzel.

Set

\[ \alpha_0:=1-e^{-\eta}>0. \tag{3} \]

Then whenever \(x>0\) and \(|\log x|\ge \eta\), we have

\[ |x-1|\ge \alpha_0. \tag{4} \]

2. Geometric lemmas

Let \(K/\mathbb Q\) be totally real of degree \(d\), with real embeddings

\[ \sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_d. \]

Define the Minkowski embedding

\[ \Phi:K\hookrightarrow \mathbb R^d, \qquad \Phi(x)=(\sigma_1(x),\dots,\sigma_d(x)). \]

Lemma 2.1: Separation of algebraic integers

If \(x,y\in\mathcal O_K\) and \(x\neq y\), then

\[ \|\Phi(x)-\Phi(y)\|_\infty\ge 1. \]

Proof

Let \(\beta=x-y\neq0\). Since \(\beta\in\mathcal O_K\),

\[ N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\beta)=\prod_{j=1}^d \sigma_j(\beta) \]

is a nonzero integer, so

\[ |N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\beta)|\ge1. \]

Thus at least one conjugate satisfies \(|\sigma_j(\beta)|\ge1\). Hence

\[ \|\Phi(x)-\Phi(y)\|_\infty\ge1. \]

\(\square\)

Lemma 2.2: van der Corput lattice-point lower bound

Let \(\Lambda\subset\mathbb R^n\) be a lattice and let \(B\subset\mathbb R^n\) be centrally symmetric and convex. Then

\[ |\Lambda\cap B| \ge \frac{\operatorname{vol}(B)}{2^n\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda)}. \]

Proof

Average \(|\Lambda\cap(x+\frac12 B)|\) over \(x\in\mathbb R^n/\Lambda\). The average equals

\[ \frac{\operatorname{vol}(\frac12B)}{\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda)} = \frac{\operatorname{vol}(B)}{2^n\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda)}. \]

Thus some translate \(x+\frac12B\) contains at least this many lattice points. If those points are \(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_m\), then the differences

\[ \lambda_j-\lambda_1 \]

are distinct points of \(\Lambda\cap B\), because

\[ \frac12B-\frac12B\subset B \]

by convexity and symmetry. Therefore \(|\Lambda\cap B|\ge m\), proving the claim.

\(\square\)

Lemma 2.3: Volume of central cube sections

Let

\[ Q_d(L):=H_d\cap[-L,L]^d. \]

For every \(d\ge2\) and \(L>0\),

\[ L^{d-1} \le \operatorname{vol}_{H_d}(Q_d(L)) \le (4L)^{d-1}. \tag{5} \]

Proof

Let

\[ n=\frac1{\sqrt d}(1,\dots,1). \]

For \(t\in[-L\sqrt d,L\sqrt d]\), define

\[ V(t) = \operatorname{vol}_{d-1} \left([-L,L]^d\cap\{x\cdot n=t\}\right). \]

By Brunn–Minkowski, \(V(t)^{1/(d-1)}\) is concave in \(t\). Since the cube is centrally symmetric, \(V(t)=V(-t)\), so \(V(t)\) is maximized at \(t=0\). Therefore

\[ (2L)^d = \int_{-L\sqrt d}^{L\sqrt d}V(t)\,dt \le 2L\sqrt d\,V(0). \]

Thus

\[ V(0)\ge \frac{(2L)^d}{2L\sqrt d} = \frac{2^{d-1}}{\sqrt d}L^{d-1} \ge L^{d-1} \]

for \(d\ge2\). This gives the lower bound.

For the upper bound, project \(H_d\) to the first \(d-1\) coordinates. The inverse map is

\[ (x_1,\dots,x_{d-1}) \mapsto (x_1,\dots,x_{d-1},-(x_1+\cdots+x_{d-1})). \]

Its Jacobian is \(\sqrt d\). The projection of \(Q_d(L)\) lies inside \([-L,L]^{d-1}\). Hence

\[ \operatorname{vol}_{H_d}(Q_d(L)) \le \sqrt d\,(2L)^{d-1} \le (4L)^{d-1}. \]

\(\square\)

3. Counting small units and algebraic integers

For \(L\ge1\), define

\[ U_K(L) = \{u\in\mathcal O_K^{\times,+}:\|\ell(u)\|_\infty\le L\}. \]

For \(R\ge1\), define

\[ C_K(R) = \{c\in\mathcal O_K:\|\Phi(c)\|_\infty\le R\}. \]

Lemma 3.1: Counting small totally positive units

There are absolute constants \(a,b>0\) such that, for every \(K\) in Martinet’s tower and every \(L\ge1\), once \(d=[K:\mathbb Q]\) is sufficiently large depending only on \(L\), we have

\[ |U_K(L)|\ge (aL)^d, \tag{6} \]

and for all \(d\ge2\),

\[ |U_K(2L)|\le (bL)^d. \tag{7} \]

Proof

Recall that

\[ U_K(L)=\ell^{-1}(\Lambda_K^+\cap Q_d(L)). \]

Using Lemma 2.2, Lemma 2.3, and the covolume bound (1), we get

\[ |U_K(L)| = |\Lambda_K^+\cap Q_d(L)| \ge \frac{L^{d-1}}{2^{d-1}M_0^d}. \]

Set

\[ a:=\frac1{4M_0}. \]

Then

\[ \frac{L^{d-1}}{2^{d-1}M_0^d} = \frac{2^{d+1}}{L}(aL)^d. \]

For fixed \(L\), if \(d\) is large enough that \(2^{d+1}\ge L\), then

\[ |U_K(L)|\ge (aL)^d. \]

This proves (6).

For the upper bound, Schinzel separation (2) implies that distinct points of \(\Lambda_K^+\) are separated by at least \(\eta\) in sup norm. Therefore the translates

\[ x+Q_d(\eta/2), \qquad x\in \Lambda_K^+\cap Q_d(2L), \]

have disjoint interiors and are all contained in \(Q_d(2L+\eta/2)\). Hence

\[ |U_K(2L)| \le \frac{\operatorname{vol}_{H_d}(Q_d(2L+\eta/2))} {\operatorname{vol}_{H_d}(Q_d(\eta/2))}. \]

Using Lemma 2.3,

\[ |U_K(2L)| \le \left(\frac{8(2L+\eta/2)}{\eta}\right)^{d-1}. \]

Since \(L\ge1\), this is at most \((bL)^d\) for a sufficiently large absolute constant \(b\). This proves (7).

\(\square\)

Lemma 3.2: Counting algebraic integers in boxes

For every \(K\) in Martinet’s tower and every \(R\ge1\),

\[ |C_K(R)|\ge \left(\frac{R}{\sqrt\rho}\right)^d, \tag{8} \]

and

\[ |C_K(R)|\le (3R)^d. \tag{9} \]

Proof

The lattice \(\Phi(\mathcal O_K)\subset\mathbb R^d\) has covolume \(\sqrt{|D_K|}\). Applying Lemma 2.2 to the cube \([-R,R]^d\), we get

\[ |C_K(R)| \ge \frac{(2R)^d}{2^d\sqrt{|D_K|}} = \frac{R^d}{\sqrt{|D_K|}}. \]

Since \(|D_K|^{1/d}\le\rho\),

\[ \sqrt{|D_K|}\le \rho^{d/2}, \]

so

\[ |C_K(R)|\ge \left(\frac{R}{\sqrt\rho}\right)^d. \]

For the upper bound, Lemma 2.1 says that distinct points of \(\Phi(\mathcal O_K)\) are sup-norm separated by at least \(1\). Therefore the unit cubes centered at the points of \(\Phi(C_K(R))\) have disjoint interiors and lie inside

\[ [-R-\tfrac12,R+\tfrac12]^d. \]

Hence

\[ |C_K(R)|\le (2R+1)^d\le (3R)^d. \]

\(\square\)

4. Choice of absolute parameters

We now choose constants once and for all.

Let \(a,b,\rho,\eta\) be as above. Choose \(L\ge1\) so large that

\[ q:=\frac{b}{a^2L}<1. \tag{10} \]

Set

\[ \alpha_0=1-e^{-\eta}. \]

Define

\[ \kappa:=\frac{2(1+e^{2L})}{\alpha_0}. \tag{11} \]

Now choose \(R\ge1\) so large that

\[ m:=\frac{aLR}{\sqrt\rho}>1 \tag{12} \]

and

\[ \Sigma:=5e^L(\kappa+2)R<m^2. \tag{13} \]

This is possible because \(m^2\) grows quadratically in \(R\), while \(\Sigma\) grows only linearly in \(R\).

Finally set

\[ P:=3bLR. \tag{14} \]

Since \(q<1\) and \(P>1\), define

\[ \delta_\times:=\frac{\log(1/q)}{\log P}>0. \tag{15} \]

Also, by \(\Sigma<m^2\) and \(m>1\), define

\[ \delta_+:=2-\frac{\log\Sigma}{\log m}>0. \tag{16} \]

Let

\[ \delta:=\min(\delta_+,\delta_\times,1)>0. \tag{17} \]

All these constants are absolute.

5. Construction in one field

Let \(K\) be one of the fields in Martinet’s tower, with degree \(d\) sufficiently large that Lemma 3.1 gives

\[ |U_K(L)|\ge (aL)^d. \]

Define

\[ U:=U_K(L), \qquad C:=C_K(R). \]

Let

\[ T:=\lceil \kappa R\rceil\in\mathbb Z. \]

Then

\[ T\le (\kappa+1)R, \]

because \(R\ge1\). Define

\[ B:=T+C=\{T+c:c\in C\}. \]

Finally set

\[ A_K:=UB = \{u(T+c):u\in U,\ c\in C\} \subset \mathcal O_K. \]

We now estimate \(|A_K|\), \(|A_K+A_K|\), and \(|A_KA_K|\).

6. Size of \(A_K\)

We first prove that the representation \(u(T+c)\) is unique.

Lemma 6.1: Disjointness of the translates \(uB\)

If \(u,v\in U\) and

\[ uB\cap vB\neq\varnothing, \]

then \(u=v\). Consequently,

\[ |A_K|=|U||C|. \tag{18} \]

Proof

Suppose

\[ u(T+c)=v(T+c') \]

for some \(u,v\in U\) and \(c,c'\in C\).

If \(u=v\), then since \(u\neq0\), we get \(c=c'\). So suppose \(u\neq v\). Put

\[ w:=u/v. \]

Then \(w\in\mathcal O_K^{\times,+}\) and \(w\neq1\). By Schinzel separation, there exists an embedding \(\sigma_j\) such that

\[ |\log\sigma_j(w)|\ge \eta. \]

Since \(w\) is totally positive, \(\sigma_j(w)>0\), and therefore by (4),

\[ |\sigma_j(w)-1|\ge \alpha_0. \tag{19} \]

Also, since \(u,v\in U_K(L)\),

\[ \sigma_j(w)=\frac{\sigma_j(u)}{\sigma_j(v)}\le e^{2L}. \tag{20} \]

Applying \(\sigma_j\) to

\[ u(T+c)=v(T+c') \]

and dividing by \(\sigma_j(v)>0\), we get

\[ \sigma_j(w)(T+\sigma_j(c))=T+\sigma_j(c'). \]

Thus

\[ (\sigma_j(w)-1)T = \sigma_j(c')-\sigma_j(w)\sigma_j(c). \]

Taking absolute values and using \(c,c'\in C\), we obtain

\[ |\sigma_j(w)-1|T \le R+e^{2L}R = (1+e^{2L})R. \tag{21} \]

But by (19), \(T\ge\kappa R\), and the definition of \(\kappa\),

\[ |\sigma_j(w)-1|T \ge \alpha_0\kappa R = 2(1+e^{2L})R, \tag{22} \]

contradicting (21). Therefore \(u=v\), and then \(c=c'\). Hence the sets \(uB\), \(u\in U\), are pairwise disjoint, so

\[ |A_K|=|U||C|. \]

\(\square\)

Let

\[ N:=|A_K|. \]

Using Lemmas 3.1 and 3.2,

\[ N = |U||C| \ge (aL)^d\left(\frac{R}{\sqrt\rho}\right)^d = m^d. \tag{23} \]

Also, since \(U_K(L)\subset U_K(2L)\),

\[ N \le |U_K(2L)|\,|C| \le (bL)^d(3R)^d = P^d. \tag{24} \]

Thus

\[ m^d\le N\le P^d. \tag{25} \]

7. Sumset estimate

Every element \(a\in A_K\) has the form

\[ a=u(T+c) \]

with \(u\in U\), \(c\in C\). For every embedding \(\sigma_j\),

\[ |\sigma_j(u)|\le e^L \]

and

\[ |\sigma_j(T+c)| \le T+|\sigma_j(c)| \le (\kappa+1)R+R = (\kappa+2)R. \]

Therefore

\[ |\sigma_j(a)| \le e^L(\kappa+2)R. \]

Hence, for \(a,a'\in A_K\),

\[ |\sigma_j(a+a')| \le 2e^L(\kappa+2)R. \]

By Lemma 2.1, the number of algebraic integers \(x\in\mathcal O_K\) with

\[ |\sigma_j(x)|\le 2e^L(\kappa+2)R \]

for all \(j\) is at most

\[ \left(4e^L(\kappa+2)R+1\right)^d \le \left(5e^L(\kappa+2)R\right)^d = \Sigma^d. \]

Thus

\[ |A_K+A_K|\le \Sigma^d. \tag{26} \]

By definition of \(\delta_+\),

\[ \Sigma=m^{2-\delta_+}. \]

Using \(N\ge m^d\), we get

\[ |A_K+A_K| \le m^{(2-\delta_+)d} \le N^{2-\delta_+}. \tag{27} \]

8. Product-set estimate

Since

\[ A_K=UB, \]

we have

\[ A_KA_K\subset (UU)(BB). \]

Because \(U=U_K(L)\),

\[ UU\subset U_K(2L). \]

Therefore

\[ |A_KA_K| \le |U_K(2L)|\,|BB|. \]

Trivially,

\[ |BB|\le |B|^2=|C|^2. \]

Using Lemma 3.1,

\[ |A_KA_K| \le (bL)^d |C|^2. \tag{28} \]

On the other hand,

\[ N=|U||C| \]

and

\[ |U|\ge (aL)^d. \]

Therefore

\[ N^2 = |U|^2|C|^2 \ge (aL)^{2d}|C|^2. \]

Combining with (28),

\[ |A_KA_K| \le N^2 \frac{(bL)^d}{(aL)^{2d}} = N^2 \left(\frac{b}{a^2L}\right)^d = N^2q^d. \tag{29} \]

By the definition of \(\delta_\times\),

\[ q=P^{-\delta_\times}. \]

Thus

\[ q^d=P^{-\delta_\times d}. \]

Since \(N\le P^d\), we have

\[ P^{-\delta_\times d}\le N^{-\delta_\times}. \]

Therefore

\[ |A_KA_K| \le N^{2-\delta_\times}. \tag{30} \]

\[ \tau_i:K_i\hookrightarrow\mathbb R \]

and define

\[ A_i:=\tau_i(A_{K_i})\subset\mathbb R. \]

Because \(\tau_i\) is an injective ring homomorphism,

\[ |A_i|=|A_{K_i}|, \]

and also

\[ |A_i+A_i|=|A_{K_i}+A_{K_i}|, \]
\[ |A_iA_i|=|A_{K_i}A_{K_i}|. \]

\[ N_i:=|A_i|. \]

From (23),

\[ N_i\ge m^{d_i}. \]

Since \(m>1\) and \(d_i\to\infty\), we have

\[ |A_i|=N_i\to\infty. \]

By (27) and (30),

\[ |A_i+A_i| \le N_i^{2-\delta_+}, \]

and

\[ |A_iA_i| \le N_i^{2-\delta_\times}. \]

Since

\[ \delta=\min(\delta_+,\delta_\times,1)>0, \]

we conclude that

\[ |A_i+A_i|\le |A_i|^{2-\delta} \]

and

\[ |A_iA_i|\le |A_i|^{2-\delta}. \]

Therefore

\[ \max(|A_i+A_i|,\ |A_iA_i|) \le |A_i|^{2-\delta} \]

for every \(i\), with \(|A_i|\to\infty\).

This proves the theorem. \(\square\)
