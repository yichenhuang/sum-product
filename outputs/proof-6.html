0. Audit of the previous proof

The strategy is sound, but the previous version had a few points that needed tightening:

  1. The existence of totally real fields with bounded root discriminant and exponentially bounded regulator was left as an informal arithmetic input. Below it is stated precisely and derived from Martinet’s totally real class-field tower theorem plus Landau’s residue bound and the analytic class number formula.
  2. The logarithmic unit lattice does not have covolume exactly \(R_K\) in the Euclidean metric on the hyperplane; it has covolume \(\sqrt d\,R_K\). This factor is accounted for.
  3. The lower bound for the number of small units needs a uniform lower bound for the volume of a central cube section. This is proved explicitly.
  4. The product-set argument must carefully count principal ideals and then generators per ideal. This is done below with all constants fixed.

We now give the complete proof.

1. Arithmetic input

We use two standard theorems.

Theorem A: Martinet

There exists an absolute constant \(D>1\) and an infinite sequence of totally real number fields \(K_i\) with

\[ d_i=[K_i:\mathbb Q]\to\infty \]

such that

\[ |\Delta_{K_i}|^{1/d_i}\le D \]

for every \(i\).

This follows from Martinet’s construction of an infinite totally real class-field tower.

Theorem B: Landau residue bound

There is an absolute constant \(C_L>0\) such that for every number field \(K\) of degree \(d\ge2\),

\[ \operatorname*{Res}_{s=1}\zeta_K(s) \le \left(C_L\left(1+\frac{\log|\Delta_K|}{d}\right)\right)^d. \]

For fields with bounded root discriminant this gives an exponential-in-\(d\) bound for the residue.

Lemma 1.1: Exponential regulator bound

There are absolute constants \(D,E>1\) and infinitely many totally real fields \(K_i\), with \(d_i=[K_i:\mathbb Q]\to\infty\), such that

\[ |\Delta_{K_i}|^{1/d_i}\le D \]

and

\[ R_{K_i}\le E^{d_i}. \]

Proof

Take \(K_i\) from Theorem A, so

\[ |\Delta_{K_i}|^{1/d_i}\le D. \]

Let

\[ \kappa_{K_i}:=\operatorname*{Res}_{s=1}\zeta_{K_i}(s). \]

By Theorem B,

\[ \kappa_{K_i} \le \left(C_L(1+\log D)\right)^{d_i} =:C_D^{d_i}. \]

For a totally real field \(K\) of degree \(d\), the analytic class number formula says

\[ \kappa_K = \frac{2^{d-1}h_KR_K}{\sqrt{|\Delta_K|}}. \]

Therefore

\[ R_K \le h_KR_K = 2^{1-d}\sqrt{|\Delta_K|}\,\kappa_K. \]

For \(K=K_i\),

\[ \sqrt{|\Delta_{K_i}|}\le D^{d_i/2}, \]

so

\[ R_{K_i} \le 2^{1-d_i}D^{d_i/2}C_D^{d_i} \le E^{d_i} \]

for some fixed \(E>1\), for example

\[ E:=D^{1/2}C_D+1. \]

Thus the sequence satisfies both desired bounds. \(\square\)

2. Geometry-of-numbers estimates

Let \(K/\mathbb Q\) be totally real of degree \(d\). Let

\[ \sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_d:K\hookrightarrow\mathbb R \]

be its real embeddings.

For \(Z>0\), define

\[ B_K(Z) := \left\{ \alpha\in\mathcal O_K: |\sigma_j(\alpha)|\le Z \text{ for all }j \right\}. \]

For \(t>0\), define

\[ U_K(t) := \left\{ u\in\mathcal O_K^\times: |\log|\sigma_j(u)||\le t \text{ for all }j \right\}. \]

Lemma 2.1: Algebraic-integer box bounds

For every \(Z>0\),

\[ |B_K(Z)|\le (2Z+1)^d. \]

Also,

\[ |B_K(Z)| \ge \frac{Z^d}{\sqrt{|\Delta_K|}}-1. \]

Proof

Embed \(\mathcal O_K\) into \(\mathbb R^d\) by

\[ \alpha\mapsto (\sigma_1(\alpha),\dots,\sigma_d(\alpha)). \]

This is a lattice of covolume \(\sqrt{|\Delta_K|}\).

For the upper bound, if \(\alpha\ne\beta\), then \(\gamma=\alpha-\beta\ne0\) is an algebraic integer, so

\[ |N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\gamma)| = \prod_{j=1}^d |\sigma_j(\gamma)| \]

is a positive integer. Hence at least one \(|\sigma_j(\gamma)|\ge1\). Thus the embedded lattice is \(1\)-separated in the sup norm.

The points of \(B_K(Z)\) lie in \([-Z,Z]^d\). Open sup-norm cubes of side length \(1\) centered at these points are disjoint and lie inside \([-Z-\frac12,Z+\frac12]^d\). Therefore

\[ |B_K(Z)|\le (2Z+1)^d. \]

For the lower bound, apply Blichfeldt’s principle to

\[ [-Z/2,Z/2]^d. \]

Some translate contains at least

\[ \frac{Z^d}{\sqrt{|\Delta_K|}}-1 \]

lattice points. Taking differences from one fixed point in that translate gives distinct lattice points in \([-Z,Z]^d\). Thus

\[ |B_K(Z)| \ge \frac{Z^d}{\sqrt{|\Delta_K|}}-1. \]

\(\square\)

Lemma 2.2: Many small units

For every totally real field \(K\) of degree \(d\ge2\) and every \(t>0\),

\[ |U_K(t)| \ge \frac{(t/2)^{d-1}}{R_K}-1. \]

Proof

Consider the logarithmic embedding

\[ \lambda:\mathcal O_K^\times\to H_d, \qquad u\mapsto (\log|\sigma_1(u)|,\dots,\log|\sigma_d(u)|), \]

where

\[ H_d= \left\{ x\in\mathbb R^d: x_1+\cdots+x_d=0 \right\}. \]

By Dirichlet’s unit theorem, the image \(\Lambda_K:=\lambda(\mathcal O_K^\times)\) is a lattice in \(H_d\) of rank \(d-1\). With the usual regulator normalization, its Euclidean covolume in \(H_d\) is

\[ \sqrt d\,R_K. \]

For \(r>0\), set

\[ Q_r:=H_d\cap[-r,r]^d. \]

We need a lower bound for the \((d-1)\)-dimensional volume of \(Q_r\). Put \(m=d-1\), and parametrize \(H_d\) by

\[ (y_1,\dots,y_m) \mapsto (y_1,\dots,y_m,-y_1-\cdots-y_m). \]

The Jacobian is \(\sqrt d\). Hence

\[ \operatorname{vol}_{H_d}(Q_r) = \sqrt d\, \operatorname{meas} \left\{ y\in[-r,r]^m: |y_1+\cdots+y_m|\le r \right\}. \]

Let \(Y_1,\dots,Y_m\) be independent uniform random variables on \([-r,r]\). The density of \(Y_1+\cdots+Y_m\) is even and unimodal. Therefore among intervals of length \(2r\), the interval \([-r,r]\) has maximal probability. The support is \([-mr,mr]\), which can be covered by \(m\) intervals of length \(2r\). Hence

\[ \mathbb P(|Y_1+\cdots+Y_m|\le r)\ge \frac1m. \]

Thus

\[ \operatorname{vol}_{H_d}(Q_r) \ge \sqrt d\,\frac{(2r)^m}{m} = \sqrt d\,\frac{(2r)^{d-1}}{d-1}. \]

Apply this with \(r=t/2\). Then

\[ \operatorname{vol}_{H_d}(Q_{t/2}) \ge \sqrt d\,\frac{t^{d-1}}{d-1}. \]

By Blichfeldt’s principle in the lattice \(\Lambda_K\subset H_d\), some translate of \(Q_{t/2}\) contains at least

\[ \frac{\operatorname{vol}_{H_d}(Q_{t/2})}{\sqrt d\,R_K}-1 \]

lattice points. Taking differences from one fixed point gives distinct lattice points in

\[ Q_{t/2}-Q_{t/2}\subset Q_t. \]

Therefore

\[ |\Lambda_K\cap Q_t| \ge \frac{t^{d-1}}{(d-1)R_K}-1. \]

Since \(d-1\le 2^{d-1}\),

\[ |\Lambda_K\cap Q_t| \ge \frac{(t/2)^{d-1}}{R_K}-1. \]

Finally, \(U_K(t)\) is the preimage of \(\Lambda_K\cap Q_t\) under \(\lambda\). The kernel consists of \(\{\pm1\}\), so in particular

\[ |U_K(t)|\ge |\Lambda_K\cap Q_t|. \]

Hence

\[ |U_K(t)| \ge \frac{(t/2)^{d-1}}{R_K}-1. \]

\(\square\)

Lemma 2.3: Dilation of unit boxes

Let \(0<t\le t'\). If \(q\) is an integer satisfying

\[ q>\frac{2t'}{t}, \]

then

\[ |U_K(t')|\le q^{d-1}|U_K(t)|. \]

Proof

Work in the logarithmic lattice \(\Lambda_K\subset H_d\). As above, write

\[ Q_s=H_d\cap[-s,s]^d. \]

Partition \(\Lambda_K\cap Q_{t'}\) into residue classes modulo \(q\Lambda_K\). There are \(q^{d-1}\) such classes.

Fix one nonempty class and choose \(x_0\) in it. For any other \(x\) in the same class,

\[ x-x_0=qy \]

for some \(y\in\Lambda_K\). Since \(x,x_0\in Q_{t'}\),

\[ x-x_0\in Q_{2t'}. \]

Thus

\[ y\in Q_{2t'/q}\subset Q_t. \]

So each residue class contributes at most \(|\Lambda_K\cap Q_t|\) points. Therefore

\[ |\Lambda_K\cap Q_{t'}| \le q^{d-1}|\Lambda_K\cap Q_t|. \]

Since the kernel of the logarithmic map is \(\{\pm1\}\), the same inequality holds for units:

\[ |U_K(t')|\le q^{d-1}|U_K(t)|. \]

\(\square\)

3. Choice of constants

Let \(D,E\) be the constants from Lemma 1.1.

Choose \(T\ge1\) so large that

\[ T>80E \]

and

\[ 4T+\log 5 < 2\bigl(3T-\log(8\sqrt D)\bigr). \]

This is possible because the left side is asymptotic to \(4T\), while the right side is asymptotic to \(6T\).

Set

\[ L:=4T, \qquad X:=e^L=e^{4T}, \]
\[ Y:=\frac{e^{L-T}}4=\frac{e^{3T}}4, \]

and

\[ M:=\lceil 2Y\rceil. \]

Define

\[ \alpha := \log\left(\frac{Y}{2\sqrt D}\right) = 3T-\log(8\sqrt D), \]
\[ \beta := \log(3X) = 4T+\log3, \]

and

\[ \beta_+ := \log(5X) = 4T+\log5. \]

By our choice of \(T\),

\[ \alpha>0 \]

and

\[ \frac{\beta_+}{\alpha}<2. \]

Let

\[ \delta_{\mathrm{sum}} := 2-\frac{\beta_+}{\alpha} >0. \]

Next set

\[ \rho:=\frac{T}{4E}. \]

Since \(T>80E\),

\[ \rho>20. \]

Define

\[ \eta:=\log\left(\frac{\rho}{20}\right)>0 \]

and

\[ \delta_{\mathrm{prod}} := \frac{\eta}{\beta}>0. \]

Finally, define

\[ \delta := \frac12\min\{\delta_{\mathrm{sum}},\delta_{\mathrm{prod}}\}. \]

This is a positive absolute constant.

4. Construction in one field

Let \(K\) be one of the totally real fields from Lemma 1.1, of degree

\[ d=[K:\mathbb Q]. \]

Assume \(d\) is sufficiently large; later we discard finitely many fields.

Define

\[ C_K := \left\{ M+\beta: \beta\in B_K(Y) \right\}. \]

Define

\[ \widetilde A_K := U_K(T)C_K = \{uc:u\in U_K(T),\ c\in C_K\} \subset\mathcal O_K. \]

Finally, fix the first real embedding and put

\[ A_K:=\sigma_1(\widetilde A_K)\subset\mathbb R. \]

Because \(\sigma_1\) is an injective ring homomorphism, it preserves all additive and multiplicative relations. Hence

\[ |A_K|=|\widetilde A_K|, \]
\[ |A_K+A_K|=|\widetilde A_K+\widetilde A_K|, \]

and

\[ |A_KA_K|=|\widetilde A_K\widetilde A_K|. \]

Write

\[ n:=|A_K|=|\widetilde A_K|. \]

5. Basic archimedean bounds

Let \(c\in C_K\). Then

\[ c=M+\beta \]

for some \(\beta\in B_K(Y)\). Thus for every embedding \(\sigma_j\),

\[ |\sigma_j(\beta)|\le Y. \]

Since \(M\ge2Y\),

\[ \sigma_j(c) = M+\sigma_j(\beta) \ge M-Y \ge Y. \]

Also \(Y\ge1\), so

\[ M=\lceil2Y\rceil\le2Y+1\le3Y. \]

Hence

\[ |\sigma_j(c)| \le M+|\sigma_j(\beta)| \le 3Y+Y = 4Y. \]

Thus

\[ Y\le |\sigma_j(c)|\le 4Y \]

for every \(j\).

Now take \(a\in\widetilde A_K\). Write

\[ a=uc \]

with \(u\in U_K(T)\) and \(c\in C_K\). Since

\[ e^{-T}\le |\sigma_j(u)|\le e^T, \]

we get

\[ Ye^{-T} \le |\sigma_j(a)| \le 4Ye^T. \]

But

\[ 4Ye^T = 4\cdot\frac{e^{L-T}}4\cdot e^T = e^L = X. \]

Therefore

\[ |\sigma_j(a)|\le X \]

for every \(a\in\widetilde A_K\) and every \(j\).

We will also use the lower bound

\[ |\sigma_j(a)|\ge \ell, \qquad \ell:=Ye^{-T}. \]

Since \(Y=e^{3T}/4\),

\[ \ell=\frac{e^{2T}}4. \]

6. Size of \(A_K\)

Because \(1\in U_K(T)\),

\[ C_K\subset\widetilde A_K. \]

Therefore

\[ n\ge |C_K|=|B_K(Y)|. \]

By Lemma 2.1,

\[ |B_K(Y)| \ge \frac{Y^d}{\sqrt{|\Delta_K|}}-1. \]

Since \(|\Delta_K|^{1/d}\le D\),

\[ \sqrt{|\Delta_K|}\le D^{d/2}. \]

Thus

\[ n \ge \left(\frac{Y}{\sqrt D}\right)^d-1. \]

By definition of \(\alpha\),

\[ \frac{Y}{\sqrt D} = 2e^\alpha. \]

Since \(\alpha>0\),

\[ (2e^\alpha)^d-1\ge e^{\alpha d} \]

for every \(d\ge1\). Hence

\[ n\ge e^{\alpha d}. \tag{6.1} \]

On the other hand, we proved that

\[ \widetilde A_K\subset B_K(X). \]

Therefore, by Lemma 2.1,

\[ n \le |B_K(X)| \le (2X+1)^d. \]

Since \(X\ge1\),

\[ 2X+1\le3X. \]

Hence

\[ n\le (3X)^d=e^{\beta d}. \tag{6.2} \]

7. Sumset estimate

For \(a,b\in\widetilde A_K\),

\[ |\sigma_j(a+b)| \le |\sigma_j(a)|+|\sigma_j(b)| \le 2X. \]

Thus

\[ \widetilde A_K+\widetilde A_K\subset B_K(2X). \]

By Lemma 2.1,

\[ |\widetilde A_K+\widetilde A_K| \le |B_K(2X)| \le (4X+1)^d. \]

Since \(X\ge1\),

\[ 4X+1\le5X. \]

Therefore

\[ |A_K+A_K| = |\widetilde A_K+\widetilde A_K| \le (5X)^d = e^{\beta_+d}. \]

Using \(n\ge e^{\alpha d}\), we get

\[ e^{\beta_+d} \le n^{\beta_+/\alpha}. \]

Hence

\[ |A_K+A_K| \le n^{\beta_+/\alpha} = n^{2-\delta_{\mathrm{sum}}}. \]

Since \(\delta\le\delta_{\mathrm{sum}}\),

\[ |A_K+A_K|\le n^{2-\delta}. \tag{7.1} \]

8. Product set estimate

Let

\[ U:=U_K(T). \]

Let \(\mathcal P\) be the set of principal ideals represented by elements of \(\widetilde A_K\):

\[ \mathcal P := \{(a):a\in\widetilde A_K\}. \]

8.1. Each represented ideal has many representatives

Fix \(I\in\mathcal P\). Choose \(a\in\widetilde A_K\) with

\[ I=(a). \]

Write

\[ a=uc \]

with \(u\in U\) and \(c\in C_K\). Since \(u\) is a unit,

\[ (a)=(c). \]

For every \(v\in U\),

\[ vc\in U C_K=\widetilde A_K \]

and

\[ (vc)=(c)=I. \]

The elements \(vc\) are distinct as \(v\) varies because \(c\ne0\). Therefore every ideal in \(\mathcal P\) has at least \(|U|\) representatives in \(\widetilde A_K\). Hence

\[ |\mathcal P| \le \frac{n}{|U|}. \tag{8.1} \]

8.2. Product ideals

If

\[ z=ab\in\widetilde A_K\widetilde A_K, \]

then

\[ (z)=(a)(b) \]

with \((a),(b)\in\mathcal P\). Thus the number of possible principal ideals generated by products is at most

\[ |\mathcal P|^2 \le \left(\frac{n}{|U|}\right)^2. \tag{8.2} \]

8.3. Generators per product ideal

Every \(a\in\widetilde A_K\) satisfies

\[ \ell\le|\sigma_j(a)|\le X \]

for every \(j\). Therefore every product \(z=ab\) satisfies

\[ \ell^2\le|\sigma_j(z)|\le X^2. \]

Fix a product \(z_0\in\widetilde A_K\widetilde A_K\). If another product \(z\) generates the same principal ideal, then

\[ (z)=(z_0), \]

so

\[ z=\varepsilon z_0 \]

for some unit \(\varepsilon\in\mathcal O_K^\times\).

For each embedding,

\[ |\sigma_j(\varepsilon)| = \frac{|\sigma_j(z)|}{|\sigma_j(z_0)|}. \]

Since both \(z\) and \(z_0\) have all conjugates between \(\ell^2\) and \(X^2\),

\[ \frac{\ell^2}{X^2} \le |\sigma_j(\varepsilon)| \le \frac{X^2}{\ell^2}. \]

Hence

\[ |\log|\sigma_j(\varepsilon)||\le 2\log(X/\ell). \]

Now

\[ X=e^{4T}, \qquad \ell=\frac{e^{2T}}4, \]

so

\[ \frac{X}{\ell}=4e^{2T}. \]

Thus

\[ 2\log(X/\ell)=4T+\log16. \]

Set

\[ S:=4T+\log16. \]

Then every quotient unit \(\varepsilon\) arising above lies in \(U_K(S)\). Therefore each product ideal contributes at most \(|U_K(S)|\) elements to the product set.

Combining with (8.2),

\[ |\widetilde A_K\widetilde A_K| \le \left(\frac{n}{|U_K(T)|}\right)^2 |U_K(S)|. \tag{8.3} \]

8.4. Comparing \(U_K(S)\) to \(U_K(T)\)

Since \(T\ge1\),

\[ \frac{S}{T} = 4+\frac{\log16}{T} < 7. \]

Thus

\[ 20>\frac{2S}{T}. \]

By Lemma 2.3 with \(q=20\),

\[ |U_K(S)|\le20^{d-1}|U_K(T)|\le20^d|U_K(T)|. \]

Substitute into (8.3):

\[ |\widetilde A_K\widetilde A_K| \le n^2\frac{20^d}{|U_K(T)|}. \tag{8.4} \]

8.5. Lower bound for \(|U_K(T)|\)

By Lemma 2.2,

\[ |U_K(T)| \ge \frac{(T/2)^{d-1}}{R_K}-1. \]

Since \(R_K\le E^d\),

\[ |U_K(T)| \ge \frac{(T/2)^{d-1}}{E^d}-1. \]

Recall

\[ \rho=\frac{T}{4E}. \]

Then

\[ \frac{(T/2)^{d-1}}{E^d} = \rho^d\frac{2^{d+1}}{T}. \]

Since \(d\to\infty\), for all sufficiently large \(d\),

\[ \frac{2^{d+1}}{T}\ge2. \]

Because \(\rho>20>1\), we then have

\[ \frac{(T/2)^{d-1}}{E^d}-1 \ge 2\rho^d-1 \ge \rho^d. \]

Thus, after discarding finitely many fields,

\[ |U_K(T)|\ge\rho^d. \tag{8.5} \]

Combining (8.4) and (8.5),

\[ |\widetilde A_K\widetilde A_K| \le n^2\left(\frac{20}{\rho}\right)^d. \]

Since

\[ \eta=\log\left(\frac{\rho}{20}\right)>0, \]

this is

\[ |\widetilde A_K\widetilde A_K| \le n^2e^{-\eta d}. \]

Using the upper size bound \(n\le e^{\beta d}\), we have

\[ d\ge\frac{\log n}{\beta}. \]

Therefore

\[ e^{-\eta d} \le n^{-\eta/\beta}. \]

Hence

\[ |A_KA_K| = |\widetilde A_K\widetilde A_K| \le n^{2-\eta/\beta} = n^{2-\delta_{\mathrm{prod}}}. \]

Since \(\delta\le\delta_{\mathrm{prod}}\),

\[ |A_KA_K|\le n^{2-\delta}. \tag{8.6} \]

9. Completion

Let \(K_i\) be the infinite sequence from Lemma 1.1. Discard finitely many initial fields so that the large-degree assumption used in (8.5) holds for every remaining \(K_i\). For each remaining \(K_i\), construct

\[ A_i:=A_{K_i}\subset\mathbb R \]

as above.

By (6.1),

\[ |A_i|\ge e^{\alpha d_i}. \]

Since \(d_i\to\infty\) and \(\alpha>0\),

\[ |A_i|\to\infty. \]

By (7.1) and (8.6),

\[ |A_i+A_i|\le |A_i|^{2-\delta} \]

and

\[ |A_iA_i|\le |A_i|^{2-\delta}. \]

Therefore

\[ \max(|A_i+A_i|,\ |A_iA_i|) \le |A_i|^{2-\delta} \]

for all \(i\), with one fixed absolute constant \(\delta>0\).

This proves the theorem. \(\square\)
