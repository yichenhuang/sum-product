Critical corrections to the previous proof

The broad unit-based construction is viable, but the previous write-up had several gaps:

  1. Regulator control was not fully justified. We need an infinite totally real family with both bounded root discriminant and regulator at most exponential in the degree. This follows from Martinet’s totally real class-field towers together with Landau’s residue bound and the analytic class number formula. I state this precisely below.

  2. The random collision argument used a random threshold incorrectly. Previously I bounded \(Y\) by a multiple of \(R X\), where \(X=|S|\) is random, directly via Markov. The correct argument first proves \(X\) is close to its mean and \(Y\) is small compared with that mean, then combines the two events.

  3. The unit-lattice covolume and hyperplane-volume factors need to be handled. The logarithmic unit lattice has covolume \(\asymp \sqrt d\,R_K\), not exactly \(R_K\). This polynomial factor is harmless but must be absorbed carefully.

  4. The collision expectation had a constant error. The ratio is essentially \((C_U/\kappa)^d\), not \((C_U/\kappa^2)^d\), after comparing to the correct normalization.

With those points fixed, the proof is as follows.

Proof

We use the following standard arithmetic input.

1. Arithmetic input

There exist absolute constants \(\Delta>1\), \(C_R>1\), and an infinite sequence of totally real number fields

\[ K_i/\mathbb Q \]

with degrees

\[ d_i=[K_i:\mathbb Q]\to\infty \]

such that

\[ |D_{K_i}|^{1/d_i}\le \Delta \]

and

\[ R_{K_i}\le C_R^{d_i}, \]

where \(D_K\) is the discriminant and \(R_K\) the regulator.

Indeed, Martinet constructed infinite totally real class-field towers with bounded root discriminant. Landau’s residue estimate gives, for a number field \(K\) of degree \(d\),

\[ \operatorname*{Res}_{s=1}\zeta_K(s)\le C_1^d \]

whenever \(|D_K|^{1/d}\le \Delta\), for some \(C_1=C_1(\Delta)\). For totally real \(K\),

\[ \operatorname*{Res}_{s=1}\zeta_K(s) = \frac{2^{d-1}h_KR_K}{\sqrt{|D_K|}}. \]

Since \(h_K\ge1\), this implies

\[ R_K \le \frac{\sqrt{|D_K|}}{2^{d-1}} \operatorname*{Res}_{s=1}\zeta_K(s) \le C_R^d \]

for some \(C_R=C_R(\Delta)\).

Fix such a sequence \(K_i\).

2. Basic counting lemmas

Let \(K\) be totally real of degree \(d\), with real embeddings

\[ \sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_d:K\hookrightarrow \mathbb R. \]

Write

\[ \Sigma(x)=(\sigma_1(x),\dots,\sigma_d(x)). \]

Lemma 2.1: packing of algebraic integers

For every \(T\ge0\) and every \(y\in\mathbb R^d\),

\[ \#\left\{ \alpha\in\mathcal O_K: |\sigma_j(\alpha)-y_j|\le T\text{ for all }j \right\} \le (2T+1)^d. \]

Proof

If \(\alpha\neq\beta\in\mathcal O_K\), then

\[ N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\alpha-\beta)\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}, \]

so

\[ \prod_{j=1}^d|\sigma_j(\alpha-\beta)|\ge1. \]

Hence

\[ \max_j|\sigma_j(\alpha-\beta)|\ge1. \]

Thus \(\Sigma(\mathcal O_K)\) is \(1\)-separated in the \(\ell^\infty\)-metric. Disjoint \(\ell^\infty\)-balls of radius \(1/2\) around all points in a box of radius \(T\) lie in the concentric box of radius \(T+1/2\), whose volume is \((2T+1)^d\). \(\square\)

Lemma 2.2: Blichfeldt difference lemma

Let \(\Lambda\subset\mathbb R^r\) be a lattice of covolume \(\det\Lambda\), and let \(\Omega\subset\mathbb R^r\) be bounded and measurable. Then

\[ |\Lambda\cap(\Omega-\Omega)| \ge \frac{\operatorname{vol}(\Omega)}{\det\Lambda}. \]

This is the usual Blichfeldt principle: some translate of \(\Omega\) contains at least \(\operatorname{vol}(\Omega)/\det\Lambda\) lattice points, and their pairwise differences lie in \(\Omega-\Omega\).

3. Many bounded units

Let

\[ H_d=\left\{x\in\mathbb R^d:\sum_{j=1}^d x_j=0\right\}. \]

The logarithmic embedding of units is

\[ \ell:\mathcal O_K^\times\to H_d, \qquad \ell(u)=\left(\log|\sigma_1(u)|,\dots,\log|\sigma_d(u)|\right). \]

Its image \(\Lambda_K=\ell(\mathcal O_K^\times)\) is a lattice in \(H_d\) with covolume \(\asymp \sqrt d\,R_K\).

For \(L>0\), define

\[ Q_L^{(d)}=\{x\in H_d:\|x\|_\infty\le L\}. \]

By the cube-slicing theorem of Vaaler/Ball, there is an absolute constant \(c_0>0\) such that

\[ \operatorname{vol}_{H_d}(Q_L^{(d)})\ge (c_0L)^{d-1}. \]

Also, by a simple covering argument, there is an absolute constant \(C_U>1\) such that for every lattice \(\Lambda\subset H_d\),

\[ |\Lambda\cap Q_{2L}^{(d)}| \le C_U^d|\Lambda\cap Q_L^{(d)}|. \]

Indeed, cover the cube \([-2L,2L]^d\) by \(O(1)^d\) cubes of side length \(L\). In each small cube, after subtracting one lattice point, all other lattice points inject into \(\Lambda\cap Q_L^{(d)}\).

Now choose \(L>1\) large, to be specified later, and define

\[ U_K= \left\{ u\in\mathcal O_K^\times: |\log|\sigma_j(u)||\le L\text{ for all }j \right\}. \]

Equivalently,

\[ e^{-L}\le |\sigma_j(u)|\le e^L \qquad \forall u\in U_K,\ \forall j. \]

By Lemma 2.2 applied to \(\Lambda_K\subset H_d\),

\[ |\Lambda_K\cap Q_L^{(d)}| \ge \frac{\operatorname{vol}(Q_{L/2}^{(d)})}{\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda_K)} \gg \frac{(c_0L)^{d-1}}{\sqrt d\,R_K}. \]

Since \(R_K\le C_R^d\), after discarding finitely many fields,

\[ |U_K|\ge \rho^d \]

for some

\[ \rho=\rho(L)>1, \]

and by taking \(L\) sufficiently large, \(\rho\) can be made arbitrarily large.

On the other hand, Lemma 2.1 gives

\[ |U_K|\le (2e^L+1)^d. \]

Set

\[ \rho_+=2e^L+1. \]

Thus

\[ \rho^d\le |U_K|\le \rho_+^d. \]

Moreover,

\[ U_KU_K,\ U_KU_K^{-1} \subset \{u\in\mathcal O_K^\times:\|\ell(u)\|_\infty\le 2L\}, \]

so the lattice doubling bound gives

\[ |U_KU_K|\le C_U^d|U_K|, \qquad |U_KU_K^{-1}|\le C_U^d|U_K|. \]

4. A large bounded pool of algebraic integers

Fix a large parameter \(W>1\), to be chosen later. Define

\[ T_{0,K} = \left\{ t\in\mathcal O_K: |\sigma_j(t)|\le 2W\text{ for all }j \right\}. \]

Let

\[ P_K=|T_{0,K}|. \]

By Lemma 2.2 applied to the lattice \(\Sigma(\mathcal O_K)\subset\mathbb R^d\), whose covolume is \(\sqrt{|D_K|}\),

\[ P_K \ge \frac{(2W)^d}{\sqrt{|D_K|}} \ge \left(\frac{2W}{\sqrt\Delta}\right)^d. \]

Set

\[ \Pi_-=\frac{2W}{\sqrt\Delta}. \]

By Lemma 2.1,

\[ P_K\le (4W+1)^d. \]

Now choose the integer

\[ M=\lceil 4W\rceil \]

and set

\[ T_K=M+T_{0,K}. \]

Then

\[ |T_K|=P_K, \]

and every \(s\in T_K\) satisfies

\[ W\le \sigma_j(s)\le 7W \qquad \forall j. \]

In particular, every element of \(T_K\) is nonzero.

5. Random sparse selection

Write

\[ U=U_K,\qquad N=|U|. \]

Choose a random subset

\[ S\subset T_K \]

by including each element independently with probability

\[ p=\frac1{\kappa^dN}, \]

where \(\kappa>1\) is a constant to be chosen later.

Let

\[ X=|S|, \qquad \mu=\mathbb E X=pP_K. \]

We also count collisions:

\[ Y= \#\left\{ (u,u',s,s')\in U^2\times S^2: us=u's',\ (u,s)\neq(u',s') \right\}. \]

For \(us=u's'\), we have

\[ s'=u'^{-1}us. \]

Let

\[ w=u'^{-1}u\in U^{-1}U. \]

For fixed \(w\), there are at most \(N\) ordered pairs \((u,u')\in U^2\) with \(u'^{-1}u=w\). Also,

\[ |U^{-1}U|\le C_U^dN. \]

For each fixed \(w\neq1\), the number of \(s\in T_K\) with \(ws\in T_K\) is at most \(P_K\). Since every \(s\in T_K\) is nonzero, \(ws=s\) would force \(w=1\). Hence for \(w\neq1\), the two elements \(s\) and \(ws\) are distinct, so the probability that both lie in \(S\) is \(p^2\).

Therefore

\[ \mathbb E Y \le N|U^{-1}U|P_Kp^2 \le C_U^dN^2P_Kp^2. \]

Since

\[ p=\frac1{\kappa^dN}, \]

we get

\[ \mathbb E Y \le \left(\frac{C_U}{\kappa^2}\right)^dP_K. \]

But

\[ N\mu=NpP_K=\frac{P_K}{\kappa^d}. \]

Thus

\[ \mathbb E Y \le \left(\frac{C_U}{\kappa}\right)^dN\mu. \]

Choose \(\kappa\) so large that, for all sufficiently large \(d\),

\[ \left(\frac{C_U}{\kappa}\right)^d\le \frac1{1000}. \]

Then

\[ \mathbb E Y\le \frac1{1000}N\mu. \]

Later we choose \(W\) so large that

\[ \mu=pP_K = \frac{P_K}{\kappa^dN} \ge \left(\frac{\Pi_-}{\kappa\rho_+}\right)^d \to\infty. \]

Hence by Chernoff’s inequality,

\[ \frac12\mu\le X\le2\mu \]

with probability tending to \(1\). By Markov’s inequality,

\[ Y\le \frac18N\mu \]

with probability at least \(1-8/1000\).

Thus, for all sufficiently large \(d\), there exists a subset \(S_K\subset T_K\) such that

\[ \frac12\mu\le |S_K|\le2\mu \]

and

\[ Y\le \frac18N\mu. \]

Since \(|S_K|\ge\mu/2\), this implies

\[ Y\le \frac14N|S_K|. \]

Fix such an \(S_K\).

6. The algebraic set

Define

\[ \mathcal A_K=U_KS_K = \{us:u\in U_K,\ s\in S_K\} \subset\mathcal O_K. \]

Let

\[ n_K=|\mathcal A_K|. \]

For \(a\in\mathcal A_K\), let

\[ r(a)=|\{(u,s)\in U_K\times S_K:us=a\}|. \]

Then

\[ \sum_a r(a)=N|S_K|. \]

Also,

\[ \sum_a r(a)^2 \]

counts all pairs of representations of the same element. The diagonal contribution is \(N|S_K|\), and the off-diagonal contribution is \(Y\). Hence

\[ \sum_a r(a)^2 \le N|S_K|+\frac14N|S_K| = \frac54N|S_K|. \]

By Cauchy–Schwarz,

\[ (N|S_K|)^2 \le n_K\sum_a r(a)^2 \le \frac54n_KN|S_K|. \]

Thus

\[ n_K\ge \frac45N|S_K|. \]

In particular,

\[ \frac12N|S_K|\le n_K\le N|S_K|. \]

Using \(|S_K|\ge\mu/2\), \(\mu=P_K/(\kappa^dN)\), we get

\[ n_K\ge \frac12N\cdot\frac12\mu = \frac{P_K}{4\kappa^d}. \]

Using \(P_K\ge \Pi_-^d\),

\[ n_K\ge \frac{\Pi_-^d}{4\kappa^d} \ge \left(\frac{\Pi_-}{4\kappa}\right)^d. \]

Set

\[ \nu=\frac{\Pi_-}{4\kappa}. \]

Then

\[ n_K\ge \nu^d. \]

Similarly, since \(|S_K|\le2\mu\),

\[ n_K\le N|S_K|\le 2N\mu=\frac{2P_K}{\kappa^d}\le2P_K. \]

Since \(P_K\le(4W+1)^d\), after enlarging constants,

\[ n_K\le \Pi_+^d, \qquad \Pi_+=2(4W+1). \]

7. Choice of parameters

We now choose constants in order.

First choose \(L\) so large that the corresponding \(\rho\) satisfies

\[ \rho>(4C_U)^4. \]

Set

\[ C_\times=4C_U. \]

Next choose \(\kappa\) sufficiently large in terms of \(C_U\), as above.

Finally choose \(W\) sufficiently large that:

  1. \(\Pi_->8\kappa\rho_+\), ensuring \(\mu\to\infty\);

  2. with

    \[ \gamma=\frac{\log\rho}{\log\Pi_+}, \]

    one has

    \[ C_\times\le \nu^{\gamma/2}; \]

  3. and

    \[ 30e^LW\le \nu^{2-\gamma/4}. \]

Such a \(W\) exists. Indeed, as \(W\to\infty\),

\[ \frac{\log\nu}{\log\Pi_+}\to1, \]

so

\[ \nu^{\gamma/2} = \exp\left(\frac{\gamma}{2}\log\nu\right) \to \exp\left(\frac12\log\rho\right) = \rho^{1/2} > C_\times. \]

Also,

\[ \nu^{2-\gamma/4} \asymp W^{2-o(1)}, \]

while \(30e^LW\) grows only linearly in \(W\). Hence condition 3 also holds for large \(W\).

Now fix

\[ \delta=\frac{\gamma}{4}>0. \]

All constants are absolute.

8. Sumset bound

For every \(a=us\in\mathcal A_K\),

\[ |\sigma_j(a)| \le |\sigma_j(u)|\,|\sigma_j(s)| \le e^L\cdot7W = 7e^LW. \]

Therefore every element of

\[ \mathcal A_K+\mathcal A_K \]

has all conjugates bounded in absolute value by

\[ 14e^LW. \]

By Lemma 2.1,

\[ |\mathcal A_K+\mathcal A_K| \le (28e^LW+1)^d \le (30e^LW)^d. \]

By the choice of \(W\),

\[ 30e^LW\le \nu^{2-\gamma/4}. \]

Since \(n_K\ge\nu^d\),

\[ |\mathcal A_K+\mathcal A_K| \le \nu^{(2-\gamma/4)d} \le n_K^{2-\gamma/4} = n_K^{2-\delta}. \]

9. Product-set bound

We have

\[ \mathcal A_K\mathcal A_K = (U_KS_K)(U_KS_K) \subset (U_KU_K)(S_KS_K). \]

Hence

\[ |\mathcal A_K\mathcal A_K| \le |U_KU_K|\,|S_KS_K|. \]

Using

\[ |U_KU_K|\le C_U^d|U_K| \]

and

\[ |S_KS_K|\le |S_K|^2, \]

we obtain

\[ |\mathcal A_K\mathcal A_K| \le C_U^dN|S_K|^2. \]

Since

\[ n_K\ge\frac12N|S_K|, \]

we have

\[ |S_K|\le \frac{2n_K}{N}. \]

Therefore

\[ |\mathcal A_K\mathcal A_K| \le 4C_U^d\frac{n_K^2}{N} \le C_\times^d\frac{n_K^2}{N}. \]

Now \(N=|U_K|\ge\rho^d\), while \(n_K\le\Pi_+^d\). Thus

\[ N\ge n_K^\gamma, \qquad \gamma=\frac{\log\rho}{\log\Pi_+}. \]

So

\[ |\mathcal A_K\mathcal A_K| \le C_\times^d n_K^{2-\gamma}. \]

By the choice of \(W\),

\[ C_\times\le \nu^{\gamma/2}. \]

Since \(n_K\ge\nu^d\),

\[ C_\times^d\le n_K^{\gamma/2}. \]

Therefore

\[ |\mathcal A_K\mathcal A_K| \le n_K^{2-\gamma/2} \le n_K^{2-\delta}. \]

10. Projection to the real line

For each sufficiently large \(K_i\) in the tower, choose one real embedding

\[ \sigma_{i,1}:K_i\hookrightarrow\mathbb R. \]

Define

\[ A_i=\sigma_{i,1}(\mathcal A_{K_i})\subset\mathbb R. \]

Since \(\sigma_{i,1}\) is injective,

\[ |A_i|=|\mathcal A_{K_i}|=n_{K_i}. \]

Also,

\[ A_i+A_i = \sigma_{i,1}(\mathcal A_{K_i}+\mathcal A_{K_i}), \]

and

\[ A_iA_i = \sigma_{i,1}(\mathcal A_{K_i}\mathcal A_{K_i}), \]

so cardinalities are preserved.

Thus

\[ |A_i+A_i| \le |A_i|^{2-\delta} \]

and

\[ |A_iA_i| \le |A_i|^{2-\delta}. \]

Finally,

\[ |A_i|=n_{K_i}\ge \nu^{d_i}, \]

and \(d_i\to\infty\), so \(|A_i|\to\infty\).

Therefore

\[ \max(|A_i+A_i|,\ |A_iA_i|) \le |A_i|^{2-\delta} \]

for infinitely many finite sets \(A_i\subset\mathbb R\), with one absolute constant \(\delta>0\).

This proves the theorem. \(\square\)
