Assistant

Below is the approach I think is most promising for a complete proof, developed to a precise reduction. The key idea is to replace the usual integer/smooth-number constructions by high-degree totally real number fields with many small units.

The reason units are natural is:

  • Multiplication of units is governed by a rank-\(d-1\) lattice, so a bounded box of units has controlled multiplicative doubling.
  • Addition of two units is not a unit, but if all conjugates of the units are bounded, then all conjugates of their sums are bounded; hence the sumset is controlled by the number of algebraic integers in a fixed archimedean box.

The proof below reduces the theorem to a concrete arithmetic construction of totally real fields satisfying two counting estimates. I prove the reduction and several lattice-counting lemmas. The main remaining gap is an arithmetic one: constructing fields with sufficiently many bounded units but not too many bounded algebraic integers.

1. The proposed construction

Let \(K/\mathbb Q\) be a totally real number field of degree \(d\), with real embeddings

\[ \sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_d:K\hookrightarrow \mathbb R. \]

For \(R>0\), define the set of \(R\)-bounded units

\[ U_K(R) := \left\{ u\in \mathcal O_K^\times: e^{-R}\le |\sigma_j(u)|\le e^R \ \text{for every } j=1,\dots,d \right\}. \]

Equivalently, under the logarithmic embedding

\[ \ell: \mathcal O_K^\times \to H_d :=\left\{(x_1,\dots,x_d)\in \mathbb R^d:\sum_{j=1}^d x_j=0\right\}, \]
\[ \ell(u)=(\log|\sigma_1(u)|,\dots,\log|\sigma_d(u)|), \]

we have

\[ U_K(R)=\ell^{-1}(C_R), \qquad C_R:=H_d\cap [-R,R]^d. \]

Now define a finite subset of \(\mathbb R\) by choosing one embedding, say \(\sigma_1\), and setting

\[ A_K(R):=\sigma_1\bigl(U_K(R)\bigr)\subset \mathbb R. \]

Because \(\sigma_1\) is injective on \(K\),

\[ |A_K(R)|=|U_K(R)|. \]

The goal is to find a sequence of totally real fields \(K_i\) with \(d_i=[K_i:\mathbb Q]\to\infty\), and a fixed \(R>0\), such that

\[ |U_{K_i}(R)|\to\infty \]

and both the sumset and product set of \(A_{K_i}(R)\) are \(< |A_{K_i}(R)|^{2-\delta}\).

The following reduction isolates the arithmetic input needed.

2. Basic containment estimates

For \(M>0\), define the set of algebraic integers with all conjugates bounded by \(M\):

\[ B_K(M):= \left\{ x\in \mathcal O_K: |\sigma_j(x)|\le M \ \text{for every } j=1,\dots,d \right\}. \]

We need two simple containments.

Lemma 1: Sumset and product set containment

Let \(K\) be totally real and \(R>0\). Put \(A=A_K(R)=\sigma_1(U_K(R))\). Then

\[ A+A \subseteq \sigma_1\bigl(B_K(2e^R)\bigr), \]

and

\[ AA \subseteq \sigma_1\bigl(U_K(2R)\bigr). \]

Consequently,

\[ |A+A|\le |B_K(2e^R)|, \]

and

\[ |AA|\le |U_K(2R)|. \]

Proof

Take \(u,v\in U_K(R)\). For every embedding \(\sigma_j\),

\[ |\sigma_j(u+v)| \le |\sigma_j(u)|+|\sigma_j(v)| \le 2e^R. \]

Also \(u+v\in\mathcal O_K\). Hence

\[ u+v\in B_K(2e^R), \]

which gives

\[ A+A\subseteq \sigma_1(B_K(2e^R)). \]

For products,

\[ \sigma_j(uv)=\sigma_j(u)\sigma_j(v), \]

so

\[ e^{-2R}\le |\sigma_j(uv)|\le e^{2R} \]

for every \(j\). Thus

\[ uv\in U_K(2R), \]

and therefore

\[ AA\subseteq \sigma_1(U_K(2R)). \]

Since \(\sigma_1\) is injective, the cardinality bounds follow. \(\square\)

So the problem becomes: find fields \(K\) for which

  1. \(U_K(R)\) is large;
  2. \(B_K(2e^R)\) is not too large;
  3. \(U_K(2R)\) is not too large compared with \(U_K(R)\).

The third point follows from a general lattice-doubling lemma.

3. A lattice-doubling lemma for units

Let

\[ \Lambda_K:=\ell(\mathcal O_K^\times)\subset H_d \]

be the unit lattice. Since \(K\) is totally real, \(\ker \ell=\{\pm 1\}\), so

\[ |U_K(R)|=2|\Lambda_K\cap C_R|. \]

We need to control \(|U_K(2R)|\) in terms of \(|U_K(R)|\).

Lemma 2: Lattice points in a doubled convex body

Let \(\Lambda\) be a lattice of rank \(r\) in a real vector space \(V\), and let \(C\subset V\) be a centrally symmetric convex body. Then

\[ |\Lambda\cap 2C|\le 5^r |\Lambda\cap C|. \]

Proof

Set

\[ S:=\Lambda\cap 2C. \]

Choose a maximal subset \(Y\subseteq S\) such that for distinct \(y,y'\in Y\),

\[ y-y'\notin C. \]

Then the translates

\[ y+\frac12 C,\qquad y\in Y, \]

are pairwise disjoint. Indeed, if

\[ \left(y+\frac12 C\right)\cap\left(y'+\frac12 C\right)\neq\varnothing, \]

then

\[ y-y'\in \frac12 C-\frac12 C=C, \]

contradicting the choice of \(Y\).

Also, since \(y\in 2C\),

\[ y+\frac12 C\subseteq \frac52 C. \]

Therefore,

\[ |Y|\operatorname{vol}\left(\frac12 C\right) \le \operatorname{vol}\left(\frac52 C\right). \]

Since \(\dim V=r\),

\[ \frac{\operatorname{vol}((5/2)C)} {\operatorname{vol}((1/2)C)} = 5^r. \]

Hence

\[ |Y|\le 5^r. \]

By maximality of \(Y\), every \(s\in S\) satisfies

\[ s-y\in C \]

for some \(y\in Y\). Since \(s,y\in\Lambda\), we have \(s-y\in\Lambda\cap C\). Thus

\[ S\subseteq Y+(\Lambda\cap C), \]

and therefore

\[ |\Lambda\cap 2C|=|S| \le |Y|\,|\Lambda\cap C| \le 5^r|\Lambda\cap C|. \]

\(\square\)

Applying this to \(C=C_R\subset H_d\), whose ambient dimension is \(d-1\), gives:

Corollary 3: Unit doubling

For every totally real field \(K\) of degree \(d\),

\[ |U_K(2R)|\le 5^{d-1}|U_K(R)|. \]

Proof

Since \(C_{2R}=2C_R\),

\[ |U_K(2R)| = 2|\Lambda_K\cap C_{2R}| = 2|\Lambda_K\cap 2C_R|. \]

By Lemma 2,

\[ |\Lambda_K\cap 2C_R| \le 5^{d-1}|\Lambda_K\cap C_R|. \]

Thus

\[ |U_K(2R)| \le 2\cdot 5^{d-1}|\Lambda_K\cap C_R| = 5^{d-1}|U_K(R)|. \]

\(\square\)

This already gives a robust multiplicative estimate. The price is the factor \(5^d\). If one can prove stronger regularity of the unit lattice, this factor can plausibly be improved to \(2^{d+o(d)}\), but \(5^d\) is enough for a first reduction.

4. Conditional theorem: the arithmetic input needed

The preceding lemmas show that the sum-product theorem follows from the existence of totally real fields with many bounded units and comparatively few bounded algebraic integers.

Here is the clean conditional statement.

Proposition 4: Reduction to two counting estimates

Suppose there exist constants

\[ R>0,\qquad \lambda>0,\qquad \mu>0 \]

with

\[ \lambda>\log 5 \]

and

\[ \mu<2\lambda, \]

and an infinite sequence of totally real fields \(K_i\) of degrees \(d_i\to\infty\), such that

\[ |U_{K_i}(R)|\ge \exp\bigl((\lambda-o(1))d_i\bigr), \tag{1} \]

and

\[ |B_{K_i}(2e^R)|\le \exp\bigl((\mu+o(1))d_i\bigr). \tag{2} \]

Then there exists \(\delta>0\) and an infinite sequence of finite sets \(A_i\subset\mathbb R\), with \(|A_i|\to\infty\), such that

\[ \max(|A_i+A_i|,|A_iA_i|) \le |A_i|^{2-\delta}. \]

More explicitly, any

\[ 0<\delta< \min\left\{ 1-\frac{\log 5}{\lambda}, \, 2-\frac{\mu}{\lambda} \right\} \]

works.

Proof

Let

\[ A_i:=A_{K_i}(R)=\sigma_{i,1}(U_{K_i}(R)). \]

Since \(\sigma_{i,1}\) is injective,

\[ |A_i|=|U_{K_i}(R)|. \]

By assumption,

\[ |A_i| \ge \exp((\lambda-o(1))d_i), \]

so \(|A_i|\to\infty\).

For the sumset, Lemma 1 gives

\[ |A_i+A_i| \le |B_{K_i}(2e^R)| \le \exp((\mu+o(1))d_i). \]

Since

\[ |A_i|\ge \exp((\lambda-o(1))d_i), \]

we have

\[ |A_i+A_i| \le |A_i|^{\mu/\lambda+o(1)}. \]

Because \(\mu<2\lambda\), for every

\[ \delta<2-\frac{\mu}{\lambda}, \]

we get

\[ |A_i+A_i|\le |A_i|^{2-\delta} \]

for all sufficiently large \(i\).

For the product set, Lemma 1 and Corollary 3 give

\[ |A_iA_i| \le |U_{K_i}(2R)| \le 5^{d_i-1}|U_{K_i}(R)| = 5^{d_i-1}|A_i|. \]

Again using

\[ |A_i|\ge \exp((\lambda-o(1))d_i), \]

we get

\[ 5^{d_i-1} \le |A_i|^{\log 5/\lambda+o(1)}. \]

Hence

\[ |A_iA_i| \le |A_i|^{1+\log 5/\lambda+o(1)}. \]

Because \(\lambda>\log 5\),

\[ 1+\frac{\log 5}{\lambda}<2. \]

Therefore, for every

\[ \delta<1-\frac{\log 5}{\lambda}, \]

we have

\[ |A_iA_i|\le |A_i|^{2-\delta} \]

for all sufficiently large \(i\).

Choosing

\[ 0<\delta< \min\left\{ 1-\frac{\log 5}{\lambda}, \, 2-\frac{\mu}{\lambda} \right\} \]

proves the claim. \(\square\)

So the theorem is reduced to proving the following arithmetic input.

5. The arithmetic input to prove next

We need:

Arithmetic Input. There exist constants \(R>0\), \(\lambda>\log 5\), \(\mu<2\lambda\), and infinitely many totally real fields \(K_i\) of degrees \(d_i\to\infty\), such that

\[ |U_{K_i}(R)|\ge \exp((\lambda-o(1))d_i), \]

and

\[ |B_{K_i}(2e^R)|\le \exp((\mu+o(1))d_i). \]

The rest of this answer explains why this input is plausible, how to attack it, and what the precise remaining gaps are.

6. How one expects to prove the arithmetic input

The two quantities in the arithmetic input are controlled by two lattices attached to \(K\):

  1. The unit log lattice

    \[ \Lambda_K=\ell(\mathcal O_K^\times)\subset H_d. \]

  2. The Minkowski lattice

    \[ \Sigma_K(\mathcal O_K) := \{(\sigma_1(x),\dots,\sigma_d(x)):x\in\mathcal O_K\} \subset \mathbb R^d. \]

The first controls \(U_K(R)\). The second controls \(B_K(2e^R)\).

6.1. Counting bounded units from the regulator and lattice shape

Let

\[ \mathcal R_K:=\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda_K\subset H_d). \]

Up to a factor \(d^{1/2}\), this is the usual regulator of \(K\). Since \(d^{1/2}=\exp(o(d))\), this factor is irrelevant for exponential estimates.

The body

\[ C_R=H_d\cap[-R,R]^d \]

has \((d-1)\)-dimensional volume

\[ \operatorname{vol}_{H_d}(C_R) = \exp(o(d))(2R)^{d-1}. \tag{3} \]

This follows, for example, from standard cube-slicing estimates: the central hyperplane section of the cube \([-R,R]^d\) has volume \(e^{o(d)}(2R)^{d-1}\).

So if the unit lattice is reasonably well-distributed, one expects

\[ |U_K(R)| = 2|\Lambda_K\cap C_R| \approx \frac{\operatorname{vol}_{H_d}(C_R)}{\mathcal R_K} \approx \frac{(2R)^d}{\mathcal R_K}. \]

Thus a sufficient condition for

\[ |U_K(R)|\ge \exp(\lambda d) \]

is roughly

\[ \mathcal R_K\le \exp(\rho d) \]

with

\[ \lambda<\log(2R)-\rho. \]

To make \(\lambda>\log 5\), we want

\[ \log(2R)-\rho>\log 5. \]

For example, if \(R=4\), then \(\log(2R)=\log 8\), so one needs roughly

\[ \rho<\log 8-\log 5=\log(8/5)\approx 0.470. \]

That is not absurd: known lower bounds for regulators are exponential, but the exponent is small enough that this numerical range is plausible.

A precise lattice-counting lemma is useful here.

Lemma 5: Counting lattice points from a bounded fundamental domain

Let \(\Lambda\subset H_d\) be a lattice, and suppose \(\Lambda\) has a measurable fundamental domain \(F\subset C_\eta\). Then for \(R>\eta\),

\[ |\Lambda\cap C_R| \ge \frac{\operatorname{vol}_{H_d}(C_{R-\eta})} {\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda)}. \]

Proof

Every point \(y\in H_d\) has a unique representation

\[ y=\lambda+f, \]

with \(\lambda\in\Lambda\), \(f\in F\).

If \(y\in C_{R-\eta}\) and \(f\in C_\eta\), then

\[ \lambda=y-f\in C_R. \]

Thus

\[ C_{R-\eta} \subseteq \bigcup_{\lambda\in \Lambda\cap C_R}(\lambda+F). \]

Taking volumes gives

\[ \operatorname{vol}_{H_d}(C_{R-\eta}) \le |\Lambda\cap C_R|\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda). \]

So

\[ |\Lambda\cap C_R| \ge \frac{\operatorname{vol}_{H_d}(C_{R-\eta})} {\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda)}. \]

\(\square\)

Thus, if one can construct fields \(K_i\) whose unit lattices have:

  • covolume \(\mathcal R_{K_i}\le \exp(\rho d_i)\), and
  • bounded covering radius in the \(\|\cdot\|_\infty\)-geometry of \(H_d\),

then one gets the desired lower bound for \(|U_K(R)|\).

6.2. Counting bounded algebraic integers from the discriminant and lattice shape

Now consider

\[ B_K(M) = \{x\in\mathcal O_K:|\sigma_j(x)|\le M\ \forall j\}. \]

Under the Minkowski embedding, this is

\[ \Sigma_K(\mathcal O_K)\cap[-M,M]^d. \]

The Minkowski lattice has covolume

\[ \operatorname{covol}\bigl(\Sigma_K(\mathcal O_K)\bigr) = \sqrt{D_K}, \]

where \(D_K\) is the absolute discriminant of \(K\).

If this lattice is reasonably well-distributed, then one expects

\[ |B_K(M)| \approx \frac{(2M)^d}{\sqrt{D_K}}. \]

Writing the root discriminant as

\[ \operatorname{rd}(K):=D_K^{1/d}, \]

this becomes

\[ |B_K(M)| \approx \left(\frac{2M}{\sqrt{\operatorname{rd}(K)}}\right)^d. \]

For our application,

\[ M=2e^R. \]

So the desired upper bound

\[ |B_K(2e^R)|\le \exp(\mu d) \]

should follow from

\[ \mu > \log(4e^R)-\frac12\log\operatorname{rd}(K), \]

again assuming decent lattice shape.

Here is a precise version.

Lemma 6: Counting algebraic integers from a bounded fundamental domain

Let \(L\subset\mathbb R^d\) be a full-rank lattice with covolume \(\det L\). Suppose \(L\) has a measurable fundamental domain \(F\subset [-\eta,\eta]^d\). Then for every \(M>0\),

\[ |L\cap[-M,M]^d| \le \frac{(2(M+\eta))^d}{\det L}. \]

In particular, for \(L=\Sigma_K(\mathcal O_K)\),

\[ |B_K(M)| \le \frac{(2(M+\eta))^d}{\sqrt{D_K}}. \]

Proof

For every \(x\in L\cap[-M,M]^d\), the translate \(x+F\) lies inside

\[ [-M-\eta,M+\eta]^d. \]

The translates \(x+F\) are disjoint as \(x\) ranges over \(L\). Therefore,

\[ |L\cap[-M,M]^d|\det L \le (2(M+\eta))^d. \]

Dividing by \(\det L\) proves the claim. \(\square\)

Thus, if we can arrange

\[ \sqrt{D_K}\ge \exp\left(\frac r2 d\right) \]

with \(r=\log\operatorname{rd}(K)\), and if the Minkowski lattice has bounded shape, then

\[ |B_K(2e^R)| \lesssim \exp\left( \left[ \log(4e^R+O(1))-\frac r2 \right]d \right). \]

So we want

\[ \mu \approx \log(4e^R)-\frac12\log\operatorname{rd}(K) \]

to satisfy

\[ \mu<2\lambda. \]

7. Numerical feasibility of the plan

The inequalities have genuine room.

Suppose, optimistically but plausibly, that for a sequence of fields \(K_i\) we have:

\[ \mathcal R_{K_i}\le \exp(\rho d_i), \]

with \(\rho\approx 0.30\), and the unit lattice is regular enough that

\[ |U_{K_i}(R)| \approx \exp((\log(2R)-\rho)d_i). \]

Take \(R=4\). Then

\[ \log(2R)=\log 8\approx 2.079, \]

so

\[ \lambda\approx 2.079-0.30=1.779. \]

This exceeds

\[ \log 5\approx 1.609. \]

So the product-set condition is satisfied, because

\[ 1+\frac{\log 5}{\lambda} \approx 1+\frac{1.609}{1.779} \approx 1.904<2. \]

For the sumset, we need

\[ |B_K(2e^4)|\le \exp(\mu d) \]

with

\[ \mu<2\lambda\approx 3.558. \]

The naive volume/discriminant heuristic gives

\[ \mu \approx \log(4e^4)-\frac12\log\operatorname{rd}(K) = (4+\log 4)-\frac12\log\operatorname{rd}(K). \]

Since

\[ 4+\log 4\approx 5.386, \]

we need roughly

\[ 5.386-\frac12\log\operatorname{rd}(K)<3.558, \]

or

\[ \frac12\log\operatorname{rd}(K)>1.828, \]

i.e.

\[ \operatorname{rd}(K)>e^{3.656}\approx 38.7. \]

That is also a plausible range for high-degree totally real fields.

Thus the inequalities are numerically compatible. The heart of the matter is to construct fields where the regulator is small enough and both lattices have controlled shape.

8. Precise remaining gaps

The reduction above leaves a clear arithmetic problem. I separate it into several sub-gaps.

Gap 1: Many bounded units

We need infinitely many totally real fields \(K_i\), \(d_i\to\infty\), and a fixed \(R>0\), such that

\[ |U_{K_i}(R)| \ge \exp((\lambda-o(1))d_i) \]

for some

\[ \lambda>\log 5. \]

A sufficient structural condition is:

  1. The unit regulator satisfies

    \[ \mathcal R_{K_i}\le \exp(\rho d_i) \]

    with

    \[ \rho<\log(2R)-\log 5. \]

  2. The unit log lattice \(\Lambda_{K_i}\) has bounded covering radius in \(H_{d_i}\) with respect to \(\|\cdot\|_\infty\), or at least enough regularity that

    \[ |\Lambda_{K_i}\cap C_R| \gg \frac{\operatorname{vol}(C_R)}{\mathcal R_{K_i}} \]

    exponentially sharply.

Assessment

This is a serious arithmetic construction problem, but it is the most plausible heart of the proof.

Merely having a small regulator is not quite enough: the unit lattice could be extremely skew, in which case the box \(C_R\) might contain far fewer points than its volume suggests. So the construction must control the shape of the unit lattice, not just its covolume.

Possible routes:

  • Use class field towers with many independent relative units of bounded height.
  • Construct totally real Galois fields with many “flat” units whose logarithmic embeddings behave like a random or well-rounded lattice.
  • Use arithmetic torus/Cartan-lattice constructions to realize well-rounded log-unit lattices.

This is plausibly closeable but nontrivial.

Gap 2: Few bounded algebraic integers

We need

\[ |B_{K_i}(2e^R)| \le \exp((\mu+o(1))d_i) \]

for some

\[ \mu<2\lambda. \]

A sufficient condition is:

  1. The root discriminant is large enough:

    \[ \operatorname{rd}(K_i)\ge e^r \]

    with

    \[ \log(4e^R)-\frac r2<2\lambda. \]

  2. The Minkowski lattice \(\Sigma_K(\mathcal O_K)\) is not too skew, so that the volume estimate

    \[ |B_K(2e^R)| \lesssim \frac{(4e^R)^d}{\sqrt{D_K}} \]

    is exponentially accurate.

Assessment

This is also serious. A large discriminant alone does not automatically give a sharp upper bound for lattice points in a box if the lattice is very skew. One needs shape control.

However, this gap is somewhat orthogonal to Gap 1: the unit lattice lives in logarithmic space, while the Minkowski lattice lives in additive archimedean space. The construction must balance them.

The desired inequality allows

\[ |B_K(2e^R)| \]

to be exponentially larger than

\[ |U_K(R)| \]

as long as the exponent is \(<2\lambda\). So the additive lattice does not need to be extremely sparse; it just cannot be too dense.

Gap 3: Product-set control

This gap is essentially closed by Lemma 2.

We proved unconditionally that

\[ |U_K(2R)| \le 5^{d-1}|U_K(R)|. \]

Thus, if

\[ |U_K(R)|\ge e^{\lambda d} \]

with

\[ \lambda>\log 5, \]

then

\[ |U_K(2R)| \le |U_K(R)|^{1+\log 5/\lambda+o(1)} = |U_K(R)|^{2-\delta_1} \]

for

\[ \delta_1<1-\frac{\log 5}{\lambda}. \]

This is enough. If one proves additional regularity of the unit lattice, one can likely replace \(5\) by \(2+o(1)\), improving constants, but that is not necessary for the reduction.

Gap 4: Injectivity into \(\mathbb R\)

No gap here. Once \(K\) is totally real, any embedding

\[ \sigma:K\hookrightarrow\mathbb R \]

is injective, so cardinalities are preserved when passing from algebraic units to real numbers.

9. Step-by-step proof plan from here

Here is the full proof plan in modular form.

Step 1: Choose numerical constants

Choose constants \(R,\lambda,\mu\) such that

\[ \lambda>\log 5, \qquad \mu<2\lambda. \]

A concrete target is something like:

\[ R=4,\qquad \lambda\approx 1.75,\qquad \mu\approx 3.3. \]

These numbers would give a positive \(\delta\), for example

\[ \delta < \min\left\{ 1-\frac{\log 5}{1.75}, \, 2-\frac{3.3}{1.75} \right\} \approx \min\{0.0806,0.1143\}. \]

So even weak margins suffice.

Step 2: Construct totally real fields with many bounded units

Prove that there are totally real fields \(K_i\), \(d_i\to\infty\), such that

\[ |U_{K_i}(R)| \ge \exp((\lambda-o(1))d_i). \]

A sufficient route is to prove:

  • the regulators satisfy
\[ \mathcal R_{K_i}\le \exp(\rho d_i) \]

with

\[ \rho<\log(2R)-\lambda; \]
  • the unit lattices are well-rounded enough that the box \(C_R\) contains the expected number of lattice points.

This is the central arithmetic construction.

Step 3: Prove the bounded-integer estimate

For the same fields, prove

\[ |B_{K_i}(2e^R)| \le \exp((\mu+o(1))d_i). \]

A sufficient route is to show:

  • the root discriminants satisfy
\[ \operatorname{rd}(K_i)\ge e^r \]

with

\[ \log(4e^R)-\frac r2<\mu; \]
  • the Minkowski lattices have controlled shape, so Lemma 6 gives an exponentially sharp upper bound.

Step 4: Define the sets \(A_i\)

Fix an embedding

\[ \sigma_i:K_i\hookrightarrow\mathbb R, \]

and define

\[ A_i:=\sigma_i(U_{K_i}(R)). \]

Then

\[ |A_i|=|U_{K_i}(R)|\to\infty. \]

Step 5: Bound the sumset

By Lemma 1,

\[ A_i+A_i \subseteq \sigma_i(B_{K_i}(2e^R)). \]

Therefore,

\[ |A_i+A_i| \le |B_{K_i}(2e^R)| \le \exp((\mu+o(1))d_i). \]

Since

\[ |A_i|\ge \exp((\lambda-o(1))d_i), \]

we get

\[ |A_i+A_i| \le |A_i|^{\mu/\lambda+o(1)} \le |A_i|^{2-\delta} \]

for every

\[ \delta<2-\frac{\mu}{\lambda}. \]

Step 6: Bound the product set

By Lemma 1,

\[ A_iA_i \subseteq \sigma_i(U_{K_i}(2R)). \]

By Corollary 3,

\[ |U_{K_i}(2R)| \le 5^{d_i-1}|U_{K_i}(R)|. \]

Thus

\[ |A_iA_i| \le 5^{d_i}|A_i|. \]

Since

\[ |A_i|\ge \exp((\lambda-o(1))d_i), \]

we have

\[ 5^{d_i} \le |A_i|^{\log 5/\lambda+o(1)}. \]

Hence

\[ |A_iA_i| \le |A_i|^{1+\log 5/\lambda+o(1)} \le |A_i|^{2-\delta} \]

for every

\[ \delta<1-\frac{\log 5}{\lambda}. \]

Step 7: Choose \(\delta\)

Take

\[ 0<\delta< \min\left\{ 1-\frac{\log 5}{\lambda}, \, 2-\frac{\mu}{\lambda} \right\}. \]

Then for all sufficiently large \(i\),

\[ \max(|A_i+A_i|,|A_iA_i|) \le |A_i|^{2-\delta}. \]

Discarding finitely many initial terms gives the desired infinite sequence.

10. Why this approach seems more promising than standard constructions

It is useful to contrast the number-field-unit strategy with more classical attempts.

10.1. Smooth integers do not seem to give a fixed power saving

Let \(A\) be the set of \(y\)-smooth integers up to \(X\). Then

\[ |A+A|\le 2X, \]

so the sumset is small if \(A\) is dense enough in \([1,X]\). Also,

\[ AA\subseteq \{y\text{-smooth integers }\le X^2\}. \]

However, standard Dickman–de Bruijn asymptotics show that whenever

\[ |A|=X^{\alpha+o(1)} \]

for fixed \(\alpha>0\), one typically has

\[ |AA|=X^{2\alpha+o(1)} = |A|^{2+o(1)}. \]

Thus smooth-number constructions naturally give only subpolynomial savings, not a fixed power saving.

10.2. One-generator algebraic digit sets also hit a square barrier

Take a fixed algebraic number \(\theta\) and sets of the form

\[ A_n= \left\{ \sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\varepsilon_j\theta^j: \varepsilon_j\in\{0,1\} \right\}. \]

Then

\[ A_n+A_n \]

has digit set \(\{0,1,2\}\), so addition is controlled. But products have digit convolutions of length roughly \(2n\). The growth of the number of distinct values is governed by the Mahler measure of \(\theta\), and the product set naturally has growth roughly the square of the growth of \(A_n\). Without additional structure, this does not yield a fixed \(\delta>0\).

10.3. Finite-field or CRT lifting has a carry-volume obstruction

One might try to lift a finite field or finite ring into real numbers through representatives modulo an ideal. Modulo the ideal, addition and multiplication are small. But exact real sums and products include “carry” information. In one dimension this is precisely why representatives of \(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z\) in \([0,q)\) have product set essentially quadratic. In high-dimensional number fields, one can reduce the carry problem using geometry of numbers, but then one is led back to the same issue: one needs many small representatives closed under multiplication. Units provide exactly that mechanism.

\[ \frac12 C-\frac12 C=C. \]

Lemma 6: Coordinatewise cube collisions cannot be realized in a field

Let \(F\) be an integral domain of characteristic \(0\), and let \(r\ge 2\). Suppose there is an injective map

\[ \phi:\{0,1\}^r\to F \]

with the following two properties.

  1. Whenever

    \[ \varepsilon+\eta=\varepsilon'+\eta' \]

    coordinatewise as vectors in \(\{0,1,2\}^r\), one has

    \[ \phi(\varepsilon)+\phi(\eta) = \phi(\varepsilon')+\phi(\eta'). \]

    \[ \varepsilon\eta=\varepsilon'\eta' \]

    coordinatewise as vectors in \(\{0,1\}^r\), one has

    \[ \phi(\varepsilon)\phi(\eta) = \phi(\varepsilon')\phi(\eta'). \]

Then no such injective \(\phi\) exists.

Proof

Let \(0\in\{0,1\}^r\) denote the zero vector, and let \(e_i\) be the \(i\)-th standard basis vector.

The additive relations imply that \(\phi\) is affine-linear on the cube.

Indeed, set

\[ c:=\phi(0), \qquad a_i:=\phi(e_i)-c. \]

If \(S\subseteq\{1,\dots,r\}\), write \(1_S\in\{0,1\}^r\) for its indicator vector.

We claim

\[ \phi(1_S)=c+\sum_{i\in S}a_i. \]

This follows by induction on \(|S|\). If \(i\in S\), then coordinatewise

\[ 1_S+0=1_{S\setminus\{i\}}+e_i. \]

Therefore,

\[ \phi(1_S)+\phi(0) = \phi(1_{S\setminus\{i\}})+\phi(e_i). \]

Using the induction hypothesis,

\[ \phi(1_S)+c = \left(c+\sum_{j\in S\setminus\{i\}}a_j\right)+(c+a_i), \]

so

\[ \phi(1_S)=c+\sum_{j\in S}a_j. \]

Now use the multiplicative collision condition.

For every \(\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^r\),

\[ \varepsilon\cdot 0=0. \]

Thus

\[ \phi(\varepsilon)\phi(0)=\phi(0)\phi(0). \]

So

\[ \phi(\varepsilon)c=c^2 \]

for every \(\varepsilon\).

If \(c\ne 0\), then since \(F\) is an integral domain,

\[ \phi(\varepsilon)=c \]

for every \(\varepsilon\), contradicting injectivity.

Hence \(c=0\).

Now for distinct \(i,j\), we have

\[ e_i\cdot e_j=0. \]

Therefore,

\[ \phi(e_i)\phi(e_j)=\phi(0)\phi(0)=0. \]

Since \(c=0\), \(\phi(e_i)=a_i\). Hence

\[ a_i a_j=0 \]

for all \(i\ne j\). Because \(F\) is an integral domain, at most one of the \(a_i\) is nonzero. But if \(r\ge 2\), this makes \(\phi\) non-injective on the cube.

Contradiction. \(\square\)

So the most naive tensoring of constant-factor savings cannot work in \(\mathbb R\).

\[ p x_1=0. \]

\[ x_1=0. \]

\[ x_a=0 \]

2. Valid product-set estimate

Let

\[ H_d=\left\{(x_1,\dots,x_d)\in\mathbb R^d:\sum_j x_j=0\right\}, \]

and let

\[ \ell:\mathcal O_K^\times\to H_d \]

be the logarithmic embedding

\[ \ell(u)= (\log|\sigma_1(u)|,\dots,\log|\sigma_d(u)|). \]

The image

\[ \Lambda_K=\ell(\mathcal O_K^\times) \]

is a lattice of rank \(d-1\), and

\[ \ker \ell=\{\pm1\}. \]

Let

\[ C_R=H_d\cap[-R,R]^d. \]

Then

\[ |U_K(R)|=2|\Lambda_K\cap C_R|. \]

Lemma 2

Let \(\Lambda\) be a rank \(r\) lattice in a real vector space, and let \(C\) be centrally symmetric and convex. Then

\[ |\Lambda\cap 2C|\le 5^r|\Lambda\cap C|. \]

Proof

Let

\[ S=\Lambda\cap 2C. \]

Choose a maximal subset \(Y\subseteq S\) such that for distinct \(y,y'\in Y\),

\[ y-y'\notin C. \]

Then the translates \(y+\frac12 C\), \(y\in Y\), are disjoint. They all lie in \(\frac52C\). Therefore

\[ |Y|\operatorname{vol}\left(\frac12C\right) \le \operatorname{vol}\left(\frac52C\right), \]

so

\[ |Y|\le 5^r. \]

By maximality of \(Y\), every \(s\in S\) satisfies \(s-y\in C\) for some \(y\in Y\). Hence

\[ S\subseteq Y+(\Lambda\cap C). \]

Thus

\[ |\Lambda\cap 2C| \le |Y|\,|\Lambda\cap C| \le 5^r|\Lambda\cap C|. \]

\(\square\)

Corollary 3

For every totally real \(K\) of degree \(d\),

\[ |U_K(2R)|\le 5^{d-1}|U_K(R)|. \]

Proof

Since \(C_{2R}=2C_R\),

\[ |U_K(2R)| = 2|\Lambda_K\cap C_{2R}| = 2|\Lambda_K\cap 2C_R|. \]

By Lemma 2,

\[ |\Lambda_K\cap 2C_R| \le 5^{d-1}|\Lambda_K\cap C_R|. \]

Therefore

\[ |U_K(2R)| \le 5^{d-1}|U_K(R)|. \]

\(\square\)

3. Correct conditional theorem

The previous argument proves the following conditional statement.

Proposition

Suppose there exist constants

\[ R>0,\qquad \lambda>\log 5,\qquad \mu<2\lambda, \]

and totally real fields \(K_i\) with \(d_i=[K_i:\mathbb Q]\to\infty\), such that

\[ |U_{K_i}(R)|\ge \exp((\lambda-o(1))d_i), \tag{1} \]

and

\[ |B_{K_i}(2e^R)|\le \exp((\mu+o(1))d_i). \tag{2} \]

Then there are finite sets \(A_i\subset\mathbb R\), \(|A_i|\to\infty\), and some \(\delta>0\), such that

\[ \max(|A_i+A_i|,|A_iA_i|) \le |A_i|^{2-\delta}. \]

Proof

Let

\[ A_i=\sigma_i(U_{K_i}(R)). \]

Then

\[ |A_i|=|U_{K_i}(R)| \ge \exp((\lambda-o(1))d_i), \]

so \(|A_i|\to\infty\).

For the sumset, Lemma 1 gives

\[ |A_i+A_i| \le |B_{K_i}(2e^R)| \le \exp((\mu+o(1))d_i). \]

Since

\[ |A_i|\ge \exp((\lambda-o(1))d_i), \]

we get

\[ |A_i+A_i| \le |A_i|^{\mu/\lambda+o(1)}. \]

Because \(\mu<2\lambda\), any

\[ \delta_1<2-\frac{\mu}{\lambda} \]

works for the sumset.

For the product set, Lemma 1 and Corollary 3 imply

\[ |A_iA_i| \le |U_{K_i}(2R)| \le 5^{d_i-1}|U_{K_i}(R)| = 5^{d_i-1}|A_i|. \]

Using \(|A_i|\ge \exp((\lambda-o(1))d_i)\),

\[ 5^{d_i} \le |A_i|^{\log 5/\lambda+o(1)}. \]

Therefore

\[ |A_iA_i| \le |A_i|^{1+\log 5/\lambda+o(1)}. \]

Since \(\lambda>\log 5\), any

\[ \delta_2<1-\frac{\log 5}{\lambda} \]

works for the product set.

Choose

\[ 0<\delta< \min\left\{ 2-\frac{\mu}{\lambda}, 1-\frac{\log 5}{\lambda} \right\}. \]

Then for all sufficiently large \(i\),

\[ \max(|A_i+A_i|,|A_iA_i|) \le |A_i|^{2-\delta}. \]

\(\square\)

4. The fatal missing step

To turn this into a proof of the stated theorem, one must prove the following strong arithmetic construction.

Missing Arithmetic Theorem

There exist constants

\[ R>0,\qquad \lambda>\log 5,\qquad \mu<2\lambda, \]

and infinitely many totally real fields \(K_i\), \(d_i\to\infty\), such that

\[ |U_{K_i}(R)|\ge \exp((\lambda-o(1))d_i), \]

and

\[ |B_{K_i}(2e^R)|\le \exp((\mu+o(1))d_i). \]

The previous proof did not establish this.

This is not a minor omission. It requires constructing totally real fields with simultaneously:

  1. exponentially many uniformly bounded units; and
  2. few uniformly bounded algebraic integers.

Those two conditions pull in opposite directions and cannot be obtained from regulator/discriminant estimates alone.

5. Why the previous heuristics are insufficient

5.1. Large discriminant alone does not control \(B_K(M)\)

The Minkowski lattice

\[ \Sigma_K(\mathcal O_K) = \{(\sigma_1(x),\dots,\sigma_d(x)):x\in\mathcal O_K\} \]

has covolume \(\sqrt{D_K}\). A naive estimate would be

\[ |B_K(M)| \approx \frac{(2M)^d}{\sqrt{D_K}}. \]

But determinant alone does not control lattice points in boxes. For example,

\[ L_T=\mathbb Z^{d-1}\oplus T\mathbb Z \]

has determinant \(T\), but for fixed \(M\ge1\) and \(T>M\),

\[ |L_T\cap[-M,M]^d| = (2\lfloor M\rfloor+1)^{d-1}, \]

independent of \(T\).

So a discriminant lower bound is useless without shape control.

5.2. There is a universal packing bound, but it is too weak

One can prove the following.

Lemma

For every totally real field \(K\) and every \(M>0\),

\[ |B_K(M)|\le (\lfloor 2M\rfloor+1)^d. \]

Proof

Partition \([-M,M]^d\) into half-open cubes of side length \(<1\). If two distinct algebraic integers \(x,y\in B_K(M)\) lie in the same cube, then for every \(j\),

\[ |\sigma_j(x-y)|<1. \]

But \(x-y\in\mathcal O_K\setminus\{0\}\), so

\[ 0\ne N_{K/\mathbb Q}(x-y)\in\mathbb Z. \]

On the other hand,

\[ |N_{K/\mathbb Q}(x-y)| = \prod_{j=1}^d |\sigma_j(x-y)| <1, \]

impossible. Hence each cube contains at most one point. The number of cubes is at most \((\lfloor2M\rfloor+1)^d\). \(\square\)

For \(M=2e^R\), this gives roughly

\[ |B_K(2e^R)| \le \exp((R+O(1))d). \]

This is far too weak for the reduction, because even an optimally dense unit lattice would give at most roughly

\[ |U_K(R)|\approx \exp((\log R+O(1))d), \]

and \(R\) dominates \(\log R\).

Thus the proof genuinely needs discriminant and shape information, not just packing.

5.3. Exact tensor-product constructions cannot live in a field

Another tempting idea is to imitate the coordinatewise cube \(\{0,1\}^d\), whose coordinatewise sumset has size \(3^d\) and coordinatewise product set has size \(2^d\). But this cannot be embedded exactly into a field.

Already a \(2\)-coordinate, \(2\)-digit obstruction appears.

Suppose four field elements \(x_{00},x_{01},x_{10},x_{11}\) satisfied the coordinatewise relations

\[ x_{00}+x_{11}=x_{01}+x_{10} \]

and

\[ x_{00}x_{11}=x_{01}x_{10}. \]

Then the unordered pairs \(\{x_{00},x_{11}\}\) and \(\{x_{01},x_{10}\}\) have the same sum and product. Hence they are the same pair of roots of the same quadratic polynomial. Thus

\[ \{x_{00},x_{11}\}=\{x_{01},x_{10}\}, \]

contradicting injectivity.

So the most obvious tensoring mechanism is algebraically impossible in an integral domain.

6. Conclusion

The earlier argument proves only the conditional proposition above. It does not prove the stated theorem.

To obtain a complete proof, one must supply a new arithmetic construction proving the Missing Arithmetic Theorem: infinitely many totally real fields with exponentially many bounded units and sufficiently few bounded algebraic integers.

Without that construction, the proof cannot be completed. Any purported complete proof must explicitly establish that arithmetic input or replace the number-field-unit strategy with a different construction that gives the same two estimates.
