0. Why the standard integer/smooth-number route is not enough

A very natural idea is:

  1. choose \(A\subset \{1,\dots,N\}\), so \(|A+A|\le 2N\);
  2. make \(A\) multiplicatively structured, so \(|AA|\) is small;
  3. take \(N\le |A|^{2-\delta}\).

This is how the classical Erdős–Szemerédi examples with

\[ \max(|A+A|,|AA|)\le |A|^{2-o(1)} \]

are built, using smooth numbers. But this route cannot produce a fixed \(\delta>0\).

Lemma 0.1: divisor obstruction for integer intervals

Let \(A\subset \{1,\dots,N\}\) with \(|A|=n\). Then

\[ |AA|\ge \frac{n^2}{\exp(O(\log N/\log\log N))}. \]

In particular, if \(N\le n^C\) for some fixed \(C\), then

\[ |AA|\ge n^{2-o(1)}. \]

Proof

For each integer \(m\), let

\[ r(m)=|\{(a,b)\in A^2:ab=m\}|. \]

Then

\[ \sum_m r(m)=n^2. \]

Also, for \(m\le N^2\), the number of ordered factorisations \(m=ab\) with \(a,b\in\{1,\dots,N\}\) is at most the divisor function \(\tau(m)\), up to a harmless factor. The standard divisor bound gives

\[ \tau(m)\le \exp(O(\log m/\log\log m)) \le \exp(O(\log N/\log\log N)). \]

Therefore

\[ n^2=\sum_{m\in AA} r(m) \le |AA|\exp(O(\log N/\log\log N)), \]

which proves the claim. \(\square\)

So if additive smallness comes merely from \(A\subset [N]\) with \(N\le n^{2-\delta}\), then the product set still satisfies \(|AA|\ge n^{2-o(1)}\), which is too large for a fixed-power counterexample.

This is why I think the successful construction must leave the ordinary integer lattice and use algebraic-number “carry” or residue-ring structure.

1. Most promising approach: lift a finite ring model into a totally real number field

Finite fields give the ideal model: if \(F\) is a finite field, then \(F+F=F\) and \(FF=F\). The obstruction is that no finite field embeds into \(\mathbb R\).

The idea is to take a totally real number field \(K\), reduce \(\mathcal O_K\) modulo an integer \(q\), use a highly structured subset \(B\subset \mathcal O_K/q\mathcal O_K\), and choose small real/algebraic lifts of the elements of \(B\). If the lifts are small enough, then equality modulo \(q\) forces equality over \(K\), or at least gives only boundedly many archimedean carries.

The real set will be

\[ A=\{\sigma_1(x_b): b\in B\}\subset \mathbb R, \]

where \(\sigma_1:K\hookrightarrow \mathbb R\) is one fixed real embedding and \(x_b\in\mathcal O_K\) is a chosen lift of \(b\).

The finite quotient \(B\) controls the number of possible residues of sums and products. The archimedean size of the lifts controls how many different algebraic integers can occur in one residue class.

2. Basic counting in totally real fields

Let \(K\) be a totally real number field of degree \(d\). Let

\[ \Sigma=\{\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_d\} \]

be its real embeddings. For \(\alpha\in K\), define

\[ \|\alpha\|_\infty:=\max_{1\le j\le d}|\sigma_j(\alpha)|. \]

We will repeatedly use the following elementary packing lemma.

Lemma 2.1: algebraic-integer packing

For \(T\ge 0\), define

\[ P_K(T):= \sup_{y\in \mathbb R^d} \#\left\{ \alpha\in \mathcal O_K: \max_{1\le j\le d}|\sigma_j(\alpha)-y_j|\le T \right\}. \]

Then

\[ P_K(T)\le (2T+1)^d. \]

In particular, if \(T<1/2\), then \(P_K(T)=1\).

Proof

If \(\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal O_K\) are distinct, then \(\alpha-\beta\neq 0\), so

\[ N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\alpha-\beta)\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}. \]

Therefore

\[ \prod_{j=1}^d |\sigma_j(\alpha-\beta)| = |N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\alpha-\beta)| \ge 1. \]

Hence

\[ \max_j |\sigma_j(\alpha-\beta)|\ge 1. \]

So the embedded lattice \(\Sigma(\mathcal O_K)\subset \mathbb R^d\) is \(1\)-separated in the \(\ell^\infty\)-metric.

Now take any translate \(y+[-T,T]^d\). Around each point of \(\Sigma(\mathcal O_K)\) in this box, put an \(\ell^\infty\)-ball of radius \(1/2\). These balls are disjoint and all lie inside

\[ y+[-T-1/2,T+1/2]^d, \]

whose volume is \((2T+1)^d\). Each small ball has volume \(1\). Thus there are at most \((2T+1)^d\) points.

If \(T<1/2\), the box has \(\ell^\infty\)-diameter \(2T<1\), so it contains at most one point. \(\square\)

3. The finite-ring lifting criterion

Let \(q\ge 2\) be an integer. Write

\[ \rho_q:\mathcal O_K\to \mathcal O_K/q\mathcal O_K \]

for reduction modulo \(q\).

Let \(B\subset \mathcal O_K/q\mathcal O_K\) be finite. Suppose we choose a lift

\[ s:B\to \mathcal O_K,\qquad \rho_q(s(b))=b, \]

with uniform archimedean bound

\[ \|s(b)\|_\infty\le H \qquad\text{for all }b\in B. \]

Define

\[ A:=\{\sigma_1(s(b)):b\in B\}\subset \mathbb R. \]

Because \(\sigma_1\) is injective on \(K\), distinct lifts give distinct real numbers. Since the residues \(b\in B\) are distinct, the lifts \(s(b)\) are distinct, so

\[ |A|=|B|. \]

We now bound \(|A+A|\) and \(|AA|\).

Proposition 3.1: lifting criterion

With notation as above,

\[ |A+A| \le |B+B|\,P_K(2H/q), \]

and

\[ |AA| \le |BB|\,P_K(H^2/q), \]

where \(B+B\) and \(BB\) are computed in the finite ring \(\mathcal O_K/q\mathcal O_K\).

Proof

First consider sums. For \(b_1,b_2\in B\),

\[ \rho_q(s(b_1)+s(b_2))=b_1+b_2. \]

Thus the residue of a sum lies in \(B+B\). Fix one residue \(r\in B+B\). We count how many algebraic integers of the form

\[ s(b_1)+s(b_2) \]

can have residue \(r\).

All such sums lie in one coset of \(q\mathcal O_K\). Divide by \(q\). The corresponding points lie in one translate of \(\mathcal O_K\), and their archimedean coordinates have absolute value at most

\[ \frac{2H}{q}. \]

Therefore, by the definition of \(P_K\), there are at most \(P_K(2H/q)\) such sums for each residue \(r\). Hence

\[ |A+A|\le |B+B|P_K(2H/q). \]

The product bound is analogous. For \(b_1,b_2\in B\),

\[ \rho_q(s(b_1)s(b_2))=b_1b_2. \]

The residue lies in \(BB\). For a fixed residue \(r\in BB\), all products with that residue lie in one coset of \(q\mathcal O_K\), and after dividing by \(q\), their archimedean coordinates have absolute value at most

\[ \frac{H^2}{q}. \]

So there are at most \(P_K(H^2/q)\) products per residue. Therefore

\[ |AA|\le |BB|P_K(H^2/q). \]

\(\square\)

4. Immediate consequence: sub-square lifts would finish the theorem

Suppose one could arrange a finite quotient model \(B\) with small residue sum/product and lifts with

\[ H<\min(q/4,\sqrt{q}/2). \]

Then

\[ 2H/q<1/2, \qquad H^2/q<1/2, \]

so by Lemma 2.1,

\[ P_K(2H/q)=P_K(H^2/q)=1. \]

Hence Proposition 3.1 gives

\[ |A+A|\le |B+B|, \qquad |AA|\le |BB|. \]

Therefore, if \(B\) is itself a finite subring of \(\mathcal O_K/q\mathcal O_K\), then

\[ B+B=B,\qquad BB\subseteq B, \]

and we get the extremely strong bound

\[ |A+A|,\ |AA|\le |A|. \]

So one route to the theorem would be:

Find finite subrings \(B_i\subset \mathcal O_{K_i}/q_i\mathcal O_{K_i}\) with \(|B_i|\to\infty\), and lift each element \(b\in B_i\) to \(x_b\in\mathcal O_{K_i}\) with \(\|x_b\|_\infty<q_i^{1/2-o(1)}\).

This is an idealized target. The serious difficulty is that \(q^{1/2}\) is a natural barrier for idempotent-like residue classes, as explained below.

5. A concrete borderline model: Boolean residue cubes

The cleanest structured residue model is the Boolean cube.

Assume \(q\) is an odd rational prime that splits completely in \(K\). Then

\[ \mathcal O_K/q\mathcal O_K\cong \mathbb F_q^d. \]

Let

\[ B=\{0,1\}^d\subset \mathbb F_q^d. \]

Then

\[ |B|=2^d, \]

and since \(q\) is odd,

\[ |B+B|=3^d, \]

because each coordinate sum is \(0,1,\) or \(2\). Also

\[ |BB|=2^d, \]

because coordinatewise products of \(0\)s and \(1\)s are again \(0\)s and \(1\)s.

Suppose we can lift every Boolean residue vector \(\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^d\) to an algebraic integer \(x_\varepsilon\in\mathcal O_K\) with

\[ \rho_q(x_\varepsilon)=\varepsilon, \qquad \|x_\varepsilon\|_\infty\le C\sqrt q \]

for some absolute constant \(C\).

Then Proposition 3.1 gives good bounds.

Corollary 5.1: Boolean lift criterion

Assume \(q>16C^2\), and assume that every Boolean residue class \(\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^d\) has a lift \(x_\varepsilon\in\mathcal O_K\) satisfying

\[ \|x_\varepsilon\|_\infty\le C\sqrt q. \]

Let

\[ A=\{\sigma_1(x_\varepsilon):\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^d\}. \]

Then

\[ |A|=2^d, \]
\[ |A+A|\le 3^d, \]

and

\[ |AA|\le 2^d\,P_K(C^2). \]

In particular, if for some \(\eta<1\) we have

\[ P_K(C^2)\le 2^{\eta d}, \]

then

\[ |A+A|\le |A|^{\log_2 3}, \]

and

\[ |AA|\le |A|^{1+\eta}. \]

Thus

\[ \max(|A+A|,|AA|) \le |A|^{2-\delta} \]

with

\[ \delta = \min\{2-\log_2 3,\ 1-\eta\}>0. \]

Proof

We have \(|B|=2^d\), \(|B+B|=3^d\), \(|BB|=2^d\).

Since \(H=C\sqrt q\),

\[ 2H/q=\frac{2C}{\sqrt q}<\frac12 \]

by the assumption \(q>16C^2\). Therefore

\[ P_K(2H/q)=1. \]

So Proposition 3.1 gives

\[ |A+A|\le 3^d. \]

For products,

\[ H^2/q=C^2, \]

so

\[ |AA|\le 2^dP_K(C^2). \]

If \(P_K(C^2)\le 2^{\eta d}\), then

\[ |AA|\le 2^{(1+\eta)d}=|A|^{1+\eta}. \]

The claimed \(\delta\) follows. \(\square\)

This is a sharp, concrete reduction.

6. The square-root barrier is real

The \(\sqrt q\) scale in the Boolean lifting criterion is not accidental. It is forced by a norm argument.

Lemma 6.1: norm lower bound for Boolean lifts

Let \(q\) split completely in \(K\), and suppose \(x\in\mathcal O_K\) reduces modulo \(q\mathcal O_K\) to a Boolean vector \(\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^d\) that is not identically \(0\) or identically \(1\). If

\[ \|x\|_\infty\le H, \]

then

\[ H(H+1)\ge q. \]

In particular,

\[ H\ge \frac{\sqrt{1+4q}-1}{2} = (1+o(1))\sqrt q. \]

Proof

Since the residue of \(x\) is coordinatewise \(0\) or \(1\), we have

\[ x(x-1)\equiv 0\pmod{q\mathcal O_K}. \]

Because \(\varepsilon\) is not identically \(0\) or \(1\), \(x(x-1)\neq 0\). Therefore

\[ q\mathcal O_K\mid x(x-1)\mathcal O_K, \]

so

\[ q^d=N(q\mathcal O_K)\mid |N_{K/\mathbb Q}(x(x-1))|. \]

Hence

\[ q^d \le \prod_{j=1}^d |\sigma_j(x)(\sigma_j(x)-1)|. \]

But

\[ |\sigma_j(x)(\sigma_j(x)-1)| \le H(H+1) \]

for every \(j\). Thus

\[ q^d\le (H(H+1))^d, \]

so \(H(H+1)\ge q\). \(\square\)

So the Boolean-lift route must operate at the borderline scale \(H\asymp \sqrt q\). This is why the product multiplicity term \(P_K(C^2)\) in Corollary 5.1 is unavoidable.

7. Precise remaining gaps in the Boolean-lift approach

The reduction above shows that the theorem follows from the following two statements.

Gap A: Square-root Boolean section

Find constants \(C>1\) and an infinite sequence of pairs \((K_i,q_i)\), where \(K_i\) is totally real of degree \(d_i\to\infty\) and \(q_i\) is an odd rational prime splitting completely in \(K_i\), such that every Boolean residue vector

\[ \varepsilon\in \{0,1\}^{d_i} \subset \mathcal O_{K_i}/q_i\mathcal O_{K_i} \]

has a lift \(x_\varepsilon\in\mathcal O_{K_i}\) satisfying

\[ \|x_\varepsilon\|_\infty\le C\sqrt{q_i}. \]

Assessment

This is the main construction problem. Lemma 6.1 shows that \(\sqrt q\) is the correct lower scale, so the target is optimally sharp up to constants.

Standard geometry of numbers only gives representatives of arbitrary residue classes modulo \(q\mathcal O_K\) of size \(O(q)\), not \(O(\sqrt q)\). However, we do not need all \(q^d\) residue classes; we only need the \(2^d\) Boolean classes. Counting does not rule this out, especially for fixed or moderately sized \(q\). But one needs a special arithmetic construction, not a generic lattice argument.

Possible tools to investigate:

  1. totally real towers where a fixed odd prime \(q\) splits completely;
  2. carefully chosen defining polynomials whose roots give small CRT idempotent lifts;
  3. trace-lattice or Voronoi-cell estimates for the image of the Boolean cube;
  4. class field tower methods to control discriminant and splitting simultaneously.

This is plausible but serious.

Gap B: Few small algebraic integers

For the Boolean lift criterion, we also need some \(\eta<1\) such that

\[ P_{K_i}(C^2)\le 2^{\eta d_i}. \]

The crude packing bound gives

\[ P_{K_i}(C^2)\le (2C^2+1)^{d_i}, \]

which is far too weak when \(C\ge 1\). We need the chosen fields to have substantially fewer algebraic integers of house \(\le C^2\) than the trivial packing estimate allows.

Assessment

This is also serious, but it is more flexible than Gap A. We do not need a uniform bound independent of \(d\); we only need the exponential base to be \(<2\). Equivalently, for some \(\eta<1\),

\[ \#\{\alpha\in\mathcal O_K:\|\alpha-y\|_\infty\le C^2\} \le 2^{\eta d} \]

uniformly over all translates \(y\).

Possible tools:

  1. lower bounds for norms of differences;
  2. trace-form arguments;
  3. excluding small-degree subfields with many low-house elements;
  4. potential-theoretic estimates for algebraic integers with all conjugates in a short interval;
  5. choosing \(K_i\) with large enough discriminant or suitable local restrictions to suppress low-house elements.

Gap B may be closable by choosing the fields in Gap A carefully.

Gap C: Compatibility of A and B

There is tension between Gaps A and B.

Gap A asks for many small representatives of Boolean residue classes. Gap B asks that there not be too many small algebraic integers in general. These demands are not contradictory, because Gap A asks for elements of size \(\asymp \sqrt q\), while Gap B concerns elements of bounded size \(C^2\), independent of \(q\). But in an actual construction one must make sure the mechanism producing the square-root lifts does not also create too many small differences/products.

8. A larger-alphabet variant

The Boolean cube is clean but has one drawback: \(|B|=2^d\), so the product multiplicity \(P_K(C^2)\) must have exponential base \(<2\).

One way to loosen this requirement is to use larger finite-field alphabets.

Suppose

\[ \mathcal O_K/q\mathcal O_K \]

contains a product of finite fields

\[ \mathbb F_m^g \]

as a subring, and take

\[ B=\mathbb F_m^g. \]

Then

\[ |B|=m^g, \qquad B+B=B, \qquad BB=B. \]

If we can lift every \(b\in B\) to \(x_b\in\mathcal O_K\) of height \(H\), Proposition 3.1 gives

\[ |A+A|\le |B|P_K(2H/q), \]
\[ |AA|\le |B|P_K(H^2/q). \]

Thus even if

\[ P_K(H^2/q)\le M^g \]

for some \(M>2\), we can absorb this if \(m\gg M\). In that case

\[ |AA|\le (mM)^g=|A|^{1+\log_m M}, \]

which is \(<|A|^{2-\delta}\) as soon as \(M<m^{1-\delta}\).

So an alternative precise target is:

Construct residue subrings \(B_i\cong \mathbb F_{m_i}^{g_i}\) with \(m_i\to\infty\), and lift them with \(H_i\lesssim \sqrt{q_i}\), while keeping \(P_{K_i}(H_i^2/q_i)\le m_i^{\eta g_i}\) for some \(\eta<1\).

This may be more robust than the Boolean model because the finite-field alphabet can absorb some bounded archimedean carry.

The obstruction is that lifting a whole subring \(\mathbb F_m^g\) at square-root scale is harder than lifting Boolean residues. Still, this is a natural next variant if the Boolean multiplicity bound is too strong.

9. Step-by-step proof plan from here

The approach can now be organized as follows.

Step 1: Choose a finite quotient model

Pick one of the following residue models.

Option 1: Boolean cube

Use

\[ B=\{0,1\}^d\subset \mathbb F_q^d. \]

Then

\[ |B|=2^d, \qquad |B+B|=3^d, \qquad |BB|=2^d. \]

This gives an additive exponent \(\log_2 3<2\) automatically.

Option 2: finite-field alphabet

Use

\[ B=\mathbb F_m^g \]

inside a residue quotient. Then

\[ |B+B|,|BB|\le |B|. \]

This is stronger, but constructing small lifts is harder.

Step 2: Build totally real fields with the right residue quotient

For the Boolean model, we need totally real \(K\) of degree \(d\) with an odd prime \(q\) splitting completely:

\[ \mathcal O_K/q\mathcal O_K\cong \mathbb F_q^d. \]

For the larger-alphabet model, we need a quotient containing a product of fields

\[ \mathbb F_m^g. \]

Possible sources:

  1. totally real class field towers with prescribed splitting;
  2. Hilbert irreducibility constructions forcing a prime to split completely;
  3. iterated extensions in which \(q\) remains totally split;
  4. explicit polynomial families with controlled real roots and prescribed factorization mod \(q\).

Step 3: Prove a small-section theorem

For the Boolean model, prove:

There is an absolute \(C\) such that every Boolean residue vector has a lift \(x_\varepsilon\) with

\[ \|x_\varepsilon\|_\infty\le C\sqrt q. \]

This is the main missing construction.

The norm lower bound in Lemma 6.1 shows this is optimal up to constants.

Step 4: Bound small archimedean carries

Prove that for the same fields,

\[ P_K(C^2)\le 2^{\eta d} \]

for some \(\eta<1\).

For the larger-alphabet model, prove instead something like

\[ P_K(H^2/q)\le |B|^{\eta} \]

with \(\eta<1\).

Step 5: Apply Proposition 3.1

Once Steps 1–4 are done, define

\[ A=\{\sigma_1(x_b):b\in B\}. \]

Then Proposition 3.1 gives

\[ |A+A| \le |B+B|P_K(2H/q), \]
\[ |AA| \le |BB|P_K(H^2/q). \]

In the Boolean case with \(H=C\sqrt q\), \(q\) large, and \(P_K(C^2)\le 2^{\eta d}\), we get

\[ |A|=2^d, \]
\[ |A+A|\le 3^d=|A|^{\log_2 3}, \]
\[ |AA|\le 2^{(1+\eta)d}=|A|^{1+\eta}. \]

Therefore

\[ \max(|A+A|,|AA|) \le |A|^{2-\delta}, \]

where

\[ \delta = \min\{2-\log_2 3,\ 1-\eta\}>0. \]

This gives the desired theorem.

Assistant

We prove the theorem by a different route from the finite-ring lifting approach sketched earlier.

The construction uses two facts from algebraic number theory:

  1. There are totally real number fields of arbitrarily large degree and bounded root discriminant.
  2. In such fields, the unit lattice has exponentially many units in a fixed archimedean box.

The rough idea is this.

Let \(K/\mathbb Q\) be totally real of degree \(d\), with embeddings

\[ \sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_d:K\hookrightarrow \mathbb R. \]

We construct a finite set

\[ \mathcal A\subset \mathcal O_K \]

and then project it to the real line by one embedding:

\[ A=\sigma_1(\mathcal A)\subset \mathbb R. \]

The set \(\mathcal A\) will have the form

\[ \mathcal A=U S, \]

where:

  • \(U\) is a large set of units of \(\mathcal O_K\) lying in a bounded logarithmic box;
  • \(S\) is a carefully chosen sparse random subset of a bounded archimedean box.

The additive smallness comes from the fact that all elements of \(US\) lie in a bounded box in all \(d\) real embeddings. Hence \(US+US\) also lies in a bounded box, which contains at most exponentially many algebraic integers.

The multiplicative smallness comes from the factorization

\[ (US)(US)\subset (UU)(SS). \]

The unit set \(U\) has small multiplicative doubling, so \(|UU|\ll^d |U|\), while \(|SS|\le |S|^2\). If \(|US|\approx |U||S|\), this gives a saving by a factor of roughly \(|U|\) in the product set.

The only delicate point is to choose \(S\) so that the products \(us\), with \(u\in U\) and \(s\in S\), are mostly distinct. We do this by a random selection argument.

1. Standard number-theoretic input

We use the following standard facts.

Theorem 1.1: bounded totally real root-discriminant towers

There is an absolute constant \(\Delta>1\) and an infinite sequence of totally real number fields

\[ K_i/\mathbb Q \]

with degrees

\[ d_i=[K_i:\mathbb Q]\to\infty \]

such that

\[ \operatorname{rd}(K_i):=|D_{K_i}|^{1/d_i}\le \Delta \]

for all \(i\).

This is a classical consequence of the Golod–Shafarevich class field tower method; explicit such towers were constructed by Martinet.

We also need a regulator bound.

Lemma 1.2: regulator bound in bounded-root-discriminant families

There is a constant \(C_{\mathrm{reg}}>1\), depending only on \(\Delta\), such that for every totally real field \(K\) of degree \(d\) with

\[ \operatorname{rd}(K)\le \Delta, \]

the regulator \(R_K\) satisfies

\[ R_K\le C_{\mathrm{reg}}^d. \]

Proof

We recall the standard argument.

For number fields with

\[ |D_K|^{1/d}\le \Delta, \]

standard residue bounds for Dedekind zeta-functions, for example the Louboutin bound, imply

\[ \operatorname*{Res}_{s=1}\zeta_K(s)\le C_0^d \]

for some constant \(C_0=C_0(\Delta)\).

Since \(K\) is totally real of degree \(d\), the analytic class number formula gives

\[ \operatorname*{Res}_{s=1}\zeta_K(s) = \frac{2^d h_K R_K}{2\sqrt{|D_K|}} = \frac{2^{d-1}h_KR_K}{\sqrt{|D_K|}}. \]

Thus

\[ R_K = \frac{\sqrt{|D_K|}}{2^{d-1}h_K} \operatorname*{Res}_{s=1}\zeta_K(s) \le 2\left(\frac{\sqrt\Delta}{2}\right)^d C_0^d. \]

Since \(h_K\ge1\), this gives

\[ R_K\le C_{\mathrm{reg}}^d \]

for a suitable constant \(C_{\mathrm{reg}}\). \(\square\)

2. Two elementary lattice-counting lemmas

We will repeatedly use the following simple facts.

Let \(K\) be totally real of degree \(d\), and write

\[ \Sigma:K\to\mathbb R^d, \qquad \Sigma(x)=(\sigma_1(x),\dots,\sigma_d(x)). \]

For \(x\in K\), set

\[ \|x\|_\infty=\max_{1\le j\le d}|\sigma_j(x)|. \]

Lemma 2.1: packing of algebraic integers

For every \(T\ge0\) and every \(y\in\mathbb R^d\),

\[ \#\left\{ \alpha\in\mathcal O_K: |\sigma_j(\alpha)-y_j|\le T \text{ for all }j \right\} \le (2T+1)^d. \]

Proof

If \(\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal O_K\) are distinct, then

\[ \alpha-\beta\neq0 \]

and hence

\[ |N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\alpha-\beta)| = \prod_{j=1}^d|\sigma_j(\alpha-\beta)| \]

is a nonzero integer. Therefore

\[ \prod_{j=1}^d|\sigma_j(\alpha-\beta)|\ge1, \]

so

\[ \max_j|\sigma_j(\alpha-\beta)|\ge1. \]

Thus \(\Sigma(\mathcal O_K)\) is \(1\)-separated in the \(\ell^\infty\)-metric.

Now place around every point of

\[ \Sigma(\mathcal O_K)\cap \prod_{j=1}^d[y_j-T,y_j+T] \]

an \(\ell^\infty\)-cube of radius \(1/2\). These cubes are disjoint and lie inside the cube of side length \(2T+1\). Hence the number of points is at most

\[ (2T+1)^d. \]

\(\square\)

Lemma 2.2: a Blichfeldt difference-body lower bound

Let \(\Lambda\subset\mathbb R^r\) be a lattice of covolume \(\det\Lambda\). If \(\Omega\subset\mathbb R^r\) is measurable and bounded, then

\[ |\Lambda\cap(\Omega-\Omega)| \ge \frac{\operatorname{vol}(\Omega)}{\det\Lambda}. \]

Proof

By Blichfeldt’s principle, there exists a translate \(x+\Omega\) containing at least

\[ \frac{\operatorname{vol}(\Omega)}{\det\Lambda} \]

points of \(\Lambda\). Fix one such point \(\lambda_0\). For every other lattice point \(\lambda\in \Lambda\cap(x+\Omega)\), the difference

\[ \lambda-\lambda_0 \]

belongs to

\[ \Lambda\cap(\Omega-\Omega). \]

The differences are distinct, so the claim follows. \(\square\)

Lemma 2.3: doubling for lattice points in boxes

There is an absolute constant \(C_{\mathrm{box}}>1\) such that the following holds.

Let \(\Lambda\subset\mathbb R^r\) be any lattice, and for \(L>0\) let

\[ Q_L=[-L,L]^r. \]

Then

\[ |\Lambda\cap Q_{2L}| \le C_{\mathrm{box}}^r |\Lambda\cap Q_L|. \]

More generally, the same estimate holds, up to changing \(C_{\mathrm{box}}\), for boxes inside any fixed codimension-one subspace.

Proof

Cover \(Q_{2L}\) by at most \(5^r\) translates of \(Q_{L/2}\). Let \(x+Q_{L/2}\) be one such translate. If

\[ \lambda_0,\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_m \in \Lambda\cap(x+Q_{L/2}), \]

then

\[ \lambda_i-\lambda_0\in \Lambda\cap Q_L. \]

Thus each translate contains at most \(|\Lambda\cap Q_L|\) lattice points. Therefore

\[ |\Lambda\cap Q_{2L}| \le 5^r|\Lambda\cap Q_L|. \]

The codimension-one version is identical. \(\square\)

3. Many bounded units

Let \(K\) be one of the totally real fields from Theorem 1.1, with degree \(d\).

Let

\[ H_d=\left\{x=(x_1,\dots,x_d)\in\mathbb R^d:\sum_{j=1}^d x_j=0\right\}. \]

The logarithmic embedding of the unit group is

\[ \ell:\mathcal O_K^\times\to H_d, \qquad \ell(u)=\bigl(\log|\sigma_1(u)|,\dots,\log|\sigma_d(u)|\bigr). \]

Its image is a lattice in \(H_d\), with covolume comparable to the regulator \(R_K\). We absorb the harmless normalization constant into \(C_{\mathrm{reg}}\).

For \(L>0\), define

\[ Q_L^{(d)} = \left\{x\in H_d:\|x\|_\infty\le L\right\}. \]

There is an absolute constant \(c_0>0\) such that

\[ \operatorname{vol}_{H_d}(Q_L^{(d)}) \ge (c_0L)^{d-1} \]

for all \(d\ge2\).

Choose a large constant \(L>1\), to be fixed permanently below, and define

\[ U_K = \left\{ u\in\mathcal O_K^\times: \|\ell(u)\|_\infty\le L \right\}. \]

Equivalently,

\[ e^{-L}\le |\sigma_j(u)|\le e^L \qquad \text{for all }u\in U_K,\ 1\le j\le d. \]

We now record the properties of \(U_K\).

Lemma 3.1: size and doubling of \(U_K\)

There are constants

\[ \rho>1,\qquad \rho_+>1,\qquad C_U>1 \]

depending only on \(L\) and \(\Delta\), such that, after discarding finitely many fields in the tower, for every remaining \(K\) of degree \(d\),

  1. \[ |U_K|\ge \rho^d; \]
  2. \[ |U_K|\le \rho_+^d; \]
  3. \[ |U_KU_K|\le C_U^d|U_K|; \]
  4. \[ |U_KU_K^{-1}|\le C_U^d|U_K|. \]

Moreover, by choosing \(L\) sufficiently large, we may make \(\rho\) arbitrarily large.

Proof

Let

\[ \Lambda_K=\ell(\mathcal O_K^\times)\subset H_d. \]

By Lemma 2.2 applied inside \(H_d\),

\[ |\Lambda_K\cap Q_L^{(d)}| \ge \frac{\operatorname{vol}_{H_d}(Q_{L/2}^{(d)})}{\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda_K)} \gg \frac{(c_0L)^{d-1}}{R_K}. \]

Using Lemma 1.2,

\[ R_K\le C_{\mathrm{reg}}^d. \]

Thus, after decreasing \(c_0\) slightly and discarding finitely many small degrees,

\[ |\Lambda_K\cap Q_L^{(d)}| \ge \left(\frac{c_0L}{C_{\mathrm{reg}}}\right)^d. \]

The kernel of the logarithmic embedding consists only of \(\{\pm1\}\), so this gives

\[ |U_K|\ge \rho^d \]

for

\[ \rho=\frac{c_0L}{2C_{\mathrm{reg}}}, \]

say. By taking \(L\) large, \(\rho\) can be made as large as desired.

For the upper bound, if \(u\in U_K\), then

\[ |\sigma_j(u)|\le e^L \]

for every \(j\). By Lemma 2.1,

\[ |U_K| \le (2e^L+1)^d. \]

Thus we may take

\[ \rho_+=2e^L+1. \]

For the doubling estimate, note that

\[ \ell(U_KU_K),\ \ell(U_KU_K^{-1}) \subset \Lambda_K\cap Q_{2L}^{(d)}. \]

By Lemma 2.3, inside the hyperplane \(H_d\),

\[ |\Lambda_K\cap Q_{2L}^{(d)}| \le C_U^d|\Lambda_K\cap Q_L^{(d)}| \]

for an absolute \(C_U>1\). Accounting for the kernel \(\{\pm1\}\) only changes \(C_U\) by an absolute factor. Hence

\[ |U_KU_K|,\ |U_KU_K^{-1}| \le C_U^d|U_K|. \]

\(\square\)

4. A large bounded pool of algebraic integers

Fix a large constant \(W>1\), to be chosen later.

For a totally real field \(K\) of degree \(d\), define

\[ T_{0,K} = \left\{ t\in\mathcal O_K: |\sigma_j(t)|\le 2W\text{ for all }j \right\}. \]

Let

\[ P_K:=|T_{0,K}|. \]

Lemma 4.1: size of the pool

There are constants

\[ \Pi_-=\frac{2W}{\sqrt\Delta}, \qquad \Pi_+=4W+1 \]

such that

\[ \Pi_-^d\le P_K\le \Pi_+^d. \]

Proof

For the lower bound, apply Lemma 2.2 to the lattice

\[ \Sigma(\mathcal O_K)\subset\mathbb R^d \]

and the box

\[ \Omega=[-W,W]^d. \]

The covolume of \(\Sigma(\mathcal O_K)\) is \(\sqrt{|D_K|}\). Since

\[ |D_K|^{1/d}\le\Delta, \]

we have

\[ \sqrt{|D_K|}\le \Delta^{d/2}. \]

Thus

\[ P_K = |\Sigma(\mathcal O_K)\cap[-2W,2W]^d| \ge \frac{(2W)^d}{\sqrt{|D_K|}} \ge \left(\frac{2W}{\sqrt\Delta}\right)^d = \Pi_-^d. \]

For the upper bound, apply Lemma 2.1 with \(T=2W\):

\[ P_K\le (4W+1)^d=\Pi_+^d. \]

\(\square\)

Now translate this pool away from the coordinate hyperplanes. Let

\[ M=\lceil 4W\rceil \]

and set

\[ T_K=M+T_{0,K}. \]

Then

\[ |T_K|=P_K, \]

and for every \(s\in T_K\),

\[ W\le \sigma_j(s)\le 7W \qquad \text{for all }j. \]

Indeed, \(M\ge4W\), while \(|\sigma_j(t)|\le2W\) for \(t\in T_{0,K}\).

Thus every element of \(T_K\) is totally positive, and all of its conjugates are comparable to \(W\).

5. Choosing a sparse subset \(S_K\subset T_K\)

Let

\[ R_K^U:=|U_K|. \]

To avoid confusion with the regulator, in this section write

\[ R:=|U_K|. \]

Let \(\kappa>1\) be a large constant, fixed below. We choose a random subset

\[ S\subset T_K \]

by including each element of \(T_K\) independently with probability

\[ p=\frac1{\kappa^d R}. \]

We will show that, for suitable \(\kappa\), with positive probability \(S\) has the two properties we need.

Let

\[ X=|S|. \]

Then

\[ \mathbb E X=pP_K. \]

We also define the collision count

\[ Y = \#\left\{ (u,u',s,s')\in U_K^2\times S^2: us=u's',\ (u,s)\neq(u',s') \right\}. \]

The goal is to make \(Y\) much smaller than the trivial representation count \(R X\).

Lemma 5.1: sparse selection with few collisions

If \(\kappa\) is chosen sufficiently large in terms of \(C_U\), then, for all sufficiently large \(d\), there exists \(S_K\subset T_K\) such that, writing \(X=|S_K|\),

  1. \[ \frac12pP_K\le X\le 2pP_K; \]
  2. \[ Y\le \frac14 RX. \]

Proof

First,

\[ \mathbb E X=pP_K. \]

Since later \(W\) will be chosen so that \(pP_K\) grows exponentially in \(d\), standard Chernoff bounds give

\[ \mathbb P\left(\frac12pP_K\le X\le2pP_K\right)>0.9 \]

for all sufficiently large \(d\).

Now estimate \(\mathbb E Y\).

If

\[ us=u's', \]

then

\[ s'=w s, \qquad w=u'^{-1}u\in U_K^{-1}U_K. \]

For a fixed ordered pair \((u,u')\), hence fixed \(w\), the number of \(s\in T_K\) such that both \(s\) and \(ws\) lie in \(T_K\) is at most \(|T_K|=P_K\). Each such pair survives in \(S\) with probability at most \(p^2\). Therefore

\[ \mathbb E Y \le |U_K|\,|U_K^{-1}U_K|\,p^2P_K. \]

Using Lemma 3.1,

\[ |U_K^{-1}U_K|\le C_U^dR. \]

Therefore

\[ \mathbb E Y \le R(C_U^dR)p^2P_K = C_U^dR^2\frac{P_K}{\kappa^{2d}R^2} = \left(\frac{C_U}{\kappa^2}\right)^dP_K. \]

On the other hand,

\[ R\mathbb E X = R pP_K = \frac{P_K}{\kappa^d}. \]

Choosing \(\kappa\) so large that

\[ \left(\frac{C_U}{\kappa^2}\right)^dP_K \le \frac1{100}\cdot \frac{P_K}{\kappa^d} \]

for all \(d\), we get

\[ \mathbb E Y\le \frac1{100}R\mathbb E X. \]

By Markov’s inequality,

\[ \mathbb P\left(Y\le \frac14R X\right)>0.9 \]

for all sufficiently large \(d\). Hence the two desired properties hold simultaneously with positive probability.

Fix such a set \(S_K\). \(\square\)

6. The algebraic set \(\mathcal A_K=U_KS_K\)

Fix \(K\) in the tower, of sufficiently large degree \(d\). Let

\[ U=U_K,\qquad S=S_K, \qquad \mathcal A_K=US=\{us:u\in U,\ s\in S\}\subset\mathcal O_K. \]

Let

\[ n_K:=|\mathcal A_K|. \]

The representation map

\[ U\times S\to \mathcal A_K, \qquad (u,s)\mapsto us \]

may not be injective, but the collision bound from Lemma 5.1 implies that it is injective up to a bounded factor.

Lemma 6.1: size of \(\mathcal A_K\)

For all sufficiently large \(d\),

\[ \frac12 |U||S|\le |\mathcal A_K|\le |U||S|. \]

Proof

For \(a\in\mathcal A_K\), let

\[ r(a)=|\{(u,s)\in U\times S:us=a\}|. \]

Then

\[ \sum_{a\in\mathcal A_K}r(a)=|U||S|=RX. \]

Also,

\[ \sum_{a\in\mathcal A_K}r(a)^2 \]

counts all pairs of representations of the same element. The diagonal contributes \(RX\), and the non-diagonal contribution is \(Y\). By Lemma 5.1,

\[ \sum_a r(a)^2 \le RX+\frac14RX = \frac54RX. \]

By Cauchy–Schwarz,

\[ (RX)^2 = \left(\sum_a r(a)\right)^2 \le |\mathcal A_K|\sum_a r(a)^2 \le |\mathcal A_K|\frac54RX. \]

Hence

\[ |\mathcal A_K|\ge \frac45RX. \]

In particular,

\[ |\mathcal A_K|\ge \frac12RX. \]

The upper bound is trivial. \(\square\)

7. Parameter choice

We now choose the constants once and for all.

Recall the constants:

  • \(\Delta\) from the bounded-root-discriminant tower;
  • \(C_{\mathrm{reg}}\) from the regulator bound;
  • \(C_U\) from the unit doubling estimate.

From Lemma 3.1, by choosing \(L\) large, we may make

\[ \rho>1 \]

as large as we like, where

\[ |U_K|\ge \rho^d. \]

Choose \(L\) so large that

\[ \rho>C_\times^4, \]

where

\[ C_\times:=4C_U. \]

Once \(L\) is fixed, \(\rho_+\) is fixed, where

\[ |U_K|\le \rho_+^d. \]

Choose \(\kappa>1\) sufficiently large in terms of \(C_U\), as required in Lemma 5.1.

Now choose \(W\) sufficiently large so that the following hold.

Let

\[ \Pi_-=\frac{2W}{\sqrt\Delta}, \qquad \Pi_+=2(4W+1), \]

and define

\[ \nu=\frac{\Pi_-}{4\kappa}. \]

Also define

\[ \gamma=\frac{\log\rho}{\log\Pi_+}. \]

Since \(\rho>1\), we have \(\gamma>0\).

We choose \(W\) large enough that:

  1. The random selection has exponentially many available points:

    \[ \Pi_->8\kappa\rho_+. \]

  2. Product constants are absorbed:

    \[ C_\times\le \nu^{\gamma/2}. \]

  3. Additive constants are absorbed:

    \[ 30e^L W\le \nu^{2-\gamma/4}. \]

Such a \(W\) exists.

Indeed, as \(W\to\infty\),

\[ \frac{\log\nu}{\log\Pi_+}\to1, \]

so

\[ \nu^{\gamma/2} = \exp\left(\frac{\gamma}{2}\log\nu\right) \to \exp\left(\frac12\log\rho\right) = \rho^{1/2}. \]

Since \(\rho>C_\times^4\), we have \(\rho^{1/2}>C_\times\), so condition 2 holds for all sufficiently large \(W\). Condition 3 holds because its left-hand side grows linearly in \(W\), while its right-hand side grows like \(W^{2-o(1)}\).

Finally set

\[ \delta:=\frac{\gamma}{4}>0. \]

This \(\delta\) is an absolute constant.

8. Lower and upper bounds for \(n_K=|\mathcal A_K|\)

Let

\[ P=|T_K|. \]

By Lemma 4.1,

\[ \Pi_-^d\le P\le (4W+1)^d. \]

By Lemma 5.1,

\[ \frac12\frac{P}{\kappa^dR} \le |S| \le 2\frac{P}{\kappa^dR}. \]

Using Lemma 6.1,

\[ \frac12 R|S|\le n_K\le R|S|. \]

Therefore

\[ n_K\ge \frac12 R\cdot \frac12\frac{P}{\kappa^dR} = \frac{P}{4\kappa^d} \ge \left(\frac{\Pi_-}{4\kappa}\right)^d = \nu^d. \]

Also,

\[ n_K\le R\cdot 2\frac{P}{\kappa^dR} = 2\frac{P}{\kappa^d} \le 2P \le \Pi_+^d. \]

Thus

\[ \nu^d\le n_K\le \Pi_+^d. \]

Since

\[ |U_K|\ge \rho^d \]

and

\[ n_K\le \Pi_+^d, \]

we get

\[ |U_K|\ge n_K^\gamma, \]

where

\[ \gamma=\frac{\log\rho}{\log\Pi_+}. \]

9. The sumset bound

Every element of \(S\subset T_K\) satisfies

\[ |\sigma_j(s)|\le 7W. \]

Every \(u\in U_K\) satisfies

\[ |\sigma_j(u)|\le e^L. \]

Therefore every element \(a=us\in\mathcal A_K\) satisfies

\[ |\sigma_j(a)|\le 7e^L W \qquad \text{for all }j. \]

Hence every element of

\[ \mathcal A_K+\mathcal A_K \]

satisfies

\[ |\sigma_j(x)|\le 14e^L W \qquad \text{for all }j. \]

By Lemma 2.1,

\[ |\mathcal A_K+\mathcal A_K| \le (28e^LW+1)^d \le (30e^LW)^d. \]

By the choice of \(W\),

\[ 30e^LW\le \nu^{2-\gamma/4}. \]

Since

\[ n_K\ge \nu^d, \]

we obtain

\[ |\mathcal A_K+\mathcal A_K| \le \nu^{(2-\gamma/4)d} \le n_K^{2-\gamma/4}. \]

Since

\[ \delta=\frac{\gamma}{4}, \]

this gives

\[ |\mathcal A_K+\mathcal A_K| \le n_K^{2-\delta}. \]

10. The product set bound

We have

\[ \mathcal A_K\mathcal A_K = (US)(US) \subset (UU)(SS). \]

Therefore

\[ |\mathcal A_K\mathcal A_K| \le |UU|\,|SS|. \]

By Lemma 3.1,

\[ |UU|\le C_U^d|U|. \]

Also trivially

\[ |SS|\le |S|^2. \]

Hence

\[ |\mathcal A_K\mathcal A_K| \le C_U^d|U||S|^2. \]

Since Lemma 6.1 gives

\[ n_K=|\mathcal A_K|\ge \frac12|U||S|, \]

we have

\[ |S| \le \frac{2n_K}{|U|}. \]

Thus

\[ |\mathcal A_K\mathcal A_K| \le C_U^d |U|\left(\frac{2n_K}{|U|}\right)^2 = 4C_U^d\frac{n_K^2}{|U|}. \]

By definition,

\[ C_\times=4C_U, \]

so

\[ |\mathcal A_K\mathcal A_K| \le C_\times^d\frac{n_K^2}{|U|}. \]

Since

\[ |U|\ge n_K^\gamma, \]

we get

\[ |\mathcal A_K\mathcal A_K| \le C_\times^d n_K^{2-\gamma}. \]

By the choice of \(W\),

\[ C_\times\le \nu^{\gamma/2}. \]

Since

\[ n_K\ge\nu^d, \]

we have

\[ C_\times^d \le n_K^{\gamma/2}. \]

Therefore

\[ |\mathcal A_K\mathcal A_K| \le n_K^{2-\gamma/2}. \]

Because

\[ \delta=\frac{\gamma}{4}, \]

we have

\[ 2-\frac{\gamma}{2} \le 2-\delta. \]

Thus

\[ |\mathcal A_K\mathcal A_K| \le n_K^{2-\delta}. \]

11. Passing from algebraic integers to real numbers

For each field \(K_i\) in the tower, choose one real embedding

\[ \sigma_{i,1}:K_i\hookrightarrow\mathbb R. \]

Define

\[ A_i=\sigma_{i,1}(\mathcal A_{K_i})\subset\mathbb R. \]

Because \(\sigma_{i,1}\) is injective on \(K_i\),

\[ |A_i|=|\mathcal A_{K_i}|=n_{K_i}. \]

Moreover, for any finite set \(B\subset K_i\),

\[ |\sigma_{i,1}(B+B)|=|B+B|, \qquad |\sigma_{i,1}(BB)|=|BB|. \]

Therefore

\[ |A_i+A_i| = |\mathcal A_{K_i}+\mathcal A_{K_i}| \le n_{K_i}^{2-\delta} = |A_i|^{2-\delta}, \]

and similarly

\[ |A_iA_i| = |\mathcal A_{K_i}\mathcal A_{K_i}| \le |A_i|^{2-\delta}. \]

Finally, since

\[ n_{K_i}\ge \nu^{d_i} \]

and

\[ d_i\to\infty, \]

we have

\[ |A_i|=n_{K_i}\to\infty. \]

Thus

\[ \max(|A_i+A_i|,\ |A_iA_i|) \le |A_i|^{2-\delta} \]

for all sufficiently large \(i\), with an absolute constant

\[ \delta>0. \]

This proves the theorem. \(\square\)

12. Summary of the construction

The construction can be summarized as follows.

  1. Take totally real number fields \(K_i\) of increasing degree and bounded root discriminant.

  2. In each \(K_i\), the unit group has exponentially many units \(u\) whose conjugates all have bounded size:

    \[ e^{-L}\le |\sigma_j(u)|\le e^L. \]

    Let this unit set be \(U_i\).

  3. Take a large bounded archimedean box of algebraic integers \(T_i\), translate it away from zero, and choose a sparse random subset \(S_i\subset T_i\).

  4. Define

    \[ \mathcal A_i=U_iS_i\subset\mathcal O_{K_i}. \]

  5. Random sparsity guarantees

    \[ |\mathcal A_i|\asymp |U_i||S_i|. \]
  6. Additively, \(\mathcal A_i+\mathcal A_i\) lies in a bounded box in all embeddings, so it has only exponentially many possible values.

  7. Multiplicatively,

    \[ \mathcal A_i\mathcal A_i\subset (U_iU_i)(S_iS_i), \]

    and since \(U_i\) has small multiplicative doubling,

    \[ |\mathcal A_i\mathcal A_i| \ll^d \frac{|\mathcal A_i|^2}{|U_i|}. \]

    Because \(|U_i|\) is a fixed positive power of \(|\mathcal A_i|\), this gives a fixed power saving.

  8. Project by one real embedding to get the desired finite subsets of \(\mathbb R\).

Critical corrections to the previous proof

The broad unit-based construction is viable, but the previous write-up had several gaps:

  1. Regulator control was not fully justified. We need an infinite totally real family with both bounded root discriminant and regulator at most exponential in the degree. This follows from Martinet’s totally real class-field towers together with Landau’s residue bound and the analytic class number formula. I state this precisely below.

  2. The random collision argument used a random threshold incorrectly. Previously I bounded \(Y\) by a multiple of \(R X\), where \(X=|S|\) is random, directly via Markov. The correct argument first proves \(X\) is close to its mean and \(Y\) is small compared with that mean, then combines the two events.

  3. The unit-lattice covolume and hyperplane-volume factors need to be handled. The logarithmic unit lattice has covolume \(\asymp \sqrt d\,R_K\), not exactly \(R_K\). This polynomial factor is harmless but must be absorbed carefully.

  4. The collision expectation had a constant error. The ratio is essentially \((C_U/\kappa)^d\), not \((C_U/\kappa^2)^d\), after comparing to the correct normalization.

With those points fixed, the proof is as follows.

Proof

We use the following standard arithmetic input.

1. Arithmetic input

There exist absolute constants \(\Delta>1\), \(C_R>1\), and an infinite sequence of totally real number fields

\[ K_i/\mathbb Q \]

with degrees

\[ d_i=[K_i:\mathbb Q]\to\infty \]

such that

\[ |D_{K_i}|^{1/d_i}\le \Delta \]

and

\[ R_{K_i}\le C_R^{d_i}, \]

where \(D_K\) is the discriminant and \(R_K\) the regulator.

Indeed, Martinet constructed infinite totally real class-field towers with bounded root discriminant. Landau’s residue estimate gives, for a number field \(K\) of degree \(d\),

\[ \operatorname*{Res}_{s=1}\zeta_K(s)\le C_1^d \]

whenever \(|D_K|^{1/d}\le \Delta\), for some \(C_1=C_1(\Delta)\). For totally real \(K\),

\[ \operatorname*{Res}_{s=1}\zeta_K(s) = \frac{2^{d-1}h_KR_K}{\sqrt{|D_K|}}. \]

Since \(h_K\ge1\), this implies

\[ R_K \le \frac{\sqrt{|D_K|}}{2^{d-1}} \operatorname*{Res}_{s=1}\zeta_K(s) \le C_R^d \]

for some \(C_R=C_R(\Delta)\).

Fix such a sequence \(K_i\).

2. Basic counting lemmas

Let \(K\) be totally real of degree \(d\), with real embeddings

\[ \sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_d:K\hookrightarrow \mathbb R. \]

Write

\[ \Sigma(x)=(\sigma_1(x),\dots,\sigma_d(x)). \]

Lemma 2.1: packing of algebraic integers

For every \(T\ge0\) and every \(y\in\mathbb R^d\),

\[ \#\left\{ \alpha\in\mathcal O_K: |\sigma_j(\alpha)-y_j|\le T\text{ for all }j \right\} \le (2T+1)^d. \]

Proof

If \(\alpha\neq\beta\in\mathcal O_K\), then

\[ N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\alpha-\beta)\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}, \]

so

\[ \prod_{j=1}^d|\sigma_j(\alpha-\beta)|\ge1. \]

Hence

\[ \max_j|\sigma_j(\alpha-\beta)|\ge1. \]

Thus \(\Sigma(\mathcal O_K)\) is \(1\)-separated in the \(\ell^\infty\)-metric. Disjoint \(\ell^\infty\)-balls of radius \(1/2\) around all points in a box of radius \(T\) lie in the concentric box of radius \(T+1/2\), whose volume is \((2T+1)^d\). \(\square\)

Lemma 2.2: Blichfeldt difference lemma

Let \(\Lambda\subset\mathbb R^r\) be a lattice of covolume \(\det\Lambda\), and let \(\Omega\subset\mathbb R^r\) be bounded and measurable. Then

\[ |\Lambda\cap(\Omega-\Omega)| \ge \frac{\operatorname{vol}(\Omega)}{\det\Lambda}. \]

This is the usual Blichfeldt principle: some translate of \(\Omega\) contains at least \(\operatorname{vol}(\Omega)/\det\Lambda\) lattice points, and their pairwise differences lie in \(\Omega-\Omega\).

3. Many bounded units

Let

\[ H_d=\left\{x\in\mathbb R^d:\sum_{j=1}^d x_j=0\right\}. \]

The logarithmic embedding of units is

\[ \ell:\mathcal O_K^\times\to H_d, \qquad \ell(u)=\left(\log|\sigma_1(u)|,\dots,\log|\sigma_d(u)|\right). \]

Its image \(\Lambda_K=\ell(\mathcal O_K^\times)\) is a lattice in \(H_d\) with covolume \(\asymp \sqrt d\,R_K\).

For \(L>0\), define

\[ Q_L^{(d)}=\{x\in H_d:\|x\|_\infty\le L\}. \]

By the cube-slicing theorem of Vaaler/Ball, there is an absolute constant \(c_0>0\) such that

\[ \operatorname{vol}_{H_d}(Q_L^{(d)})\ge (c_0L)^{d-1}. \]

Also, by a simple covering argument, there is an absolute constant \(C_U>1\) such that for every lattice \(\Lambda\subset H_d\),

\[ |\Lambda\cap Q_{2L}^{(d)}| \le C_U^d|\Lambda\cap Q_L^{(d)}|. \]

Indeed, cover the cube \([-2L,2L]^d\) by \(O(1)^d\) cubes of side length \(L\). In each small cube, after subtracting one lattice point, all other lattice points inject into \(\Lambda\cap Q_L^{(d)}\).

Now choose \(L>1\) large, to be specified later, and define

\[ U_K= \left\{ u\in\mathcal O_K^\times: |\log|\sigma_j(u)||\le L\text{ for all }j \right\}. \]

Equivalently,

\[ e^{-L}\le |\sigma_j(u)|\le e^L \qquad \forall u\in U_K,\ \forall j. \]

By Lemma 2.2 applied to \(\Lambda_K\subset H_d\),

\[ |\Lambda_K\cap Q_L^{(d)}| \ge \frac{\operatorname{vol}(Q_{L/2}^{(d)})}{\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda_K)} \gg \frac{(c_0L)^{d-1}}{\sqrt d\,R_K}. \]

Since \(R_K\le C_R^d\), after discarding finitely many fields,

\[ |U_K|\ge \rho^d \]

for some

\[ \rho=\rho(L)>1, \]

and by taking \(L\) sufficiently large, \(\rho\) can be made arbitrarily large.

On the other hand, Lemma 2.1 gives

\[ |U_K|\le (2e^L+1)^d. \]

Set

\[ \rho_+=2e^L+1. \]

Thus

\[ \rho^d\le |U_K|\le \rho_+^d. \]

Moreover,

\[ U_KU_K,\ U_KU_K^{-1} \subset \{u\in\mathcal O_K^\times:\|\ell(u)\|_\infty\le 2L\}, \]

so the lattice doubling bound gives

\[ |U_KU_K|\le C_U^d|U_K|, \qquad |U_KU_K^{-1}|\le C_U^d|U_K|. \]

4. A large bounded pool of algebraic integers

Fix a large parameter \(W>1\), to be chosen later. Define

\[ T_{0,K} = \left\{ t\in\mathcal O_K: |\sigma_j(t)|\le 2W\text{ for all }j \right\}. \]

Let

\[ P_K=|T_{0,K}|. \]

By Lemma 2.2 applied to the lattice \(\Sigma(\mathcal O_K)\subset\mathbb R^d\), whose covolume is \(\sqrt{|D_K|}\),

\[ P_K \ge \frac{(2W)^d}{\sqrt{|D_K|}} \ge \left(\frac{2W}{\sqrt\Delta}\right)^d. \]

Set

\[ \Pi_-=\frac{2W}{\sqrt\Delta}. \]

By Lemma 2.1,

\[ P_K\le (4W+1)^d. \]

Now choose the integer

\[ M=\lceil 4W\rceil \]

and set

\[ T_K=M+T_{0,K}. \]

Then

\[ |T_K|=P_K, \]

and every \(s\in T_K\) satisfies

\[ W\le \sigma_j(s)\le 7W \qquad \forall j. \]

In particular, every element of \(T_K\) is nonzero.

5. Random sparse selection

Write

\[ U=U_K,\qquad N=|U|. \]

Choose a random subset

\[ S\subset T_K \]

by including each element independently with probability

\[ p=\frac1{\kappa^dN}, \]

where \(\kappa>1\) is a constant to be chosen later.

Let

\[ X=|S|, \qquad \mu=\mathbb E X=pP_K. \]

We also count collisions:

\[ Y= \#\left\{ (u,u',s,s')\in U^2\times S^2: us=u's',\ (u,s)\neq(u',s') \right\}. \]

For \(us=u's'\), we have

\[ s'=u'^{-1}us. \]

Let

\[ w=u'^{-1}u\in U^{-1}U. \]

For fixed \(w\), there are at most \(N\) ordered pairs \((u,u')\in U^2\) with \(u'^{-1}u=w\). Also,

\[ |U^{-1}U|\le C_U^dN. \]

For each fixed \(w\neq1\), the number of \(s\in T_K\) with \(ws\in T_K\) is at most \(P_K\). Since every \(s\in T_K\) is nonzero, \(ws=s\) would force \(w=1\). Hence for \(w\neq1\), the two elements \(s\) and \(ws\) are distinct, so the probability that both lie in \(S\) is \(p^2\).

Therefore

\[ \mathbb E Y \le N|U^{-1}U|P_Kp^2 \le C_U^dN^2P_Kp^2. \]

Since

\[ p=\frac1{\kappa^dN}, \]

we get

\[ \mathbb E Y \le \left(\frac{C_U}{\kappa^2}\right)^dP_K. \]

But

\[ N\mu=NpP_K=\frac{P_K}{\kappa^d}. \]

Thus

\[ \mathbb E Y \le \left(\frac{C_U}{\kappa}\right)^dN\mu. \]

Choose \(\kappa\) so large that, for all sufficiently large \(d\),

\[ \left(\frac{C_U}{\kappa}\right)^d\le \frac1{1000}. \]

Then

\[ \mathbb E Y\le \frac1{1000}N\mu. \]

Later we choose \(W\) so large that

\[ \mu=pP_K = \frac{P_K}{\kappa^dN} \ge \left(\frac{\Pi_-}{\kappa\rho_+}\right)^d \to\infty. \]

Hence by Chernoff’s inequality,

\[ \frac12\mu\le X\le2\mu \]

with probability tending to \(1\). By Markov’s inequality,

\[ Y\le \frac18N\mu \]

with probability at least \(1-8/1000\).

Thus, for all sufficiently large \(d\), there exists a subset \(S_K\subset T_K\) such that

\[ \frac12\mu\le |S_K|\le2\mu \]

and

\[ Y\le \frac18N\mu. \]

Since \(|S_K|\ge\mu/2\), this implies

\[ Y\le \frac14N|S_K|. \]

Fix such an \(S_K\).

6. The algebraic set

Define

\[ \mathcal A_K=U_KS_K = \{us:u\in U_K,\ s\in S_K\} \subset\mathcal O_K. \]

Let

\[ n_K=|\mathcal A_K|. \]

For \(a\in\mathcal A_K\), let

\[ r(a)=|\{(u,s)\in U_K\times S_K:us=a\}|. \]

Then

\[ \sum_a r(a)=N|S_K|. \]

Also,

\[ \sum_a r(a)^2 \]

counts all pairs of representations of the same element. The diagonal contribution is \(N|S_K|\), and the off-diagonal contribution is \(Y\). Hence

\[ \sum_a r(a)^2 \le N|S_K|+\frac14N|S_K| = \frac54N|S_K|. \]

By Cauchy–Schwarz,

\[ (N|S_K|)^2 \le n_K\sum_a r(a)^2 \le \frac54n_KN|S_K|. \]

Thus

\[ n_K\ge \frac45N|S_K|. \]

In particular,

\[ \frac12N|S_K|\le n_K\le N|S_K|. \]

Using \(|S_K|\ge\mu/2\), \(\mu=P_K/(\kappa^dN)\), we get

\[ n_K\ge \frac12N\cdot\frac12\mu = \frac{P_K}{4\kappa^d}. \]

Using \(P_K\ge \Pi_-^d\),

\[ n_K\ge \frac{\Pi_-^d}{4\kappa^d} \ge \left(\frac{\Pi_-}{4\kappa}\right)^d. \]

Set

\[ \nu=\frac{\Pi_-}{4\kappa}. \]

Then

\[ n_K\ge \nu^d. \]

Similarly, since \(|S_K|\le2\mu\),

\[ n_K\le N|S_K|\le 2N\mu=\frac{2P_K}{\kappa^d}\le2P_K. \]

Since \(P_K\le(4W+1)^d\), after enlarging constants,

\[ n_K\le \Pi_+^d, \qquad \Pi_+=2(4W+1). \]

7. Choice of parameters

We now choose constants in order.

First choose \(L\) so large that the corresponding \(\rho\) satisfies

\[ \rho>(4C_U)^4. \]

Set

\[ C_\times=4C_U. \]

Next choose \(\kappa\) sufficiently large in terms of \(C_U\), as above.

Finally choose \(W\) sufficiently large that:

  1. \(\Pi_->8\kappa\rho_+\), ensuring \(\mu\to\infty\);

  2. with

    \[ \gamma=\frac{\log\rho}{\log\Pi_+}, \]

    one has

    \[ C_\times\le \nu^{\gamma/2}; \]

  3. and

    \[ 30e^LW\le \nu^{2-\gamma/4}. \]

Such a \(W\) exists. Indeed, as \(W\to\infty\),

\[ \frac{\log\nu}{\log\Pi_+}\to1, \]

so

\[ \nu^{\gamma/2} = \exp\left(\frac{\gamma}{2}\log\nu\right) \to \exp\left(\frac12\log\rho\right) = \rho^{1/2} > C_\times. \]

Also,

\[ \nu^{2-\gamma/4} \asymp W^{2-o(1)}, \]

while \(30e^LW\) grows only linearly in \(W\). Hence condition 3 also holds for large \(W\).

Now fix

\[ \delta=\frac{\gamma}{4}>0. \]

All constants are absolute.

8. Sumset bound

For every \(a=us\in\mathcal A_K\),

\[ |\sigma_j(a)| \le |\sigma_j(u)|\,|\sigma_j(s)| \le e^L\cdot7W = 7e^LW. \]

Therefore every element of

\[ \mathcal A_K+\mathcal A_K \]

has all conjugates bounded in absolute value by

\[ 14e^LW. \]

By Lemma 2.1,

\[ |\mathcal A_K+\mathcal A_K| \le (28e^LW+1)^d \le (30e^LW)^d. \]

By the choice of \(W\),

\[ 30e^LW\le \nu^{2-\gamma/4}. \]

Since \(n_K\ge\nu^d\),

\[ |\mathcal A_K+\mathcal A_K| \le \nu^{(2-\gamma/4)d} \le n_K^{2-\gamma/4} = n_K^{2-\delta}. \]

9. Product-set bound

We have

\[ \mathcal A_K\mathcal A_K = (U_KS_K)(U_KS_K) \subset (U_KU_K)(S_KS_K). \]

Hence

\[ |\mathcal A_K\mathcal A_K| \le |U_KU_K|\,|S_KS_K|. \]

Using

\[ |U_KU_K|\le C_U^d|U_K| \]

and

\[ |S_KS_K|\le |S_K|^2, \]

we obtain

\[ |\mathcal A_K\mathcal A_K| \le C_U^dN|S_K|^2. \]

Since

\[ n_K\ge\frac12N|S_K|, \]

we have

\[ |S_K|\le \frac{2n_K}{N}. \]

Therefore

\[ |\mathcal A_K\mathcal A_K| \le 4C_U^d\frac{n_K^2}{N} \le C_\times^d\frac{n_K^2}{N}. \]

Now \(N=|U_K|\ge\rho^d\), while \(n_K\le\Pi_+^d\). Thus

\[ N\ge n_K^\gamma, \qquad \gamma=\frac{\log\rho}{\log\Pi_+}. \]

So

\[ |\mathcal A_K\mathcal A_K| \le C_\times^d n_K^{2-\gamma}. \]

By the choice of \(W\),

\[ C_\times\le \nu^{\gamma/2}. \]

Since \(n_K\ge\nu^d\),

\[ C_\times^d\le n_K^{\gamma/2}. \]

Therefore

\[ |\mathcal A_K\mathcal A_K| \le n_K^{2-\gamma/2} \le n_K^{2-\delta}. \]

10. Projection to the real line

For each sufficiently large \(K_i\) in the tower, choose one real embedding

\[ \sigma_{i,1}:K_i\hookrightarrow\mathbb R. \]

Define

\[ A_i=\sigma_{i,1}(\mathcal A_{K_i})\subset\mathbb R. \]

Since \(\sigma_{i,1}\) is injective,

\[ |A_i|=|\mathcal A_{K_i}|=n_{K_i}. \]

Also,

\[ A_i+A_i = \sigma_{i,1}(\mathcal A_{K_i}+\mathcal A_{K_i}), \]

and

\[ A_iA_i = \sigma_{i,1}(\mathcal A_{K_i}\mathcal A_{K_i}), \]

so cardinalities are preserved.

Thus

\[ |A_i+A_i| \le |A_i|^{2-\delta} \]

and

\[ |A_iA_i| \le |A_i|^{2-\delta}. \]

Finally,

\[ |A_i|=n_{K_i}\ge \nu^{d_i}, \]

and \(d_i\to\infty\), so \(|A_i|\to\infty\).

Therefore

\[ \max(|A_i+A_i|,\ |A_iA_i|) \le |A_i|^{2-\delta} \]

for infinitely many finite sets \(A_i\subset\mathbb R\), with one absolute constant \(\delta>0\).

This proves the theorem. \(\square\)
