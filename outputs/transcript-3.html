1. Most promising approach

The most promising route is to build \(A\) inside a high-degree totally real number field \(K\), then embed \(K\hookrightarrow \mathbb R\). The key idea is:

  • Use many small-height units \(u\in \mathcal O_K^\times\).

Units give multiplicative structure: if \(U\) is a set of units with small multiplicative doubling, then \(UU\) is small.

  • Multiply those units by a large “additive reservoir” \(B\subset \mathcal O_K\) consisting of many algebraic integers whose conjugates are bounded.

The set

\[ A_K := U B=\{ub:u\in U,\ b\in B\}\subset K \]

can have size close to \(|U||B|\), while

\[ A_K+A_K\subset \{x\in\mathcal O_K:\text{ all conjugates of }x\text{ are bounded}\}, \]

and

\[ A_KA_K\subset (UU)(BB). \]

The hoped-for entropy bookkeeping is as follows. Let \(n=[K:\mathbb Q]\). Suppose:

  • \(|U|\approx e^{sn}\),
  • \(U\) has height \(\le e^T\),
  • \(|UU|\le e^{\kappa n}|U|\),
  • \(B\) has \(|B|\approx e^{(r-q)n}\), where \(q=\frac1{2n}\log |\Delta_K|\), and all conjugates of \(B\) have size \(\le e^r\).

Then one expects

\[ |A|\approx e^{(s+r-q)n}, \]

while

\[ |A+A|\lesssim e^{(r+T+O(1))n}, \]

and

\[ |AA|\lesssim e^{(2r+s+\kappa+O(1))n}. \]

Thus we want constants satisfying

\[ r+T+O(1)\le (2-\delta)(s+r-q), \]

and

\[ 2r+s+\kappa+O(1)\le (2-\delta)(s+r-q). \]

The second inequality is the main one: at \(\delta=0\), it asks roughly

\[ s \gtrsim \kappa+2q. \]

So we need a set \(U\) of units whose exponential entropy \(s\) beats both the root-discriminant cost \(2q\) and the multiplicative-doubling cost \(\kappa\).

The rest of this answer develops this approach rigorously up to one precise remaining arithmetic/geometric input about the logarithmic unit lattice.

2. Number-field setup

Let \(K\) be a totally real number field of degree \(n\), with embeddings

\[ \sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_n:K\hookrightarrow \mathbb R. \]

For \(x\in K\), write

\[ \|x\|_\infty:=\max_{1\le j\le n}|\sigma_j(x)|. \]

For a unit \(u\in\mathcal O_K^\times\), define its logarithmic embedding

\[ \lambda(u):=(\log|\sigma_1(u)|,\dots,\log|\sigma_n(u)|)\in H_n, \]

where

\[ H_n:=\{(x_1,\dots,x_n)\in\mathbb R^n:x_1+\cdots+x_n=0\}. \]

Let

\[ \Delta_K:=|\operatorname{disc}(K)|,\qquad q_K:=\frac1{2n}\log \Delta_K. \]

Thus \(\Delta_K^{1/2}=e^{q_K n}\).

Eventually we want a sequence \(K_i\) with \(q_{K_i}\le q\) for an absolute constant \(q\). This is supplied by totally real class-field towers, as discussed later.

3. Basic lattice-counting lemmas

We need three elementary but important counting estimates.

Lemma 1: Algebraic integers in boxes are separated

Let \(K\) be totally real of degree \(n\). For \(X_1,\dots,X_n\ge 1\), define

\[ \operatorname{Box}_K(X_1,\dots,X_n) := \{a\in\mathcal O_K:|\sigma_j(a)|\le X_j\text{ for all }j\}. \]

Then

\[ |\operatorname{Box}_K(X_1,\dots,X_n)| \le \prod_{j=1}^n (4X_j). \]

In particular, if \(X\ge 1\), then

\[ |\{a\in\mathcal O_K:\|a\|_\infty\le X\}| \le (4X)^n. \]

Proof

Partition each interval \([-X_j,X_j]\) into at most \(4X_j\) half-open intervals of length \(<1\). This partitions the box \(\prod_j[-X_j,X_j]\) into at most \(\prod_j4X_j\) small boxes, each of side length \(<1\).

Suppose two distinct algebraic integers \(a,b\in\mathcal O_K\) lie in the same small box. Then for every \(j\),

\[ |\sigma_j(a-b)|<1. \]

Since \(a-b\neq 0\) is an algebraic integer,

\[ N_{K/\mathbb Q}(a-b)=\prod_{j=1}^n\sigma_j(a-b) \]

is a nonzero integer. But

\[ |N_{K/\mathbb Q}(a-b)| = \prod_{j=1}^n|\sigma_j(a-b)|<1, \]

impossible. Hence each small box contains at most one algebraic integer. ∎

Lemma 2: A large bounded additive reservoir

Let \(K\) be totally real of degree \(n\), and let \(R\ge 1\). Then there exists a finite set

\[ B\subset \mathcal O_K \]

such that

  1. \(\|b\|_\infty\le R\) for every \(b\in B\);
  2. \[ |B|\ge R^n\Delta_K^{-1/2}=e^{(r-q_K)n}, \]

    where \(r:=\log R\).

Proof

Let \(\Lambda:=\sigma(\mathcal O_K)\subset\mathbb R^n\) be the Minkowski lattice. Its covolume is \(\Delta_K^{1/2}\).

Let

\[ Q:=[0,R)^n. \]

By Blichfeldt’s averaging principle, there is a translate \(x+Q\) containing at least

\[ \frac{\operatorname{vol}(Q)}{\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda)} = \frac{R^n}{\Delta_K^{1/2}} \]

points of \(\Lambda\).

Let those lattice points be \(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_m\), with

\[ m\ge R^n\Delta_K^{-1/2}. \]

Choose one of them, say \(\lambda_1\), and define

\[ B:=\{\sigma^{-1}(\lambda_i-\lambda_1):1\le i\le m\}\subset \mathcal O_K. \]

Then \(|B|=m\), and since \(\lambda_i,\lambda_1\in x+Q\), we have

\[ \lambda_i-\lambda_1\in Q-Q\subset [-R,R]^n. \]

Therefore \(\|b\|_\infty\le R\) for every \(b\in B\). ∎

Lemma 3: Intersections of unit-dilates of \(B\)

Let \(B\subset\mathcal O_K\) satisfy \(\|b\|_\infty\le R\). Let \(u,v\in\mathcal O_K^\times\) be units with

\[ |\log|\sigma_j(u)||\le T,\qquad |\log|\sigma_j(v)||\le T \]

for all \(j\). Assume also

\[ R e^{-T}\ge 1. \]

Then

\[ |uB\cap vB| \le (4R)^n \exp\left(-\frac12\|\lambda(u)-\lambda(v)\|_1\right). \]

Proof

If \(x\in uB\cap vB\), then

\[ x=ub_1=vb_2 \]

for some \(b_1,b_2\in B\). Thus for every embedding \(j\),

\[ |\sigma_j(x)| \le R\min(|\sigma_j(u)|,|\sigma_j(v)|). \]

Since \(R e^{-T}\ge 1\), all radii

\[ R\min(|\sigma_j(u)|,|\sigma_j(v)|) \]

are at least \(1\). By Lemma 1,

\[ |uB\cap vB| \le \prod_{j=1}^n 4R\min(|\sigma_j(u)|,|\sigma_j(v)|). \]

Now put

\[ a_j:=\log|\sigma_j(u)|,\qquad b_j:=\log|\sigma_j(v)|. \]

Since \(u,v\) are units,

\[ \sum_j a_j=\sum_j b_j=0. \]

Also,

\[ \log\min(e^{a_j},e^{b_j}) = \frac{a_j+b_j-|a_j-b_j|}{2}. \]

Therefore

\[ \sum_{j=1}^n\log\min(|\sigma_j(u)|,|\sigma_j(v)|) = -\frac12\sum_{j=1}^n|a_j-b_j| = -\frac12\|\lambda(u)-\lambda(v)\|_1. \]

Hence

\[ \prod_{j=1}^n\min(|\sigma_j(u)|,|\sigma_j(v)|) = \exp\left(-\frac12\|\lambda(u)-\lambda(v)\|_1\right), \]

and the claim follows. ∎

4. The key conditional construction

We now isolate the exact unit-set input needed.

Unit-set hypothesis \(\mathsf{U}(q,T,s_-,s_+,\kappa,\eta)\)

Fix constants

\[ q,T,s_-,s_+,\kappa,\eta>0. \]

Suppose that for infinitely many totally real number fields \(K\) of degree \(n\to\infty\), with

\[ q_K\le q, \]

there exists a finite set of units

\[ U\subset \mathcal O_K^\times \]

such that:

  1. Size:

    \[ e^{s_- n}\le |U|\le e^{s_+ n}. \]

  2. Small multiplicative doubling:

    \[ |UU|\le e^{\kappa n}|U|. \]

  3. Bounded logarithmic height:

    \[ |\log|\sigma_j(u)||\le T \]

    for every \(u\in U\) and every embedding \(j\).

  4. Logarithmic separation: for all distinct \(u,v\in U\),

    \[ \|\lambda(u)-\lambda(v)\|_1\ge \eta n. \]

The separation condition is stronger than morally necessary, but it is a clean sufficient condition guaranteeing that the sets \(uB\) have small overlap.

5. Conditional proposition: unit sets imply sum-product counterexamples

Proposition

Assume the unit-set hypothesis above. Suppose in addition that there exist constants \(r>0\), \(\delta>0\), and a small margin \(\gamma>0\), such that

\[ r>T, \tag{5.1} \]
\[ \frac{\eta}{2}>s_+ + q + \log 4+\gamma, \tag{5.2} \]
\[ r+T+\log 8 \le (2-\delta)(s_-+r-q)-\gamma, \tag{5.3} \]

and

\[ 2r+s_+ +\kappa+\log 4 \le (2-\delta)(s_-+r-q)-\gamma. \tag{5.4} \]

Then there exists an infinite sequence of finite sets \(A_i\subset\mathbb R\) with \(|A_i|\to\infty\) and

\[ \max(|A_i+A_i|,|A_iA_i|) \le |A_i|^{2-\delta}. \]

Proof

Fix one of the fields \(K\), of degree \(n\), and choose \(U\) as above.

Let

\[ R:=e^r. \]

By Lemma 2, choose \(B\subset\mathcal O_K\) such that

\[ \|b\|_\infty\le R \]

for all \(b\in B\), and

\[ |B|\ge R^n\Delta_K^{-1/2} = e^{(r-q_K)n} \ge e^{(r-q)n}. \]

Define

\[ A_K:=UB=\{ub:u\in U,\ b\in B\}\subset K. \]

Eventually we embed \(K\) into \(\mathbb R\) using \(\sigma_1\). Since \(\sigma_1\) is injective, cardinalities of sumsets and product sets are preserved.

Step 1: Lower bound for \(|A_K|\)

The sets \(uB\), \(u\in U\), may overlap. We prove that they do not overlap too much.

For \(u=v\),

\[ |uB\cap vB|=|B|. \]

For \(u\neq v\), Lemma 3 gives

\[ |uB\cap vB| \le (4R)^n e^{-\eta n/2}. \]

Since \(|B|\ge e^{(r-q)n}=R^n e^{-qn}\), we obtain

\[ |uB\cap vB| \le e^{(\log 4+q-\eta/2)n}|B|. \]

Using condition (5.2),

\[ |uB\cap vB| \le e^{-(s_++\gamma)n}|B|. \]

Now let

\[ m(x):=\#\{u\in U:x\in uB\}. \]

Then

\[ \sum_x m(x)=|U||B|. \]

Also,

\[ \sum_x m(x)^2 = \sum_{u,v\in U}|uB\cap vB| \le |U||B|+|U|^2 e^{-(s_++\gamma)n}|B|. \]

Since \(|U|\le e^{s_+n}\),

\[ |U|^2 e^{-(s_++\gamma)n}|B| \le e^{-\gamma n}|U||B|. \]

Thus

\[ \sum_x m(x)^2 \le (1+e^{-\gamma n})|U||B|. \]

By Cauchy–Schwarz,

\[ |A_K| = |\operatorname{supp}m| \ge \frac{(\sum_x m(x))^2}{\sum_x m(x)^2} \ge (1-o(1))|U||B|. \]

In particular, for all sufficiently large \(n\),

\[ |A_K| \ge \frac12 |U||B| \ge \frac12 e^{(s_-+r-q)n}. \tag{5.5} \]

Let

\[ a:=s_-+r-q. \]

Then

\[ |A_K|\ge \frac12 e^{an}. \]

Step 2: Sumset bound

For \(a=ub\in A_K\), we have

\[ |\sigma_j(a)| = |\sigma_j(u)||\sigma_j(b)| \le e^T R = e^{T+r}. \]

Therefore

\[ A_K+A_K \subset \{x\in\mathcal O_K:\|x\|_\infty\le 2e^{T+r}\}. \]

By Lemma 1,

\[ |A_K+A_K| \le (8e^{T+r})^n = e^{(r+T+\log 8)n}. \]

By condition (5.3),

\[ |A_K+A_K| \le e^{((2-\delta)a-\gamma)n}. \]

Using (5.5), for all sufficiently large \(n\),

\[ |A_K+A_K| \le |A_K|^{2-\delta}. \]

Step 3: Product-set bound

We have

\[ A_KA_K = (UB)(UB) \subset (UU)(BB). \]

Thus

\[ |A_KA_K| \le |UU|\cdot |BB|. \]

Since \(B\subset\{x:\|x\|_\infty\le R\}\), we have

\[ BB\subset \{x\in\mathcal O_K:\|x\|_\infty\le R^2\}. \]

By Lemma 1,

\[ |BB|\le (4R^2)^n=e^{(2r+\log 4)n}. \]

Also,

\[ |UU|\le e^{\kappa n}|U|\le e^{(\kappa+s_+)n}. \]

Hence

\[ |A_KA_K| \le e^{(2r+s_++\kappa+\log4)n}. \]

By condition (5.4),

\[ |A_KA_K| \le e^{((2-\delta)a-\gamma)n}. \]

Again using (5.5), for all sufficiently large \(n\),

\[ |A_KA_K| \le |A_K|^{2-\delta}. \]

Finally, embed \(K\hookrightarrow\mathbb R\) via \(\sigma_1\). Let

\[ A:=\sigma_1(A_K)\subset\mathbb R. \]

Since \(\sigma_1\) is injective and respects addition and multiplication,

\[ |A|=|A_K|,\qquad |A+A|=|A_K+A_K|,\qquad |AA|=|A_KA_K|. \]

Thus \(A\) satisfies the desired bounds. Taking the infinitely many fields \(K\) gives an infinite sequence \(A_i\) with \(|A_i|\to\infty\). ∎

6. Parameter feasibility

The proposition is useful only if the inequalities can be satisfied. They can, provided the unit entropy \(s_-\) beats the doubling cost \(\kappa\) and the root-discriminant cost \(2q\).

Let

\[ c_\Sigma:=\log 8,\qquad c_\Pi:=\log 4. \]

Suppose

\[ M:=2s_- -s_+ -\kappa-c_\Pi-2q>0. \tag{6.1} \]

This is the product-set slack at \(\delta=0\).

Choose, for example,

\[ r:=2T+2q+c_\Sigma. \]

Then \(r>T\), and the sumset slack at \(\delta=0\) is

\[ 2(s_-+r-q)-(r+T+c_\Sigma) = r+2s_- -2q-T-c_\Sigma = T+2s_->0. \]

The product-set slack at \(\delta=0\) is exactly \(M>0\).

Therefore we may choose

\[ 0<\delta< \frac12 \min\left( \frac{T+2s_-}{s_-+r-q}, \frac{M}{s_-+r-q} \right). \]

This gives a positive absolute \(\delta\), depending only on the constants in the construction.

Thus the whole approach reduces to proving the following kind of unit-selection statement:

Find totally real fields \(K\) of unbounded degree and bounded root discriminant, and inside each \(K\), find a set \(U\) of units of bounded logarithmic height, with small doubling, large size, and enough logarithmic separation, such that

\[ 2s_- -s_+ -\kappa -2q-O(1)>0. \]

7. Where should the unit sets come from?

The natural source is the logarithmic unit lattice.

Let

\[ \Lambda_K:=\lambda(\mathcal O_K^\times)\subset H_n. \]

By Dirichlet’s unit theorem, \(\Lambda_K\) is a lattice of rank \(n-1\). Its covolume is essentially the regulator \(R_K\), up to a factor polynomial in \(n\). Passing to totally positive units or squares changes this by at most \(2^n\), which is harmless at the exponential-in-\(n\) scale.

The plan is:

  1. Choose a sequence of totally real fields \(K_i\) with bounded root discriminant:

    \[ \Delta_{K_i}^{1/[K_i:\mathbb Q]}\le \rho \]

    for an absolute \(\rho\). Then

    \[ q_i=\frac1{2[K_i:\mathbb Q]}\log\Delta_{K_i} \le q:=\frac12\log\rho. \]
  2. Use an upper bound for the regulators \(R_{K_i}\) to show that the logarithmic unit lattices have covolume at most \(C^n\).

  3. Take a large cube in logarithmic space:

    \[ C_T:=\{x\in H_n:\|x\|_\infty\le T\}. \]

    Its \((n-1)\)-dimensional volume is \(\asymp_T T^{n-1}\), up to exponential constants.

  4. Since the unit lattice has covolume \(\le C^n\), one expects roughly

    \[ \left(\frac{T}{C}\right)^n \]

    units of height \(\le e^T\).

  5. Extract from those units a large subset \(U\) with:

    • small multiplicative doubling,
    • pairwise logarithmic separation \(\gg n\),
    • size still \(\exp(sn)\), with
    \[ s\approx \log T-O(\log\log T)-O_\rho(1). \]

  6. The doubling cost should be

    \[ \kappa\approx O(\log\log T)+O_\rho(1). \]

Then for \(T\) sufficiently large,

\[ s-\kappa-2q-O(1) \approx \log T-O(\log\log T)-2q-O(1)>0, \]

so condition (6.1) holds.

8. Standard arithmetic inputs

Two standard arithmetic facts support the plan.

Fact 1: Totally real bounded-root-discriminant towers exist

There are infinite sequences of totally real number fields \(K_i\) with

\[ [K_i:\mathbb Q]\to\infty \]

and

\[ \Delta_{K_i}^{1/[K_i:\mathbb Q]}\le \rho \]

for an absolute constant \(\rho\).

One way to obtain such a sequence is via Martinet’s construction of totally real fields with infinite class-field towers. In an unramified tower, the root discriminant stays constant, and if one uses the narrow Hilbert class field tower, total reality is preserved.

This is a known input, not a new obstruction.

Fact 2: Bounded root discriminant gives exponential regulator bound

Let \(K\) be totally real of degree \(n\), with \(\Delta_K^{1/n}\le \rho\). Then

\[ R_K\le C(\rho)^n. \]

Sketch proof

The analytic class-number formula for totally real \(K\) says

\[ \operatorname*{Res}_{s=1}\zeta_K(s) = \frac{2^{n-1}h_KR_K}{\sqrt{\Delta_K}}. \]

Since \(h_K\ge 1\),

\[ R_K \le 2^{1-n}\sqrt{\Delta_K}\operatorname*{Res}_{s=1}\zeta_K(s). \]

Landau’s residue bound gives, for \(n\ge 2\),

\[ \operatorname*{Res}_{s=1}\zeta_K(s) \le \left(\frac{e\log\Delta_K}{2(n-1)}\right)^{n-1}. \]

If \(\Delta_K^{1/n}\le\rho\), then \(\log\Delta_K\le n\log\rho\), so

\[ R_K \le 2^{1-n}\rho^{n/2} \left(\frac{e n\log\rho}{2(n-1)}\right)^{n-1} \le C(\rho)^n. \]

Thus the regulator has bounded exponential growth. ∎

This means the logarithmic unit lattice has covolume \(\le C(\rho)^n\), which is exactly what the unit-selection step needs.

9. The main remaining gap: unit-selection lemma

The serious remaining gap is the following.

Desired unit-selection lemma

Let \(K_i\) be a totally real bounded-root-discriminant tower, \(n_i=[K_i:\mathbb Q]\to\infty\). Let

\[ \Lambda_i:=\lambda(\mathcal O_{K_i}^{\times,+})\subset H_{n_i} \]

be the logarithmic lattice of totally positive units. Fix a sufficiently large constant \(T\).

Then there should exist constants \(s_-,s_+,\kappa,\eta>0\), with

\[ s_-\ge \log T-O(\log\log T)-O_\rho(1), \]
\[ s_+\le s_-+O(1), \]
\[ \kappa\le O(\log\log T)+O_\rho(1), \]

and

\[ \eta\asymp \log T, \]

such that for infinitely many \(i\), there is a finite set

\[ P_i\subset \Lambda_i\cap C_T \]

satisfying:

  1. Size:

    \[ e^{s_- n_i}\le |P_i|\le e^{s_+ n_i}. \]

  2. Small additive doubling in the log lattice:

    \[ |P_i+P_i|\le e^{\kappa n_i}|P_i|. \]

  3. Pairwise separation:

    \[ \|p-p'\|_1\ge \eta n_i \]

    for all distinct \(p,p'\in P_i\).

Then taking \(U_i\) to be the corresponding totally positive units gives the unit-set hypothesis.

10. Why the unit-selection lemma is plausible

Here is the heuristic and likely route.

The body

\[ C_T=\{x\in H_n:\|x\|_\infty\le T\} \]

has volume

\[ \operatorname{vol}_{H_n}(C_T) = \exp((\log T+O(1))n). \]

The unit lattice has covolume \(\le C(\rho)^n\), so the expected number of lattice points in \(C_T\) is at least

\[ \exp((\log T-O_\rho(1))n). \]

If we demand \(\ell^1\)-separation \(\eta n\), where \(\eta\asymp\log T\), then the volume of an \(\ell^1\)-ball of radius \(\eta n\) in dimension \(n-1\) is roughly

\[ \exp((\log\eta+O(1))n). \]

So a packing argument should lose only

\[ \exp((\log\eta+O(1))n) = \exp((\log\log T+O(1))n) \]

points.

Thus one expects a separated subset of size

\[ \exp((\log T-O(\log\log T)-O_\rho(1))n). \]

For small doubling, one can aim to choose \(P_i\) as a large chunk of a convex progression inside the lattice. Standard tools that may help here include:

  • discrete John-type theorems for lattice points in convex bodies;
  • geometry-of-numbers packing/covering estimates;
  • selection of a proper generalized arithmetic progression inside \(C_T\cap\Lambda_i\);
  • or a random thinning of \(C_T\cap\Lambda_i\), followed by a Balog–Szemerédi/Gowers-type extraction.

The doubling cost should then be of order

\[ \kappa=O(\log\eta)+O_\rho(1)=O(\log\log T)+O_\rho(1). \]

Since \(q=\frac12\log\rho\) is fixed, taking \(T\) extremely large makes

\[ s_- -\kappa-2q-O(1)>0. \]

This is precisely the positivity needed in condition (6.1).

11. Why the unit-selection lemma is also the serious obstruction

The construction depends crucially on having \(|UB|\approx |U||B|\). This fails if many units in \(U\) have very similar logarithmic embeddings, because then the dilates \(uB\) overlap heavily.

The pairwise separation condition prevents this. Without it, a large cluster of near-identical units could cause

\[ |UB|\ll |U||B|, \]

destroying the entropy gain from \(U\).

Thus the real difficulty is not the sumset or product-set counting after \(U\) is found; those parts are robust. The hard part is proving that the logarithmic unit lattice contains a large enough subset that is simultaneously:

  1. bounded in \(\ell^\infty\) norm by \(T\);
  2. exponentially large;
  3. small-doubling;
  4. separated at scale \(\eta n\) in \(\ell^1\).

This is the main point for the next round.

12. A weaker alternative to separation: weighted energy

The separation condition can likely be weakened. In the proof of the union lower bound, what we actually used was that the weighted overlap sum is small. Define

\[ E_{\mathrm{log}}(U) := \sum_{\substack{u,v\in U\\u\neq v}} \exp\left(-\frac12\|\lambda(u)-\lambda(v)\|_1\right). \]

The same argument works if instead of pairwise separation we can prove

\[ E_{\mathrm{log}}(U) \le e^{-(q+\log4+\gamma)n}|U|. \tag{12.1} \]

Indeed, Lemma 3 gives

\[ \sum_{\substack{u,v\in U\\u\neq v}}|uB\cap vB| \le e^{(q+\log4)n}|B|E_{\mathrm{log}}(U), \]

so (12.1) implies

\[ \sum_{u,v}|uB\cap vB| \le (1+e^{-\gamma n})|U||B|. \]

Then the same Cauchy–Schwarz argument yields

\[ |UB|\ge (1-o(1))|U||B|. \]

Thus an alternative target is:

Find \(U\) of bounded height and small doubling such that its weighted logarithmic energy \(E_{\mathrm{log}}(U)\) is small.

This may be easier than enforcing hard pairwise separation, because it allows some close pairs as long as they are sparse.

13. Summary of the proof plan

The plan is now reduced to the following sequence of tasks.

Step 1: Choose a bounded-root-discriminant totally real tower

Use a Martinet-type tower:

\[ K_i,\qquad [K_i:\mathbb Q]=n_i\to\infty, \]

with

\[ \Delta_{K_i}^{1/n_i}\le \rho. \]

Set

\[ q:=\frac12\log\rho. \]

This is standard.

Step 2: Control regulators

Use Landau’s residue bound and the analytic class-number formula to prove

\[ R_{K_i}\le C(\rho)^{n_i}. \]

This is essentially proved above.

Step 3: Prove the unit-selection lemma

For a sufficiently large fixed \(T\), prove that the logarithmic unit lattice contains \(P_i\subset C_T\) such that:

\[ |P_i|\ge e^{s_-n_i}, \]
\[ |P_i+P_i|\le e^{\kappa n_i}|P_i|, \]
\[ \|p-p'\|_1\ge \eta n_i, \]

with

\[ 2s_- -s_+ -\kappa-2q-O(1)>0. \]

This is the main remaining gap.

A plausible quantitative target is

\[ s_-\ge \log T-C_1\log\log T-C_2(\rho), \]
\[ \kappa\le C_1\log\log T+C_2(\rho), \]

and

\[ \eta\asymp \log T. \]

Then choose \(T\) so large that

\[ s_- -\kappa-2q-O(1)>0. \]

Step 4: Build \(B_i\)

The key additional observation is the following:

1. Standard arithmetic inputs

We shall use three standard theorems.

1.1. Totally real bounded-root-discriminant towers

There exists an absolute constant \(\rho>1\) and an infinite sequence of totally real number fields

\[ K_i/\mathbb Q \]

with degrees

\[ n_i=[K_i:\mathbb Q]\to\infty \]

such that

\[ |\Delta_{K_i}|^{1/n_i}\le \rho \]

for all \(i\).

This is a standard consequence of Martinet’s construction of totally real number fields with infinite class-field towers. Passing up an unramified totally real tower preserves the root discriminant.

Fix such a tower once and for all, and put

\[ q:=\frac12\log \rho. \]

Thus for every field \(K=K_i\) in the tower,

\[ \frac1{2n}\log |\Delta_K|\le q. \]

1.2. Regulator upper bound in bounded-root-discriminant towers

Let \(K\) be totally real of degree \(n\), and let \(R_K\) denote its regulator. Since

\[ |\Delta_K|^{1/n}\le \rho, \]

there is a constant \(C_{\mathrm{reg}}=C_{\mathrm{reg}}(\rho)>0\) such that

\[ R_K\le e^{C_{\mathrm{reg}}n}. \]

Indeed, the analytic class-number formula gives

\[ \operatorname*{Res}_{s=1}\zeta_K(s) = \frac{2^{n-1}h_KR_K}{|\Delta_K|^{1/2}}. \]

Since \(h_K\ge 1\),

\[ R_K \le 2^{1-n}|\Delta_K|^{1/2}\operatorname*{Res}_{s=1}\zeta_K(s). \]

By Landau’s residue bound,

\[ \operatorname*{Res}_{s=1}\zeta_K(s) \le \left( \frac{e\log |\Delta_K|}{2(n-1)} \right)^{n-1}. \]

Since \(\log |\Delta_K|\le n\log\rho\), this is \(\le C(\rho)^n\). Hence \(R_K\le e^{C_{\mathrm{reg}}n}\).

Let

\[ E_K^+:=\{u\in\mathcal O_K^\times:\sigma_j(u)>0\text{ for every real embedding }\sigma_j\} \]

be the group of totally positive units. Its logarithmic embedding is

\[ \lambda:E_K^+\to H_n, \qquad \lambda(u):=(\log\sigma_1(u),\dots,\log\sigma_n(u)), \]

where

\[ H_n:=\left\{x\in\mathbb R^n:\sum_{j=1}^n x_j=0\right\}. \]

The image

\[ \Lambda_K^+:=\lambda(E_K^+) \]

is a full lattice in \(H_n\) of rank \(n-1\). Since \(E_K^+\) has index at most \(2^n\) in \(\mathcal O_K^\times\), and since the Euclidean covolume of the full logarithmic unit lattice is \(\sqrt n\,R_K\), there is a constant \(C_\Lambda=C_\Lambda(\rho)>0\) such that

\[ \operatorname{covol}_{H_n}(\Lambda_K^+)\le e^{C_\Lambda n} \tag{1.1} \]

for every field in the tower.

1.3. Schinzel’s height gap for totally real units

We shall use the following theorem of Schinzel.

Schinzel height gap. There exists an absolute constant \(c_{\mathrm{Sch}}>0\) such that if \(\alpha\) is a totally real algebraic integer with \(\alpha\ne 0,\pm1\), then its absolute logarithmic Weil height satisfies

\[ h(\alpha)\ge c_{\mathrm{Sch}}. \]

For a unit \(u\in E_K^+\), we have

\[ h(u) = \frac1n\sum_{j=1}^n \log^+|\sigma_j(u)|. \]

Also, because \(u\) is a unit,

\[ \sum_{j=1}^n \log|\sigma_j(u)|=0. \]

Therefore

\[ \|\lambda(u)\|_1 = \sum_{j=1}^n |\log|\sigma_j(u)|| = 2\sum_{j=1}^n \log^+|\sigma_j(u)| = 2nh(u). \]

Thus, for every nontrivial \(u\in E_K^+\),

\[ \|\lambda(u)\|_1\ge \mu n, \tag{1.2} \]

where

\[ \mu:=2c_{\mathrm{Sch}}>0. \]

Consequently, for distinct \(p,q\in \Lambda_K^+\),

\[ \|p-q\|_1\ge \mu n. \tag{1.3} \]

2. Geometric lemmas for the logarithmic unit lattice

For \(L>0\), define

\[ C_L:=\{x\in H_n:\|x\|_\infty\le L\}. \]

We need three elementary geometric facts.

Lemma 2.1: Volume of \(C_L\)

There is an absolute constant \(c_{\square}>0\) such that for all \(n\ge2\) and \(L>0\),

\[ \operatorname{vol}_{H_n}(C_L)\ge (c_{\square}L)^{n-1}. \]

More precisely,

\[ \operatorname{vol}_{H_n}(C_L)\ge \frac{(2L)^{n-1}}{\sqrt n}. \]

Proof

Let

\[ F_t:=\left\{x\in[-1,1]^n:\sum_{j=1}^n x_j=t\right\}. \]

Write

\[ f(t):=\operatorname{vol}_{n-1}(F_t). \]

By the Brunn–Minkowski theorem, \(f(t)^{1/(n-1)}\) is concave on \([-n,n]\). Also \(f(t)=f(-t)\), so \(f(0)\) is maximal.

By the coarea formula,

\[ 2^n = \operatorname{vol}_n([-1,1]^n) = \frac1{\sqrt n}\int_{-n}^n f(t)\,dt \le \frac{2n}{\sqrt n}f(0) = 2\sqrt n\, f(0). \]

Therefore

\[ f(0)\ge \frac{2^{n-1}}{\sqrt n}. \]

But \(C_1=F_0\). Hence

\[ \operatorname{vol}_{H_n}(C_1)\ge \frac{2^{n-1}}{\sqrt n}. \]

Scaling by \(L\) in dimension \(n-1\) gives

\[ \operatorname{vol}_{H_n}(C_L) = L^{n-1}\operatorname{vol}_{H_n}(C_1) \ge \frac{(2L)^{n-1}}{\sqrt n}. \]

∎

Lemma 2.2: Covering a dilate of a convex body

Let \(V\) be a real vector space of dimension \(d\), and let \(K\subset V\) be a centrally symmetric convex body. Then \(4K\) can be covered by at most \(9^d\) translates of \(K\).

Proof

Choose a maximal subset \(Y\subset 4K\) such that the translates

\[ y+\frac12K,\qquad y\in Y, \]

are pairwise disjoint. Then these disjoint translates are all contained in

\[ 4K+\frac12K=\frac92K. \]

Thus

\[ |Y|\operatorname{vol}\left(\frac12K\right) \le \operatorname{vol}\left(\frac92K\right). \]

Since \(\dim V=d\),

\[ |Y|\le \left(\frac{9/2}{1/2}\right)^d=9^d. \]

By maximality, \(4K\subset Y+K\). ∎

Lemma 2.3: Packing in the logarithmic unit lattice

Let \(K\) be one of the fields in the tower, of degree \(n\), and let

\[ \Lambda:=\Lambda_K^+\subset H_n. \]

Then every \(\ell^1\)-ball in \(H_n\) of radius \(\eta n\) contains at most

\[ \left(1+\frac{2\eta}{\mu}\right)^{n-1} \]

points of any coset of \(\Lambda\).

Proof

By (1.3), distinct points of a coset of \(\Lambda\) are separated by at least \(\mu n\) in \(\ell^1\). Therefore, if

\[ z_1,\dots,z_m \]

are points of a coset of \(\Lambda\) lying in an \(\ell^1\)-ball of radius \(\eta n\), then the balls

\[ B_1\left(z_i,\frac{\mu n}{2}\right) \]

are pairwise disjoint. They are all contained in an \(\ell^1\)-ball of radius

\[ \left(\eta+\frac{\mu}{2}\right)n. \]

Since \(\ell^1\)-balls in the \((n-1)\)-dimensional space \(H_n\) scale like radius to the power \(n-1\),

\[ m\left(\frac{\mu n}{2}\right)^{n-1} \le \left(\left(\eta+\frac{\mu}{2}\right)n\right)^{n-1}. \]

Hence

\[ m \le \left(1+\frac{2\eta}{\mu}\right)^{n-1}. \]

∎

3. The unit-selection lemma

This is the main point. It closes the gap from the previous proof plan.

Proposition 3.1: Many bounded-height units with small doubling and separation

There exist constants \(C_0,C_1>0\), depending only on the fixed tower, such that the following holds.

Let \(T\ge 2\) and \(\eta>0\). For every sufficiently large field \(K\) in the tower, of degree \(n\), there exists a finite set

\[ U\subset E_K^+ \]

such that:

  1. Bounded logarithmic height:

    \[ |\log\sigma_j(u)|\le T \]

    for every \(u\in U\) and every \(j=1,\dots,n\).

  2. Size lower bound:

    \[ |U|\ge \exp\left( \left(\log T-C_0-\log\left(1+\frac{2\eta}{\mu}\right)\right)n \right). \tag{3.1} \]

  3. Size upper bound:

    \[ |U|\le \exp\left( (\log T+C_1)n \right). \tag{3.2} \]

  4. Small multiplicative doubling:

    \[ |UU| \le \exp\left( \left(\log 9+\log\left(1+\frac{2\eta}{\mu}\right)\right)n \right)|U|. \tag{3.3} \]

  5. Pairwise logarithmic separation: for all distinct \(u,v\in U\),

    \[ \|\lambda(u)-\lambda(v)\|_1\ge \eta n. \tag{3.4} \]

Proof

Let

\[ \Lambda:=\Lambda_K^+\subset H_n. \]

Set

\[ d:=n-1. \]

Define

\[ M:=\max_{x\in H_n}|\Lambda\cap(x+C_{T/2})|. \]

By Blichfeldt’s averaging principle,

\[ M\ge \frac{\operatorname{vol}_{H_n}(C_{T/2})}{\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda)}. \]

Using Lemma 2.1 and (1.1),

\[ \operatorname{vol}_{H_n}(C_{T/2}) \ge \frac{T^{n-1}}{\sqrt n}, \]

and

\[ \operatorname{covol}(\Lambda)\le e^{C_\Lambda n}. \]

Thus, after increasing \(C_0\) if necessary and discarding finitely many fields depending on \(T\),

\[ M\ge \exp((\log T-C_0)n). \tag{3.5} \]

Choose a translate \(x+C_{T/2}\) attaining \(M\), and write

\[ S:=\Lambda\cap(x+C_{T/2}). \]

Fix \(\lambda_0\in S\), and define

\[ P_0:=S-\lambda_0 = \{\lambda-\lambda_0:\lambda\in S\}. \]

Then

\[ P_0\subset \Lambda\cap C_T, \]

and

\[ |P_0|=M. \]

Small doubling for \(P_0\)

Since \(P_0\subset C_T\),

\[ P_0+P_0\subset \Lambda\cap C_{2T}. \]

But

\[ C_{2T}=4C_{T/2}. \]

By Lemma 2.2, \(C_{2T}\) can be covered by at most \(9^d\) translates of \(C_{T/2}\). Each translate contains at most \(M\) points of \(\Lambda\), by the definition of \(M\). Therefore

\[ |P_0+P_0|\le 9^dM. \tag{3.6} \]

Extracting a separated subset

Let \(P\subset P_0\) be a maximal subset with pairwise \(\ell^1\)-distances at least \(\eta n\). Then the \(\ell^1\)-balls of radius \(\eta n\) around points of \(P\) cover \(P_0\). By Lemma 2.3, each such ball contains at most

\[ L_\eta:= \left(1+\frac{2\eta}{\mu}\right)^d \]

points of \(P_0\). Hence

\[ |P|\ge \frac{|P_0|}{L_\eta} = \frac{M}{L_\eta}. \]

Using (3.5),

\[ |P| \ge \exp((\log T-C_0)n) \cdot \exp\left(-\log\left(1+\frac{2\eta}{\mu}\right)d\right). \]

Since \(d\le n\), this gives

\[ |P|\ge \exp\left( \left(\log T-C_0-\log\left(1+\frac{2\eta}{\mu}\right)\right)n \right). \tag{3.7} \]

For an upper bound, note that \(P\subset P_0\subset C_T\), so \(P_0\) is contained in the \(\ell^1\)-ball of radius \(Tn\) around \(0\). By Lemma 2.3,

\[ |P|\le |P_0|\le \left(1+\frac{2T}{\mu}\right)^d. \]

For \(T\ge2\), there is a constant \(C_1\), depending only on \(\mu\), such that

\[ \left(1+\frac{2T}{\mu}\right)^d \le \exp((\log T+C_1)n). \tag{3.8} \]

Finally, from (3.6),

\[ |P+P| \le |P_0+P_0| \le 9^dM \le 9^dL_\eta |P|. \]

Therefore

\[ |P+P| \le \exp\left( \left(\log 9+\log\left(1+\frac{2\eta}{\mu}\right)\right)n \right)|P|. \tag{3.9} \]

Because \(\lambda:E_K^+\to \Lambda_K^+\) is an isomorphism, let \(U\subset E_K^+\) be the unique set of totally positive units satisfying

\[ \lambda(U)=P. \]

Then (3.7), (3.8), and (3.9) give (3.1), (3.2), and (3.3). Also \(P\subset C_T\), so every \(u\in U\) satisfies

\[ |\log\sigma_j(u)|\le T \]

for all \(j\). Pairwise separation follows from the construction of \(P\). ∎

4. Algebraic-integer counting lemmas

We now record the elementary estimates used to control sumsets and product sets.

Let \(K\) be totally real of degree \(n\), with embeddings

\[ \sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_n:K\hookrightarrow\mathbb R. \]

For \(x\in K\), write

\[ \|x\|_\infty:=\max_{1\le j\le n}|\sigma_j(x)|. \]

Lemma 4.1: Algebraic integers in boxes

Let \(X_1,\dots,X_n\ge1\). Then

\[ \left| \left\{ a\in\mathcal O_K: |\sigma_j(a)|\le X_j\text{ for all }j \right\} \right| \le \prod_{j=1}^n 4X_j. \]

In particular,

\[ |\{a\in\mathcal O_K:\|a\|_\infty\le X\}| \le (4X)^n \]

for \(X\ge1\).

Proof

Partition each interval \([-X_j,X_j]\) into at most \(4X_j\) half-open intervals of length \(<1\). This partitions the box

\[ \prod_{j=1}^n[-X_j,X_j] \]

into at most \(\prod_j 4X_j\) smaller boxes, each of side length \(<1\).

If two distinct algebraic integers \(a,b\in\mathcal O_K\) lie in the same small box, then

\[ |\sigma_j(a-b)|<1 \]

for every \(j\). Since \(a-b\neq0\) is an algebraic integer,

\[ N_{K/\mathbb Q}(a-b)=\prod_{j=1}^n\sigma_j(a-b) \]

is a nonzero integer. But

\[ |N_{K/\mathbb Q}(a-b)| = \prod_{j=1}^n|\sigma_j(a-b)|<1, \]

impossible. Thus each small box contains at most one algebraic integer. ∎

Lemma 4.2: A large bounded additive reservoir

Let \(R\ge1\). There exists a finite set

\[ B\subset\mathcal O_K \]

such that

  1. \(\|b\|_\infty\le R\) for every \(b\in B\);
  2. \[ |B|\ge R^n|\Delta_K|^{-1/2}. \]

Proof

Let

\[ \sigma:\mathcal O_K\hookrightarrow\mathbb R^n \]

be the Minkowski embedding. Since \(K\) is totally real, the lattice \(\sigma(\mathcal O_K)\) has covolume

\[ |\Delta_K|^{1/2}. \]

Let

\[ Q:=[0,R)^n. \]

By Blichfeldt’s theorem, some translate \(x+Q\) contains at least

\[ \frac{\operatorname{vol}(Q)}{|\Delta_K|^{1/2}} = R^n|\Delta_K|^{-1/2} \]

points of \(\sigma(\mathcal O_K)\).

Let those lattice points be

\[ y_1,\dots,y_m\in \sigma(\mathcal O_K), \qquad m\ge R^n|\Delta_K|^{-1/2}. \]

Define

\[ B:=\{\sigma^{-1}(y_i-y_1):1\le i\le m\}. \]

Then \(B\subset\mathcal O_K\), \(|B|=m\), and since \(y_i,y_1\in x+Q\),

\[ y_i-y_1\in Q-Q\subset[-R,R]^n. \]

Hence \(\|b\|_\infty\le R\) for every \(b\in B\). ∎

Lemma 4.3: Intersections of unit-dilates of \(B\)

Let \(B\subset\mathcal O_K\) satisfy

\[ \|b\|_\infty\le R \]

for every \(b\in B\). Let \(u,v\in\mathcal O_K^\times\) be units satisfying

\[ |\log|\sigma_j(u)||\le T, \qquad |\log|\sigma_j(v)||\le T \]

for every \(j\). Assume

\[ R e^{-T}\ge 1. \]

Then

\[ |uB\cap vB| \le (4R)^n \exp\left(-\frac12\|\lambda(u)-\lambda(v)\|_1\right). \]

Proof

If \(x\in uB\cap vB\), then

\[ x=ub_1=vb_2 \]

for some \(b_1,b_2\in B\). Therefore

\[ |\sigma_j(x)| \le R\min(|\sigma_j(u)|,|\sigma_j(v)|) \]

for every \(j\).

Since \(R e^{-T}\ge1\), every radius

\[ R\min(|\sigma_j(u)|,|\sigma_j(v)|) \]

is at least \(1\). By Lemma 4.1,

\[ |uB\cap vB| \le \prod_{j=1}^n 4R\min(|\sigma_j(u)|,|\sigma_j(v)|). \]

Write

\[ a_j:=\log|\sigma_j(u)|, \qquad b_j:=\log|\sigma_j(v)|. \]

Because \(u\) and \(v\) are units,

\[ \sum_j a_j=\sum_j b_j=0. \]

Also,

\[ \log\min(e^{a_j},e^{b_j}) = \frac{a_j+b_j-|a_j-b_j|}{2}. \]

Thus

\[ \sum_{j=1}^n \log\min(|\sigma_j(u)|,|\sigma_j(v)|) = -\frac12\sum_{j=1}^n|a_j-b_j| = -\frac12\|\lambda(u)-\lambda(v)\|_1. \]

Hence

\[ \prod_{j=1}^n\min(|\sigma_j(u)|,|\sigma_j(v)|) = \exp\left(-\frac12\|\lambda(u)-\lambda(v)\|_1\right). \]

The desired bound follows. ∎

\[ \sum_x m(x)^2 = \sum_{u,v\in U}|uB\cap vB|. \tag{7.2} \]

If \(u=v\), then

\[ |uB\cap vB|=|B|. \]

Suppose \(u\ne v\). Since \(r>T\), we have

\[ Re^{-T}\ge1. \]

Therefore Lemma 4.3 applies. Using (6.5),

\[ |uB\cap vB| \le (4R)^n e^{-\eta n/2}. \]

Using (6.7),

\[ R^n \le e^{qn}|B|. \]

Thus

\[ |uB\cap vB| \le e^{(\log4+q-\eta/2)n}|B|. \tag{7.3} \]

By (5.5),

\[ \eta/2-s_+-q-\log4 = \frac12\log T+1. \]

Set

\[ \gamma:=\frac12\log T+1>0. \]

Then (7.3) becomes

\[ |uB\cap vB| \le e^{-(s_++\gamma)n}|B|. \tag{7.4} \]

Therefore

\[ \sum_x m(x)^2 \le |U||B| + |U|^2 e^{-(s_++\gamma)n}|B|. \]

Using \(|U|\le e^{s_+n}\),

\[ |U|^2 e^{-(s_++\gamma)n}|B| \le e^{-\gamma n}|U||B|. \]

Hence

\[ \sum_x m(x)^2 \le (1+e^{-\gamma n})|U||B|. \tag{7.5} \]

By Cauchy–Schwarz,

\[ |A_K| = |\operatorname{supp} m| \ge \frac{(\sum_x m(x))^2}{\sum_x m(x)^2}. \]

Using (7.1) and (7.5),

\[ |A_K| \ge \frac{|U|^2|B|^2}{(1+e^{-\gamma n})|U||B|} = \frac{|U||B|}{1+e^{-\gamma n}}. \]

For all sufficiently large \(n\),

\[ |A_K|\ge \frac12|U||B|. \]

Combining this with (6.2) and (6.7),

\[ |A_K| \ge \frac12 e^{(s_-+r-q)n}. \]

Recalling \(a=s_-+r-q\), we have

\[ |A_K|\ge \frac12 e^{an}. \tag{7.6} \]

8. Sumset bound

Let \(x\in A_K\). Then \(x=ub\) for some \(u\in U\), \(b\in B\). By (6.1) and (6.6),

\[ |\sigma_j(x)| = \sigma_j(u)|\sigma_j(b)| \le e^T R = e^{T+r} \]

for every \(j\).

Therefore, if \(x,y\in A_K\),

\[ |\sigma_j(x+y)| \le 2e^{T+r}. \]

Thus

\[ A_K+A_K \subset \{z\in\mathcal O_K:\|z\|_\infty\le 2e^{T+r}\}. \]

By Lemma 4.1,

\[ |A_K+A_K| \le (8e^{T+r})^n = e^{(r+T+\log8)n}. \tag{8.1} \]

By definition,

\[ S_\Sigma = 2a-(r+T+\log8). \]

Thus

\[ r+T+\log8 = 2a-S_\Sigma \le 2a-S. \]

Therefore

\[ |A_K+A_K| \le e^{(2a-S)n}. \tag{8.2} \]

9. Product-set bound

We have

\[ A_KA_K = (UB)(UB) \subset (UU)(BB). \]

Hence

\[ |A_KA_K| \le |UU|\,|BB|. \tag{9.1} \]

\[ |UU| \le e^{\kappa n}|U| \le e^{(\kappa+s_+)n}. \tag{9.2} \]

Since every \(b\in B\) satisfies \(\|b\|_\infty\le R\), every element of \(BB\) satisfies \(\|\cdot\|_\infty\le R^2\). Thus Lemma 4.1 gives

\[ |BB|\le (4R^2)^n=e^{(2r+\log4)n}. \tag{9.3} \]

Combining (9.1), (9.2), and (9.3),

\[ |A_KA_K| \le e^{(2r+s_++\kappa+\log4)n}. \tag{9.4} \]

Now

\[ S_\Pi = 2s_- -s_+-\kappa-2q-\log4. \]

Using \(a=s_-+r-q\),

\[ 2a = 2s_-+2r-2q. \]

Therefore

\[ 2a-S_\Pi = 2r+s_++\kappa+\log4. \]

So (9.4) becomes

\[ |A_KA_K| \le e^{(2a-S_\Pi)n} \le e^{(2a-S)n}. \tag{9.5} \]

10. Comparison with \(|A_K|^{2-\delta}\)

From (7.6),

\[ |A_K|\ge \frac12e^{an}. \]

Therefore

\[ |A_K|^{2-\delta} \ge 2^{-(2-\delta)}e^{(2-\delta)an}. \tag{10.1} \]

By our definition

\[ \delta=\frac{S}{4a}, \]

so

\[ (2-\delta)a = 2a-\frac S4. \]

Meanwhile, both (8.2) and (9.5) give the stronger bound

\[ |A_K+A_K|,\ |A_KA_K| \le e^{(2a-S)n}. \]

Since

\[ 2a-S = (2-\delta)a-\frac{3S}{4}, \]

we have

\[ e^{(2a-S)n} = e^{((2-\delta)a-3S/4)n}. \]

For all sufficiently large \(n\), the exponential saving \(e^{-3Sn/4}\) dominates the fixed factor \(2^{-(2-\delta)}\) in (10.1). Hence, for all sufficiently large \(n\),

\[ |A_K+A_K| \le |A_K|^{2-\delta}, \]

and

\[ |A_KA_K| \le |A_K|^{2-\delta}. \]

11. Embedding into \(\mathbb R\)

Finally choose one real embedding

Because \(\sigma_1\) is an injective field homomorphism,

\[ |A|=|A_K|, \]
\[ |A+A|=|A_K+A_K|, \]

and

Applying this construction to all sufficiently large fields \(K_i\) in the tower gives an infinite sequence

\[ A_i\subset\mathbb R. \]

Since

\[ |A_i|\ge \frac12 e^{a n_i} \]

and \(a>0\), while \(n_i\to\infty\), we have

This proves the theorem. ∎

Proof

1. Uniform separation of totally positive units

We prove an elementary separation lemma.

Lemma 1.1

There exists an absolute constant \(\mu>0\) such that for every totally real field \(K\) of degree \(n\), and every nontrivial totally positive unit \(w\in E_K^+\), one has

\[ \|\lambda(w)\|_1\ge \mu n. \]

Consequently, for distinct \(u,v\in E_K^+\),

\[ \|\lambda(u)-\lambda(v)\|_1\ge \mu n. \]

Proof

Choose \(\mu>0\) so small that

\[ \mu e^\mu<1. \]

For example, \(\mu=1/4\) works.

Let

\[ x_j:=\log\sigma_j(w). \]

Then

\[ \sigma_j(w)=e^{x_j}. \]

Since \(w\neq1\), the algebraic integer \(w-1\) is nonzero. Hence

\[ |N_{K/\mathbb Q}(w-1)| = \prod_{j=1}^n |e^{x_j}-1| \ge 1. \]

On the other hand,

\[ |e^x-1|\le e^{|x|}|x| \]

for every real \(x\). Thus, writing

\[ S:=\sum_{j=1}^n |x_j|=\|\lambda(w)\|_1, \]

we get

\[ 1 \le \prod_{j=1}^n |e^{x_j}-1| \le e^S\prod_{j=1}^n |x_j| \le e^S\left(\frac Sn\right)^n = \left(e^{S/n}\frac Sn\right)^n. \]

If \(S\le \mu n\), then

\[ \left(e^{S/n}\frac Sn\right)^n \le (\mu e^\mu)^n<1, \]

a contradiction. Therefore \(S>\mu n\), proving the claim.

For distinct \(u,v\in E_K^+\), apply this to \(w=uv^{-1}\neq1\). ∎

2. Geometry of the logarithmic lattice

For \(L>0\), define

\[ C_L:=\{x\in H_n:\|x\|_\infty\le L\}. \]

We need three elementary facts.

Lemma 2.1

There is an absolute constant \(c>0\) such that

\[ \operatorname{vol}_{H_n}(C_L)\ge (cL)^{n-1}. \]

In particular,

\[ \operatorname{vol}_{H_n}(C_L)\ge \frac{(2L)^{n-1}}{\sqrt n}. \]

Proof

For \(L=1\), \(C_1\) is the central hyperplane section of the cube \([-1,1]^n\). By the coarea formula,

\[ 2^n = \frac1{\sqrt n}\int_{-n}^n \operatorname{vol}_{n-1}\{x\in[-1,1]^n:\sum x_j=t\}\,dt. \]

The central section is maximal by Brunn–Minkowski symmetry, so

\[ 2^n \le \frac{2n}{\sqrt n}\operatorname{vol}_{H_n}(C_1) = 2\sqrt n\,\operatorname{vol}_{H_n}(C_1). \]

Hence

\[ \operatorname{vol}_{H_n}(C_1)\ge \frac{2^{n-1}}{\sqrt n}. \]

Scaling gives the result. ∎

Lemma 2.2

If \(K\) is a centrally symmetric convex body in a real vector space of dimension \(d\), then \(4K\) can be covered by at most \(9^d\) translates of \(K\).

Proof

Take a maximal set \(Y\subset4K\) such that the translates

\[ y+\frac12K,\qquad y\in Y, \]

are pairwise disjoint. These translates lie inside

\[ 4K+\frac12K=\frac92K. \]

Therefore

\[ |Y|\operatorname{vol}\left(\frac12K\right) \le \operatorname{vol}\left(\frac92K\right), \]

so

\[ |Y|\le 9^d. \]

By maximality,

\[ 4K\subset Y+K. \]

∎

Lemma 2.3

Let \(\Lambda=\Lambda_K^+\subset H_n\). Every \(\ell^1\)-ball in \(H_n\) of radius \(\eta n\) contains at most

\[ \left(1+\frac{2\eta}{\mu}\right)^{n-1} \]

points of any coset of \(\Lambda\).

Proof

Distinct points in a coset of \(\Lambda\) differ by a nonzero element of \(\Lambda\), so by Lemma 1.1 they are separated by at least \(\mu n\) in \(\ell^1\).

If \(m\) such points lie in an \(\ell^1\)-ball of radius \(\eta n\), then the \(\ell^1\)-balls of radius \(\mu n/2\) around them are disjoint and lie inside an \(\ell^1\)-ball of radius

\[ \eta n+\frac{\mu n}{2}. \]

Since volumes scale by the \((n-1)\)-st power in \(H_n\),

\[ m\le \left( \frac{\eta+\mu/2}{\mu/2} \right)^{n-1} = \left(1+\frac{2\eta}{\mu}\right)^{n-1}. \]

∎

3. Construction of many bounded-height units

Fix \(T\ge2\) and \(\eta>0\). Let

\[ D_\eta:=\log\left(1+\frac{2\eta}{\mu}\right). \]

Proposition 3.1

For all sufficiently large fields \(K\) in the tower, there exists a finite set

\[ U\subset E_K^+ \]

such that:

  1. for all \(u\in U\) and all embeddings \(\sigma_j\),

    \[ |\log\sigma_j(u)|\le T; \]

  2. for constants \(C_0,C_1\) independent of \(T,\eta,K\),

    \[ |U|\ge \exp\bigl((\log T-C_0-D_\eta)n\bigr); \tag{2} \]

  3. also

    \[ |U|\le \exp\bigl((\log T+C_1)n\bigr); \tag{3} \]

  4. and

    \[ |UU|\le \exp\bigl((\log9+D_\eta)n\bigr)|U|; \tag{4} \]

  5. moreover, for distinct \(u,v\in U\),

    \[ \|\lambda(u)-\lambda(v)\|_1\ge \eta n. \tag{5} \]

Proof

Let

\[ \Lambda:=\Lambda_K^+\subset H_n. \]

Define

\[ M:=\max_{x\in H_n}|\Lambda\cap(x+C_{T/2})|. \]

By Blichfeldt’s theorem,

\[ M\ge \frac{\operatorname{vol}_{H_n}(C_{T/2})}{\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda)}. \]

Using Lemma 2.1 and (1),

\[ M \ge \frac{T^{n-1}/\sqrt n}{e^{C_\Lambda n}}. \]

For fixed \(T\), after discarding finitely many fields, this gives

\[ M\ge \exp((\log T-C_0)n) \tag{6} \]

for some constant \(C_0\).

Choose \(x\) attaining \(M\), and set

\[ S:=\Lambda\cap(x+C_{T/2}). \]

Choose \(\lambda_0\in S\), and define

\[ P_0:=S-\lambda_0. \]

Then

\[ P_0\subset \Lambda\cap C_T \]

and

\[ |P_0|=M. \]

Now

\[ P_0+P_0\subset \Lambda\cap C_{2T}. \]

But

\[ C_{2T}=4C_{T/2}. \]

By Lemma 2.2, \(C_{2T}\) can be covered by at most \(9^{n-1}\) translates of \(C_{T/2}\). Each translate contains at most \(M\) lattice points by the definition of \(M\). Hence

\[ |P_0+P_0|\le 9^{n-1}M. \tag{7} \]

Next choose a maximal subset

\[ P\subset P_0 \]

with pairwise \(\ell^1\)-distances at least \(\eta n\). Then the \(\ell^1\)-balls of radius \(\eta n\) around \(P\) cover \(P_0\). By Lemma 2.3, each such ball contains at most

\[ \left(1+\frac{2\eta}{\mu}\right)^{n-1} \]

points of \(P_0\). Therefore

\[ |P| \ge \frac{M}{(1+2\eta/\mu)^{n-1}} \ge \exp\bigl((\log T-C_0-D_\eta)n\bigr). \]

Also \(P\subset C_T\), so \(P\) lies inside an \(\ell^1\)-ball of radius \(Tn\). By Lemma 2.3,

\[ |P|\le \left(1+\frac{2T}{\mu}\right)^{n-1} \le \exp((\log T+C_1)n) \]

for some absolute \(C_1\).

Finally,

\[ |P+P| \le |P_0+P_0| \le 9^{n-1}M \le 9^{n-1} \left(1+\frac{2\eta}{\mu}\right)^{n-1}|P|. \]

Thus

\[ |P+P| \le \exp((\log9+D_\eta)n)|P|. \]

Since \(\lambda:E_K^+\to\Lambda_K^+\) is injective, let \(U\subset E_K^+\) be the unique set with

\[ \lambda(U)=P. \]

Then all desired properties follow. ∎

4. Algebraic-integer counting lemmas

For \(x\in K\), write

\[ \|x\|_\infty:=\max_j|\sigma_j(x)|. \]

Lemma 4.1

If \(X_1,\dots,X_n\ge1\), then

\[ \left| \{a\in\mathcal O_K:|\sigma_j(a)|\le X_j\text{ for all }j\} \right| \le \prod_{j=1}^n 4X_j. \]

Proof

Partition each interval \([-X_j,X_j]\) into at most \(4X_j\) intervals of length \(<1\). If two distinct algebraic integers \(a,b\) lie in the same small box, then

\[ |\sigma_j(a-b)|<1 \]

for all \(j\). Hence

\[ 0<|N_{K/\mathbb Q}(a-b)|<1, \]

impossible because the norm is a nonzero integer. ∎

Lemma 4.2

For every \(R\ge1\), there exists \(B\subset\mathcal O_K\) such that

\[ \|b\|_\infty\le R \]

for all \(b\in B\), and

\[ |B|\ge R^n|\Delta_K|^{-1/2}. \]

Proof

Under the Minkowski embedding,

\[ \sigma(\mathcal O_K)\subset\mathbb R^n \]

is a lattice of covolume \(|\Delta_K|^{1/2}\).

By Blichfeldt’s theorem, some translate of \([0,R)^n\) contains at least

\[ R^n|\Delta_K|^{-1/2} \]

lattice points. Subtract one of those points from all the others. The resulting differences correspond to algebraic integers lying in \([-R,R]^n\). ∎

Lemma 4.3

Let \(B\subset\mathcal O_K\) satisfy \(\|b\|_\infty\le R\). Let \(u,v\in E_K^+\) satisfy

\[ |\log\sigma_j(u)|,\ |\log\sigma_j(v)|\le T \]

for all \(j\), and assume

\[ Re^{-T}\ge1. \]

Then

\[ |uB\cap vB| \le (4R)^n \exp\left(-\frac12\|\lambda(u)-\lambda(v)\|_1\right). \]

Proof

If \(z\in uB\cap vB\), then

\[ z=ub_1=vb_2 \]

for some \(b_1,b_2\in B\). Therefore

\[ |\sigma_j(z)| \le R\min(\sigma_j(u),\sigma_j(v)). \]

Since \(Re^{-T}\ge1\), all these radii are at least \(1\). Lemma 4.1 gives

\[ |uB\cap vB| \le \prod_{j=1}^n 4R\min(\sigma_j(u),\sigma_j(v)). \]

Let

\[ a_j:=\log\sigma_j(u), \qquad b_j:=\log\sigma_j(v). \]

Because \(u,v\) are units,

\[ \sum_j a_j=\sum_j b_j=0. \]

Also

\[ \log\min(e^{a_j},e^{b_j}) = \frac{a_j+b_j-|a_j-b_j|}{2}. \]

Summing over \(j\),

\[ \sum_j \log\min(\sigma_j(u),\sigma_j(v)) = -\frac12\sum_j|a_j-b_j|. \]

Thus

\[ \prod_j\min(\sigma_j(u),\sigma_j(v)) = \exp\left(-\frac12\|\lambda(u)-\lambda(v)\|_1\right), \]

as required. ∎

\[ (2-\delta)a = 2a-\frac S4. \]

Thus

\[ e^{(2a-S)n} = e^{((2-\delta)a-\frac34S)n}. \]

For all sufficiently large \(n\), the factor \(e^{-3Sn/4}\) dominates the fixed constant \(2^{-(2-\delta)}\). Therefore, using (22) and (23),

\[ |A_K+A_K|\le |A_K|^{2-\delta}, \]

and

\[ |A_KA_K|\le |A_K|^{2-\delta}. \]

11. Embedding into \(\mathbb R\)

Choose one real embedding

\[ \sigma_1:K\hookrightarrow\mathbb R. \]

Set

\[ A:=\sigma_1(A_K)\subset\mathbb R. \]

Because \(\sigma_1\) is an injective field homomorphism,

\[ |A|=|A_K|, \qquad |A+A|=|A_K+A_K|, \qquad |AA|=|A_KA_K|. \]

Hence

\[ \max(|A+A|,|AA|) \le |A|^{2-\delta}. \]

Applying this construction to infinitely many sufficiently large fields in the tower gives finite sets

\[ A_i\subset\mathbb R \]

with

\[ |A_i|\ge \frac12e^{a n_i}\to\infty \]

and

\[ \max(|A_i+A_i|,|A_iA_i|) \le |A_i|^{2-\delta}. \]

This proves the theorem. ∎
